\section{Atmospheric Delay Modelling: Troposphere and Ionosphere}\label{sec:atmos}
% ============================================================
%  The model interiors behind the T and mu_l I terms of the
%  undifferenced observation equation.
% ============================================================

The undifferenced code and phase equations,
\dcref{eq:obs-code}{the code observation} and
\dcref{eq:obs-phase}{the phase observation} of the Foundations document,
each contain two propagation terms that are computed from \emph{models}
rather than estimated directly: the slant tropospheric delay
$T^\sat_\rx$ and the first-order slant ionospheric delay
$\mu_\sigl\, I^\sat_\rx$.  The troposphere has an a-priori \emph{zenith
hydrostatic} part, which is computed and removed before the filter sees
the measurement.  The small \emph{zenith wet} residual and two
horizontal gradients are estimated (\dcref{sec:state}{the state
vector}, \dcref{eq:tropo-mapping}{the ZWD mapping}).  In the uncombined
parameterisation the ionosphere is one slant state per satellite
(\dcref{sec:state-indexing}{dynamic indexing}).  The models in this
chapter supply its a-priori seed, an optional pseudo-observation that
ties the relative slants to a Global Ionosphere Map, and the small
second- and third-order corrections that the $1/f^2$ scaling does not
cover.  The network producer also co-estimates its own
spherical-harmonic VTEC field on one thin shell, or optionally two
(\Cref{sec:atmos-shvtec}), so it needs no external GIM.  The column
layout of that field in the network normal equations is in
\dcref{sec:net}{the network mode chapter}.  This document derives the
mapping functions, the pressure reductions, the thin-shell geometry, the
geomagnetic field, and the sign with which each term enters the modelled
range.

\subsection{The slant tropospheric delay}
% ============================================================

The neutral atmosphere is non-dispersive at GNSS frequencies.  Its delay
is the same on code and phase, and no ionosphere-free or geometry-free
combination removes it.  The standard decomposition splits the slant
delay into a hydrostatic (``dry'') and a wet zenith delay, each carried to
the line of sight by its own elevation mapping function, plus an
azimuthal gradient \cite{subirana2013gnss}:
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
T^\sat_\rx
   = \mfh(\elev)\,\zhd
   + \mfw(\elev)\,\zwd
   + \mfg(\elev)\bigl(\gN\cos\azim + \gE\sin\azim\bigr).
\label{eq:atmos-tropo-total}
\end{empheq}
$\zhd$ is supplied a priori and removed from the prefit residual.
$\zwd$, $\gN$ and $\gE$ are estimated states whose Jacobian columns are
the mapping coefficients $\mfw(\elev)$ and
$\mfg(\elev)\{\cos\azim,\sin\azim\}$
(\texttt{src/core/measurement/MeasurementModel.cpp};
\dcref{eq:tropo-mapping}{the ZWD mapping}).  The rest of this subsection
derives each factor.

\subsubsection{Saastamoinen zenith hydrostatic delay}

The total surface pressure determines the zenith hydrostatic delay to
better than a millimetre, through the Saastamoinen reduction
\cite{saastamoinen1972atmospheric} as coded in
\texttt{src/core/atmosphere/Troposphere.cpp}:
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\zhd = \frac{0.0022768\; P}{1 - 0.00266\cos 2\phi - 0.00028\, h_{\mathrm{km}}},
\label{eq:atmos-zhd}
\end{empheq}
with $P$ the total pressure in millibar at the antenna, $\phi$ the
geodetic latitude, and $h_{\mathrm{km}}=h/1000$ the ellipsoidal height in
kilometres (\texttt{Troposphere::computeSaastamoinenZHD}, denominator
\texttt{f\_lat}).  The coefficient $0.0022768~\mathrm{m\,mbar^{-1}}$
combines the refractivity constant $k_1 = 77.604~\mathrm{K\,mbar^{-1}}$
with the dry-gas constant and standard gravity.  The denominator
$f(\phi,h)$ accounts for the variation of gravity with latitude and
height.

When neither a surface met sensor nor the GPT3 grid (below) is available,
$P$ is taken from a standard-atmosphere barometric reduction of a
configured sea-level pressure $P_0$ and temperature $T_0$
(\texttt{Troposphere.cpp}, \texttt{pressureFromHeightStdAtm}):
\begin{equation}
P(h) = P_0\left(1 - \frac{L\,h}{T_0}\right)^{g/(R L)},
\qquad
L = 0.0065~\mathrm{K\,m^{-1}},
\quad \frac{g}{R L} \approx 5.25588,
\label{eq:atmos-barometric}
\end{equation}
with $g = 9.80665~\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ and $R = 287.05~\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$
the specific gas constant of dry air.  The base $(1 - Lh/T_0)$ is clamped
to $[0.1,2.0]$ and the result to $[100,1100]~\mathrm{mbar}$ as a numerical
guard.

\subsubsection{The Marini mapping form}

Both the hydrostatic and wet mappings use the normalised three-parameter
continued fraction of Marini, normalised to unity at zenith
(\texttt{Troposphere::mariniMapping}):
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
m(\elev;\,a,b,c) =
\frac{\displaystyle 1 + \cfrac{a}{1 + \cfrac{b}{1+c}}}
     {\displaystyle \sinel + \cfrac{a}{\sinel + \cfrac{b}{\sinel + c}}}.
\label{eq:atmos-marini}
\end{empheq}
The numerator is the denominator evaluated at $\elev = 90^\circ$
($\sinel = 1$), so $m(90^\circ) = 1$.  The hydrostatic and wet mappings,
and the different models, differ only in the triple $(a,b,c)$.  Two
families are implemented.

\paragraph{Niell / GMF coefficients.}
The default mapping interpolates the Niell tables \cite{niell1996global}
of $(a,b,c)$ given at five reference latitudes $15^\circ, 30^\circ,
45^\circ, 60^\circ, 75^\circ$, each as a mean plus an annually varying
amplitude (the coefficient tables inside
\texttt{Troposphere::getTroposphereMapping}).  The seasonal value
is
\begin{equation}
v(\phi,t) = \bar v(\phi)
   - \delta v(\phi)\,
     \cos\!\frac{2\pi\,(\mathrm{doy} - \mathrm{doy}_0)}{365.25},
\qquad \mathrm{doy}_0 =
\begin{cases} 28 & \phi \ge 0 \\ 28 + 182.625 & \phi < 0 \end{cases}
\label{eq:atmos-niell-season}
\end{equation}
(\texttt{Troposphere::getTroposphereMapping}, over the day of year returned
by \texttt{Troposphere::getDayOfYear}).  Two details matter.  The
amplitude term is \emph{subtracted}.  The Southern Hemisphere is put in
anti-phase by adding half a year to the phase reference, not by changing
the sign.  The latitude argument is the absolute latitude, linearly
interpolated and clamped to $[15^\circ,75^\circ]$.  The wet coefficients
have mean values only, with no annual term.

A height correction lifts the hydrostatic mapping from sea level to the
station, identical in the Niell and VMF3 paths
(\texttt{Troposphere::getTroposphereMapping}):
\begin{equation}
\mfh(\elev,h) = \mfh(\elev,0)
  + \left[\frac{1}{\sinel} - m(\elev;\,a_{\mathrm{ht}},b_{\mathrm{ht}},c_{\mathrm{ht}})\right] h_{\mathrm{km}},
\qquad
\begin{aligned}
a_{\mathrm{ht}} &= 2.53\times10^{-5},\\
b_{\mathrm{ht}} &= 5.49\times10^{-3},\\
c_{\mathrm{ht}} &= 1.14\times10^{-3},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:atmos-height-corr}
\end{equation}
with the same Marini form \eqref{eq:atmos-marini} evaluated at the height
triple.  Only the hydrostatic mapping receives this correction.

\paragraph{VMF3 coefficients.}
When configured for VMF3 \cite{landskron2018vmf3}, the wet/hydrostatic
$a$-coefficients come from the GPT3 grid (next paragraph) and the $b,c$
coefficients are synthesised on the fly by a degree- and order-12
spherical-harmonic expansion of their seasonal columns
(\texttt{src/core/atmosphere/Vmf3.cpp}).  A Cartesian-form associated
Legendre recursion builds the real and imaginary solid harmonics
$V_{nm},W_{nm}$ at the station co-latitude and longitude, the
$91 = \sum_{n=0}^{12}(n+1)$ harmonic terms are accumulated against the
stored \texttt{Vmf3Coeffs} tables, and each of $b_{\mathrm h},c_{\mathrm
h},b_{\mathrm w},c_{\mathrm w}$ is then evaluated as a five-coefficient
mean-plus-annual-plus-semiannual harmonic, with all three steps inside
\texttt{Vmf3::computeMapping}:
\begin{equation}
v(\mathrm{doy}) = A_0 + A_1\cos\omega + B_1\sin\omega
                       + A_2\cos 2\omega + B_2\sin 2\omega,
\qquad \omega = \frac{2\pi\,\mathrm{doy}}{365.25}.
\label{eq:atmos-seasonal-harmonic}
\end{equation}
The synthesised $(a,b,c)$ enter the same Marini fraction
\eqref{eq:atmos-marini}, followed by the height correction
\eqref{eq:atmos-height-corr}.  If the grid query fails the code falls
back to the Niell path without a message, so one bad sample does not
stop the run.

\subsubsection{GPT3 empirical pressure, temperature and VMF3 $a$-coefficients}

The GPT3 $5^\circ$ grid \cite{landskron2018vmf3} supplies, per cell, the
five harmonic coefficients of pressure, temperature, temperature lapse
rate, geoid undulation, and the VMF3 hydrostatic and wet $a$-coefficients
(\texttt{Gpt3::load}, in \texttt{atmosphere/Gpt3}).  Each quantity is the
same annual-plus-semiannual harmonic as \eqref{eq:atmos-seasonal-harmonic}
evaluated per cell (\texttt{Gpt3::harmonic}):
\begin{equation}
V(\mathrm{doy}) = a_0 + A_1\cos\omega + B_1\sin\omega
                       + A_2\cos 2\omega + B_2\sin 2\omega .
\label{eq:atmos-gpt3-harmonic}
\end{equation}
The grid value sits at the cell's orthometric reference height $H_s$; it is
carried to the station orthometric height $h_{\mathrm{ortho}} = h - N$
(undulation $N$) by a barometric pressure reduction and a linear
temperature lapse (\texttt{Gpt3::query}):
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{stn}} = P_{\mathrm{grid}}
   \bigl(1 - K_{\mathrm P}\,\Delta h\bigr)^{n_{\mathrm P}},
\quad
T_{\mathrm{stn}} = T_{\mathrm{grid}} + \frac{\mathrm dT}{\mathrm dz}\,\Delta h,
\qquad
\begin{aligned}
K_{\mathrm P} &= 2.26\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}},\\
n_{\mathrm P} &= 5.225,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:atmos-gpt3-reduce}
\end{equation}
with $\Delta h = h_{\mathrm{ortho}} - H_s$ and $\mathrm dT/\mathrm dz$ the
grid lapse in $\mathrm{K\,km^{-1}}$ applied to $\Delta h$ in kilometres.
The exponent $5.225$ is the same $g/(RL)$ ratio as
\eqref{eq:atmos-barometric}, rounded for the grid.  Four corners are read
and combined by bilinear interpolation in latitude and (wrapped) longitude
(also \texttt{Gpt3::query}); the resulting $P_{\mathrm{stn}}$ drives
the Saastamoinen $\zhd$ of \eqref{eq:atmos-zhd}, and the interpolated
$a_{\mathrm h},a_{\mathrm w}$ drive the VMF3 mapping.

\subsubsection{The gradient mapping and a degenerate fallback}

The azimuthal gradient in \eqref{eq:atmos-tropo-total} uses the
Chen--Herring mapping \cite{chen1997effects}, coded identically in the
Jacobian and the prefit residual
(\texttt{MeasurementModel::buildJacobianRow},
\texttt{StateInitializer::computeRawResidual}):
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\mfg(\elev) = \frac{1}{\sinel\tan\elev + 0.0032},
\qquad
T^{\mathrm{grad}} = \mfg(\elev)\bigl(\gN\cos\azim + \gE\sin\azim\bigr).
\label{eq:atmos-grad}
\end{empheq}
The partial derivatives $\partial\Tr/\partial\gN = \mfg\cos\azim$ and
$\partial\Tr/\partial\gE = \mfg\sin\azim$ are the Jacobian columns of the
north and east gradient states (\dcref{sec:state}{the state vector}).

A cold-start guard applies to the \emph{elevation} (hydrostatic and wet)
mapping, not to the gradient mapping.  When the receiver position is not
yet usable (norm below $10^3$~m, or non-finite), the geodetic latitude
and height needed by the Niell and VMF3 coefficients are unavailable.
The hydrostatic and wet mappings then use the geometry-only
Black--Eisner form $1/(\sinel + a/(\tan\elev + b))$ (the fallback branch
of \texttt{Troposphere::getTroposphereMapping}), which keeps the mapping
defined during cold start.

\subsection{The slant ionospheric delay}
% ============================================================

The ionosphere is dispersive.  To first order it advances the phase and
delays the code by the same amount, $40.3\,\stec/f^2$, where $\stec$ is
the slant total electron content in $\mathrm{el\,m^{-2}}$.  The
uncombined model carries one slant state $I^\sat_\rx$ per satellite,
referenced to GPS~L1, and scales it to each signal by the dispersion
ratio.

\subsubsection{The $1/f^2$ scaling and its sign}

For a signal of frequency $f$ on a state referenced to $f_{\mathrm{ref}} =
f_{\mathrm{L1}}^{\mathrm{GPS}}$, the dispersion coefficient is
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\mu_\sigl = \frac{f_{\mathrm{ref}}^2}{f_\sigl^2},
\qquad
\frac{\partial \code_\sigl}{\partial I} = +\mu_\sigl,
\qquad
\frac{\partial \phaseo_\sigl}{\partial I} = -\mu_\sigl,
\label{eq:atmos-iono-scaling}
\end{empheq}
so the slant-iono Jacobian column is $+\mu_\sigl$ on code and
$-\mu_\sigl$ on phase (\texttt{MeasurementModel::buildJacobianRow}).
This defines the $\mu_\sigl$ of \dcref{eq:iono-scaling}{the notation
document}.  Every constellation's slant state is referenced to GPS~L1,
which lets the between-satellite and between-receiver differencing
operators (\dcref{sec:diff}{the differencing document}) cancel the
receiver iono datum.  Because $f_{\mathrm{ref}}=f_{\mathrm{L1}}$, the L1
signal has $\mu = 1$ and the state is the L1 slant delay in metres.

\subsubsection{IONEX / GIM thin-shell slant delay}

A Global Ionosphere Map gives vertical TEC on a latitude/longitude grid at
a single thin shell of height $H$ above a base radius $R_E$.  The slant
delay along a ray at elevation $\elev$ follows from single-layer geometry
(\texttt{computeIppGeometry} in
\texttt{src/core/atmosphere/SphericalHarmonicIonosphere.cpp}, the shared helper
that \texttt{GlobalIonosphereMap::slantIonoDelay} and the co-estimated
spherical-harmonic VTEC model both call so the two never drift).
The zenith angle $z'$ at the ionospheric pierce point (IPP) obeys
\begin{equation}
\sin z' = \frac{R_E}{R_E + H}\cos\elev,
\qquad
\text{mapping } M(\elev) = \frac{1}{\cos z'},
\label{eq:atmos-gim-obliquity}
\end{equation}
so that $\stec = M(\elev)\,\vtec$
(\texttt{GlobalIonosphereMap::slantIonoDelay}).  The earth-centred angle to the
IPP is $\psi = \tfrac\pi2 - \elev - z'$, and the IPP latitude and longitude come
from the spherical-triangle solution along the satellite azimuth $\azim$
(\texttt{computeIppGeometry}).  The helper treats the receiver's geodetic
latitude $\phi_\rx$ as geocentric when crossing the thin shell.  The
approximation is worth well under a millimetre of L1 delay.  The
higher-order model \eqref{eq:atmos-dipole} later uses the IPP in the same
frame:
\begin{align}
\phi_{\mathrm{IPP}} &= \arcsin\!\bigl(\sin\phi_\rx\cos\psi
                      + \cos\phi_\rx\sin\psi\cos\azim\bigr),
\label{eq:atmos-ipp-lat}\\
\lambda_{\mathrm{IPP}} &= \lambda_\rx
   + \arcsin\!\frac{\sin\psi\,\sin\azim}{\cos\phi_{\mathrm{IPP}}}.
\label{eq:atmos-ipp-lon}
\end{align}
The VTEC at the IPP is interpolated bilinearly in space and, in time,
\emph{sun-fixed}: each bracketing map is sampled at the IPP longitude
rotated by the Earth's rotation between the map epoch and the query epoch,
$\Delta\lambda = (t - t_{\mathrm{map}})\cdot 360^\circ/86400~\mathrm{s}$,
before the two are linearly blended
(\texttt{GlobalIonosphereMap::interpolateVtec}, over
\texttt{GlobalIonosphereMap::sampleMap}).  The slant delay in metres at
frequency $f$ then uses
the dispersion constant
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
I^{\mathrm{GIM}} = \stec \cdot \frac{40.3 \times 10^{16}}{f^2}
\quad[\mathrm m],
\qquad \stec = M(\elev)\,\vtec\;[\mathrm{TECU}],
\label{eq:atmos-gim-delay}
\end{empheq}
the factor $10^{16}$ converting $\vtec$ from TECU to $\mathrm{el\,m^{-2}}$
and $40.3 = 40.3~\mathrm{m\,Hz^2\,(el\,m^{-2})^{-1}}$ the first-order
refractivity constant (both the constant and the metre conversion live in
\texttt{GlobalIonosphereMap::slantIonoDelay}).

\subsubsection{The GIM iono-weighting pseudo-observation}

The GIM is too coarse to constrain the \emph{absolute} slant of one
satellite, but it constrains the \emph{relative} slants across a
constellation well.  The constraint is applied as a Kalman
pseudo-observation
(\texttt{Application::applyGimIonoUpdate}), one row per satellite that owns a slant
state above the elevation cutoff.  The innovation is the GIM slant minus
the current state,
\begin{equation}
z_\sat = I^{\mathrm{GIM}}_\sat - I^\sat_\rx,
\qquad \jacH = \bm{I}\ \text{(selection of the iono columns)},
\label{eq:atmos-gim-innov}
\end{equation}
with the GIM evaluated at GPS~L1 to match the state reference
(\texttt{Application::applyGimIonoUpdate}).  The measurement-noise matrix
carries the structure.  Per constellation $g$, with $\bm 1$ the
all-ones vector over that constellation's rows,
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\Rmat = \bigoplus_{g\in\sysset}
        \Bigl(\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_\sat^2)
              + \sigma_c^2\,\bm 1\bm 1^{\Tr}\Bigr),
\label{eq:atmos-gim-R}
\end{empheq}
(also \texttt{Application::applyGimIonoUpdate}).  The rank-one
$\sigma_c^2\,\bm 1\bm 1^{\Tr}$ block gives a very large variance to the
per-constellation \emph{common mode} of the innovations.  That common
mode is the receiver iono datum, an arbitrary offset shared by all
satellites of a constellation, which no model can supply.  The
pseudo-observation therefore leaves it free and constrains only the
between-satellite differences, which the GIM does describe.  The update
uses the same Joseph-form pseudo-update as every other measurement
(\dcref{sec:kf}{the Kalman filter document}).  The iono process noise is
documented there (\dcref{eq:kf-Q-iono}{the iono random-walk spectral
density}).

\paragraph{Why frozen pivots are excluded.}
In the BSSD parameterisation each constellation's pivot satellite holds
its slant-iono state frozen at value $0$ with variance $0$ as the
rank-fixing datum.  Such a row is skipped entirely
(the frozen-pivot guard in \texttt{Application::applyGimIonoUpdate}).  A
zero-variance state receives zero Kalman gain, so the GIM cannot improve
it.  It does share the common-mode $\bm 1\bm 1^{\Tr}$ block of
\eqref{eq:atmos-gim-R} with the other satellites of its constellation.
If it were included, its innovation
$I^{\mathrm{GIM}}_{\piv g} - 0 \sim 5$--$30$~m would enter the gain of
every other satellite through the off-diagonal of $\Smat^{-1}$ and shift
the relative slants by the pivot's full absolute STEC.  Excluding the
pivot row is required for a correct result.  The common-mode block
absorbs the datum offset of the \emph{remaining} rows, so the datum
satellite must not appear as a measurement.

\subsubsection{The co-estimated spherical-harmonic VTEC field}\label{sec:atmos-shvtec}

The network producer can estimate the vertical TEC field itself instead
of importing a map.  The field is a truncated real spherical-harmonic
expansion on the same kind of thin shell, solved jointly with the
satellite code biases.  The coefficients are \emph{shared} columns of the
network normal equations, registered through
\texttt{NetworkEstimator::registerVtecCoeff} and reduced onto the shared
border like any other network-wide family (\dcref{sec:net}{the network
mode chapter}).  This section gives the model, its pseudo-observation,
and its datum.

The switch is \texttt{network.estimate\_iono\_sh}.  It defaults to false,
but the standing network configuration arms it and most shipped network
configurations set it, so it is the ionosphere model the producer
normally runs.  Arming it forces
\texttt{network.estimate\_sat\_code\_bias} on, because the field is what
makes the satellite code biases observable.  It also suppresses external
GIM loading, so the two ionosphere sources are never applied to the same
run (\texttt{config/GnssConfigLoadNetwork.cpp}; the SH-versus-GIM branch
guards the \texttt{networkAppendGimIonoRows\_} call site in
\texttt{app/ApplicationNetworkAssembly}).

\paragraph{Basis and normalisation.}
With $\beta$ the geographic latitude of the pierce point and $s$ its
sun-fixed longitude, the field is
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\vtec(\beta,s) = \sum_{n=0}^{N}\sum_{m=0}^{n}
   \bar P_{nm}(\sin\beta)\,
   \bigl[a_{nm}\cos(m s) + b_{nm}\sin(m s)\bigr]
\quad [\mathrm{TECU}],
\label{eq:atmos-sh-synth}
\end{empheq}
truncated triangularly at degree $N$
(\texttt{estimation.global\_vtec\_sh.max\_degree}), which gives $(N+1)^2$
real coefficients: one cosine term per $(n, m\ge 0)$ and one sine term per
$(n, m\ge 1)$, enumerated once in a fixed order that the shared-column
layout mirrors (\texttt{SphericalHarmonicIonosphere::coeffs}).  The
normalisation is
\begin{equation}
\bar P_{nm} = N_{nm}\,P_{nm},
\qquad
N_{nm} = \sqrt{(2-\delta_{0m})\,(2n+1)\,\frac{(n-m)!}{(n+m)!}},
\label{eq:atmos-sh-norm}
\end{equation}
chosen so the real surface harmonics $\{\bar P_{nm}\cos(m s),\ \bar
P_{nm}\sin(m s)\}$ are orthonormal under $(1/4\pi)\int(\cdot)\,\mathrm
d\Omega$ \cite{subirana2013gnss}.  Because \eqref{eq:atmos-sh-synth} is
linear in the coefficients, the basis values are the partial
derivatives.  \texttt{SphericalHarmonicIonosphere::basis} returns
$\partial\vtec/\partial a_{nm}$ and $\partial\vtec/\partial b_{nm}$
directly, and the Jacobian needs no numerical differentiation.  One
consequence is used repeatedly below: $\bar P_{00}\equiv 1$, so $a_{00}$
is the global mean VTEC.

\paragraph{Frame and geometry.}
The expansion uses a sun-fixed frame, $s = \lambda_{\mathrm{IPP}} -
\lambda_\odot$, with $\lambda_\odot = \alpha_\odot - \mathrm{GMST}$ the
sub-solar geographic longitude (\texttt{meanSunLongitude}).  In this
frame the field changes slowly over a session; in a geographic frame it
would move westward at the Earth's rotation rate.
\texttt{estimation.global\_vtec\_sh.sun\_fixed} selects geographic
longitude instead.  The pierce point and the thin-shell mapping
$M(\elev)$ that carries $\vtec$ to $\stec$ come from \emph{the same}
\texttt{computeIppGeometry} helper that
\texttt{GlobalIonosphereMap::slantIonoDelay} calls, evaluated at
\texttt{estimation.global\_vtec\_sh.layer\_height\_km} over base radius
\texttt{earth\_radius\_km}.  Equations \eqref{eq:atmos-gim-obliquity},
\eqref{eq:atmos-ipp-lat} and \eqref{eq:atmos-ipp-lon} are therefore also
the geometry of the co-estimated model.  The external map and the
co-estimated field share one implementation so that they stay
consistent.

\paragraph{The pin row.}
No measurement observes the field directly.  One pseudo-observation per
line of sight ties it to the estimated slant states
(\texttt{Application::networkAppendShIonoRows\_}).  For every GPS or Galileo
LOS above \texttt{ionosphere\_weighting.elevation\_cutoff\_deg} whose slant
state lives in that station's local block, the row asserts that the slant
state equals the field's prediction:
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\begin{aligned}
g^\sat_\rx &\equiv I^\sat_\rx
   \;-\; \kappa\,M(\elev)\sum_k \bar Y_k(\mathrm{IPP})\,c_k \;=\; 0,
\qquad
\kappa = \frac{40.3\times 10^{16}}{f_{\mathrm{L1}}^2},\\[2pt]
\frac{\partial g^\sat_\rx}{\partial I^\sat_\rx} &= +1,
\qquad
\frac{\partial g^\sat_\rx}{\partial c_k}
   = -\,\kappa\,M(\elev)\,\bar Y_k(\mathrm{IPP}).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:atmos-sh-pin}
\end{empheq}
Here $c_k$ runs over the field coefficients, $\bar Y_k$ over the
corresponding basis functions of \eqref{eq:atmos-sh-synth}, and $\kappa$ is
the TECU-to-L1-metre conversion already introduced in
\eqref{eq:atmos-gim-delay}.  The $+1$ goes in the station's private block
and the $-\kappa M \bar Y_k$ terms go in the \emph{shared} border.  The
second write is what turns an ionosphere prior into a joint estimate.
The right-hand side is $-I^\sat_\rx$ at the current state, and the
shared coefficients enter the absolute solve at zero offset.

The measurement noise on these rows is \emph{the same} common-mode-free
per-constellation block as the GIM pseudo-observation,
\eqref{eq:atmos-gim-R}, built from the same
\texttt{ionosphere\_weighting.common\_mode\_sigma\_m} over a per-LOS
representativeness sigma
\begin{equation}
\sigma_\sat = \max\bigl(\texttt{floor\_m},\;
   \sigma_{\mathrm{rep}}\,M(\elev)\,\kappa\bigr),
\qquad
\sigma_{\mathrm{rep}} = \texttt{global\_vtec\_sh.representation\_sigma\_tecu},
\label{eq:atmos-sh-sigma}
\end{equation}
after which the block is whitened by the Cholesky factor of $\Rmat$ into
unit-information rows before it joins the normal equations.
\eqref{eq:atmos-gim-R} is reused here for a modelling reason.  The
rank-one $\sigma_c^2\,\bm 1\bm 1^{\Tr}$ block leaves the iono datum of
each (receiver, constellation) pair free.  The receiver DCB is part of
that common mode, so the field constrains only the
\emph{between-satellite} slant structure, which is all a global VTEC
surface can describe.  As a result $a_{00}$ is only weakly observable,
through the spread of $M(\elev)$ across the network, and the datum below
has to handle that.  GLONASS is excluded from the field
(\texttt{networkAppendShIonoRows\_} skips it).  Its FDMA per-slot
inter-frequency bias does not cancel in the common-mode block and would
leak into the global coefficients.

\paragraph{The Kaula--Tikhonov prior as the estimability datum.}
The coefficients are shared columns.  A coefficient whose pierce-point
support has moved over an ocean or polar gap is nearly unobservable in
the reduced normal equations and would fail the all-or-nothing rank gate
(\texttt{NetworkSbasis::assertNullSpaceRank}).  A degree-dependent prior
is therefore applied to \emph{every} coefficient before the gate runs
(\texttt{networkInjectShVtecPrior}):
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_n &= \frac{\texttt{kaula\_scale\_tecu}}{n}\quad(n\ge 1),
\qquad
\sigma_0 = \texttt{mean\_vtec\_prior\_sigma\_tecu},\\[2pt]
\bar c_k &=
\begin{cases}
\texttt{mean\_vtec\_prior\_tecu}, & c_k = a_{00},\\
0, & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:atmos-sh-kaula}
\end{empheq}
combined in information space with an optional random-walk anchor toward the
previous epoch's estimate, inflated by a per-degree spectral density $q_n =
\texttt{q\_per\_degree}/n^2$ for $n \ge 1$.  The prior has two separate
roles.  The Kaula term provides \emph{estimability}.  It keeps every
coefficient positive definite, so the declared null-space dimension
equals the number of constellations whatever the coverage, and it pulls
unobserved coefficients toward climatology each epoch so that they do
not random-walk.  The $a_{00}$ term is a deliberately \emph{loose}
climatology anchor.  $a_{00}$ trades against the absolute satellite-DCB
level, whose only rigorous datum is the per-(constellation, signal)
sum-to-zero constraint of \dcref{sec:net}{the network mode chapter}.
Fixing $a_{00}$ to an external mean VTEC would bring back the GIM
dependency that co-estimation removes, and would bias every emitted
satellite code bias by the difference.

\paragraph{Open extensions: default OFF, not yet field-validated.}
Two extensions of this model are built, off by default, and still under
validation.

\emph{(i) A second thin shell}
(\texttt{estimation.global\_vtec\_sh.layer2\_max\_degree}, default $0$ =
single layer) registers a second coefficient set at its own height, and the
pin row \eqref{eq:atmos-sh-pin} then sums the shells, each evaluated at
\emph{its own} pierce geometry, $\sum_l \kappa\,M_l(\elev)\,\bar
Y_k(\mathrm{IPP}_l)\,c_{k,l}$ (\texttt{ShLayer}, assembled by
\texttt{shLayers}, which every consumer of the field loops so a shell can
never be evaluated at another shell's geometry).  The second shell's
$a_{00}$ anchor is deliberately \emph{tight}, for the opposite reason to
the first shell's.  Ground-only geometry barely observes how the mean
VTEC divides between the layers: only the difference of the two mapping
profiles is sensitive to it.  Prior information must therefore hold the
split, while the first shell's loose anchor leaves the \emph{total} mean
to the data.

\emph{(ii) A per-(station, satellite) representativeness deviation}
(\texttt{estimation.global\_vtec\_sh.rep\_error\_tau\_s}, default $0$ = off)
replaces the pin-to-zero of \eqref{eq:atmos-sh-pin} with a pin to a tracked
first-order Gauss--Markov deviation $d$, carried in vertical TECU so its
level and steady state are elevation-stationary.  Writing $d^-, P^-$ for the
aged prior, $\hat u, \hat V$ for the solved posterior mean and variance of
the pin functional, and $f^2$ for the pin-row floor variance, the exact
scalar posterior is
\begin{equation}
\hat d = d^- + \frac{P^-}{a}\bigl(\hat u - d^-\bigr),
\qquad
P^{+} = P^-\,\frac{U + f^2}{U + a},
\qquad
a = P^- + f^2,
\quad
U = \frac{\hat V a}{a - \hat V},
\label{eq:atmos-sh-dev}
\end{equation}
with $U$ the data-only information recovered from $\hat V$
(\texttt{shDevExactUpdate}).  Two treatments against ratcheting are built
on it, also default OFF.

\texttt{rep\_error\_demean} (\texttt{shDevDemean}) removes the
information-weighted global and per-station means of the carried priors
once per epoch.  A deviation component that is common in vertical TECU
has row signature $\kappa\,M(\elev)$, the same as the $a_{00}$ column.
The pair is therefore exactly quasi-null, and the cycle of harvesting,
ageing, and re-asserting the prior ratchets it.  The de-mean enforces
the state's own contract, which is that it carries per-LOS structure
only.

\texttt{rep\_error\_d5\_keep} (\texttt{shDevD5Retap}) makes the slip
response depend on which detector fired.  A slip confirmed by a raw
detector still \emph{erases} the deviation, following the rule that a
stiff time-linked carry must have a wired slip response
(\dcref{sec:slip}{the cycle-slip chapter}).  A re-tap triggered only by
the innovation gate keeps the level and resets the variance to the
Gauss--Markov steady state.

The warm-LOS counts that arm the de-mean are constants in the seam, not
configuration.  The loader warns that both treatments are inert unless
\texttt{rep\_error\_tau\_s} is positive, and that arming the erase-policy
split without the de-mean allows the ratchet again.

\subsubsection{Klobuchar broadcast seed}

The broadcast Klobuchar half-cosine model
\cite{klobuchar1987ionospheric} is used only as a single-frequency
a-priori: the cold-start seed of the slant state.  It is never used as a
dual-frequency correction
(\texttt{Ionosphere::computeKlobucharDelay}).  With
elevation and azimuth in semicircles, the earth-centred angle, geomagnetic
IPP latitude, local time and obliquity are
\begin{align}
\psi &= \frac{0.0137}{\elev/180 + 0.11} - 0.022, &
\phi_i &= \operatorname{clip}_{\pm 0.416}\!\bigl(\phi/180 + \psi\cos\azim\bigr),
\label{eq:atmos-klob-psi}\\
\phi_m &= \phi_i + 0.064\cos\!\bigl((\lambda_i - 1.617)\pi\bigr), &
F &= 1 + 16\,(0.53 - \elev/180)^3,
\label{eq:atmos-klob-geom}
\end{align}
with $\lambda_i = \lambda/180 + \psi\sin\azim/\cos(\phi_i\pi)$ and local
time $t_i = 43200\,\lambda_i + t \pmod{86400}$.  Cubic polynomials in the
geomagnetic latitude $\phi_m$ give the amplitude and period,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AMP} = \sum_{k=0}^{3} a_k\,\phi_m^k\ (\ge 0),
\qquad
\mathrm{PER} = \sum_{k=0}^{3} b_k\,\phi_m^k\ (\ge 72000~\mathrm s),
\label{eq:atmos-klob-amp}
\end{equation}
and the zenith delay is the truncated half-cosine, mapped to slant by $F$
and scaled to the requested frequency by the $f_{\mathrm{L1}}^2/f^2$
dispersion ratio of \eqref{eq:atmos-iono-scaling}
(the tail of \texttt{Ionosphere::computeKlobucharDelay}):
\begin{equation}
I^{\mathrm{Klob}} = c\,F\Bigl(5\!\times\!10^{-9}
   + \mathrm{AMP}\,\bigl[1 - \tfrac{X^2}{2} + \tfrac{X^4}{24}\bigr]\Bigr)
   \frac{f_{\mathrm{L1}}^2}{f^2},
\quad X = \frac{2\pi (t_i - 50400)}{\mathrm{PER}},\ |X|<1.57,
\label{eq:atmos-klob-delay}
\end{equation}
and the constant $5\!\times\!10^{-9}~\mathrm s$ night term alone when
$|X|\ge 1.57$.  The leading $c$ converts the modelled signal delay from
seconds to metres.

\subsection{Higher-order ionospheric corrections}
% ============================================================

The $1/f^2$ scaling covers the first-order term.  The second- and
third-order delays scale as $1/f^3$ and $1/f^4$ and are applied as
deterministic corrections to the modelled range
(\texttt{HigherOrderIonosphere::slantHigherOrderComponents}).
The second order is the magnetic-field--weighted delay
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
I_2^{\,\code} = 7527\,c\;\stec\,\frac{\Bvec\cdot\khat}{f^3},
\qquad
I_2^{\,\phaseo} = -\tfrac12\,I_2^{\,\code},
\label{eq:atmos-i2}
\end{empheq}
where $\stec$ is in $\mathrm{el\,m^{-2}}$, $\Bvec$ is the geomagnetic flux
density in tesla at the IPP, $\khat$ the unit propagation vector
(satellite$\to$receiver), and $c$ the speed of light.  The code term is a
delay when the signal travels along the field and an advance when it
travels against it: GPS is right-hand circularly polarized about $\khat$
(IEEE), the electrons gyrate right-handedly about $\Bvec$, so a signal
along $\Bvec$ is the extraordinary (R) mode with the larger index
deviation.  The receiver$\to$satellite vector inverts $I_2$.  The constant $7527\,c =
e\,A/(2\pi m_e) \approx 2.2564\times10^{12}$ combines the electron
charge, the first-order refractivity constant $A$, and the electron
mass.  The constants and the $-\tfrac12$ phase factor are both in
\texttt{HigherOrderIonosphere::slantHigherOrderComponents}.  The third
order depends on the path integral of $N_e^2$, which a shape factor $\eta$
relates to the F2-peak density and STEC (also
\texttt{HigherOrderIonosphere::slantHigherOrderComponents}):
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
I_3^{\,\code} = 2437\,\eta\,N_{\max}\,\frac{\stec}{f^4},
\qquad
N_{\max} = \kappa\,\stec,
\qquad
I_3^{\,\phaseo} = -\tfrac13\,I_3^{\,\code},
\label{eq:atmos-i3}
\end{empheq}
with $\eta = 0.66$ the Hoque--Jakowski shape factor
\cite{hoque2008estimate}, $2437 = 3A^2/8$, and $N_{\max} = \kappa\,\stec$
the linear Hoque--Jakowski fit ($\kappa = (20-6)\!\times\!10^{12} /
((4.55-1.38)\!\times\!10^{18})~\mathrm{el\,m^{-3}/(el\,m^{-2})}$) of the
F2-peak electron density to slant STEC.  The third-order term is
therefore \emph{linear} in $N_{\max}\,\stec$, not in $\stec^2$.  Both
corrections are below a millimetre at L1.  They differ by satellite, so
between-satellite differencing does not remove them, and each is applied
with its own sign on code and on phase.

\subsubsection{The geomagnetic dipole field}

The field at the IPP is a centred, tilted magnetic dipole evaluated in ECEF
(\texttt{MagneticField::fieldAtEcef}):
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\Bvec(\bm r) = B_0\left(\frac{R_E}{r}\right)^{\!3}
               \bigl(\mhat - 3(\mhat\cdot\rhat)\,\rhat\bigr),
\label{eq:atmos-dipole}
\end{empheq}
with $r = \lVert\bm r\rVert$, $\rhat = \bm r/r$, $B_0 = 30\times10^{-6}$~T
the equatorial surface field, $R_E = 6371.2$~km the IGRF reference radius,
and $\mhat$ the unit axis pointing to the geographic location of the
geomagnetic north pole, IGRF-13 epoch 2020.0 at
$(80.65^\circ, -72.68^\circ)$ (\texttt{MagneticField::dipoleAxisEcef}).
The Arctic geomagnetic pole is the dipole's \emph{south} pole.  With
$\mhat$ pointing at it, \eqref{eq:atmos-dipole} makes $\Bvec$ point
horizontally toward magnetic north at the magnetic equator, as a compass
does.  This convention sets the sign of $\Bvec\cdot\khat$ and therefore
of $I_2$ in \eqref{eq:atmos-i2}.  IONEX is a thin-shell model, so the IPP
ECEF position is rebuilt from the geocentric IPP latitude and longitude
at the IONEX base-plus-shell radius
(\texttt{HigherOrderIonosphere::slantHigherOrderComponents}).
