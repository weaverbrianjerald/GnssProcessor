% ============================================================
%  Observation equations
% ============================================================

This section gives the full \emph{undifferenced} observation model that
the filter linearises around at each epoch.  The PPP-RTK
parameterisation is uncombined: per-signal pseudorange and carrier
phase are consumed directly, without forming ionosphere-free or
wide-lane combinations as a preprocessing step.  Combinations
(\textsc{IF}, \textsc{MW}, \textsc{GF}) are still computed for
diagnostic and slip-detection purposes (\dcref{sec:slip}{the
cycle-slip document}; see
\texttt{slip/MwSlipDetector}, \texttt{slip/GraphicSlipDetector}) but
they do not enter the measurement update path.  This holds whenever the
uncombined parameterisation is active; the loader forces
\texttt{uncombined\_enabled=true} for the whole PPP-RTK family (user,
network producer, network consumer) in
\texttt{GnssConfig::loadFromJson}.  Plain PPP mode leaves the flag at its
default \texttt{false} and instead builds ionosphere-free combined
observables (keys such as \texttt{C1W\_C5X}) that \emph{do} feed
\texttt{MeasurementModel::buildJacobianRow} and the update; the sequel is
written for the uncombined case.

\subsection{Code (pseudorange) observation}

For receiver $\rx$ tracking satellite $\sat$ on signal $\sigl$, the
observed pseudorange is
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\code^\sat_{\rx,\sigl}
\;=\;
\geomrange^\sat_\rx
\;+\; \sLight(\mathrm{d}t_\rx - \mathrm{d}t^\sat)
\;+\; \sLight\,\delta t^{(g(\sat))}_\rx
\;+\; T^\sat_\rx
\;+\; \mu_\sigl\, I^\sat_{\rx,1}
\;+\; B_{\rx,\sigl} - B^\sat_\sigl
\;+\; \varepsilon^\code_{\rx,\sat,\sigl}.
\label{eq:obs-code}
\end{empheq}

\subsection{Carrier-phase observation}

The corresponding carrier-phase observation, scaled to metres by
$\lambda_\sigl$, is
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\phaseo^\sat_{\rx,\sigl}
\;=\;
\geomrange^\sat_\rx
\;+\; \sLight(\mathrm{d}t_\rx - \mathrm{d}t^\sat)
\;+\; \sLight\,\delta t^{(g(\sat))}_\rx
\;+\; T^\sat_\rx
\;-\; \mu_\sigl\, I^\sat_{\rx,1}
\;+\; \lambda_\sigl\, N^\sat_{\rx,\sigl}
\;+\; b_{\rx,\sigl} - b^\sat_\sigl
\;+\; \varepsilon^\phaseo_{\rx,\sat,\sigl}.
\label{eq:obs-phase}
\end{empheq}

The defining structural difference between \Cref{eq:obs-code,eq:obs-phase}
is the sign of the ionospheric term: $+\mu_\sigl I$ on code,
$-\mu_\sigl I$ on phase.  Three additional remarks:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=0pt]
\item The phase ambiguity $N^\sat_{\rx,\sigl}$ is integer in
      principle but is contaminated by the receiver-plus-satellite
      phase biases $b_{\rx,\sigl} - b^\sat_\sigl$.  In a
      \emph{float} solution these biases are absorbed into
      $N^\sat_{\rx,\sigl}$, leaving a non-integer state with no
      structural advantage over the code observation.  Recovering
      integer-valued ambiguities therefore requires either external
      Fractional Cycle Bias (FCB) products or differencing
      (\Cref{sec:diff}), which cancels common-mode bias terms.
\item The clock product $\sLight\,\mathrm{d}t^\sat$ is read from the
      analysis-centre CLK file (post-processing) or refined from the
      broadcast clock by a streamed SSR correction (real-time,
      \dcref{sec:ssr}{the SSR-corrections document}) and treated as
      deterministic in the filter; any
      residual product error appears on the observation and is absorbed
      by the receiver clock state.
\item The system time offset $\sLight\,\delta t^{(g(\sat))}_\rx$ is
      zero by construction when $g(\sat)$ is the reference
      constellation (default GPS).  Together with the receiver clock
      $\sLight\,\mathrm{d}t_\rx$ this means a multi-constellation run
      carries $|\sysset|$ clock-like states in total.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Geometric range and linearisation}\label{sec:obs-geometry}

The geometric range $\geomrange^\sat_\rx = \|\satpos - \rxpos_\rx\|_2$
enters \Cref{eq:obs-code,eq:obs-phase} non-linearly through the
receiver position $\rxpos_\rx$.  The filter linearises about the
current state estimate $\hat{\rxpos}_\rx$:
\begin{equation}
\geomrange^\sat_\rx
\;=\;
\hat{\geomrange}^\sat_\rx
\;-\; (\los^\sat_{\hat{\rx}})^{\Tr} (\rxpos_\rx - \hat{\rxpos}_\rx)
\;+\; \mathcal{O}\!\left(\|\rxpos_\rx - \hat{\rxpos}_\rx\|^2\right),
\label{eq:geom-linear}
\end{equation}
with the unit line-of-sight
\begin{equation}
\los^\sat_{\hat{\rx}}
\;:=\;
\frac{\satpos - \hat{\rxpos}_\rx}{\|\satpos - \hat{\rxpos}_\rx\|_2}.
\label{eq:los}
\end{equation}
The Jacobian row $\jacH_{(\sat,\sigl)}$ contributing to the design
matrix at the position columns is therefore $-(\los^\sat_{\hat{\rx}})^{\Tr}$.
For sub-metre residual targets this single linearisation per epoch is
sufficient; the engine optionally relinearises within the epoch
(\texttt{processing.relinearize\_geometry}, which defaults to off in
code and is enabled in the PPP, PPP-RTK, baseline, and real-time
configs but left off in the six network-producer configs) to handle
long initial position offsets.

The satellite position $\satpos$ is itself a function of the
transmission time.  Rather than iterate the emission time against the
geometric range --- which depends on $\rxpos_\rx$ ---
\texttt{Geometry::computeEmissionState} solves it in a single step from
the \emph{pseudorange}: the time of flight is $\tau = \code / \sLight$,
subtracted (with the satellite clock) from the receiver epoch.  Because
the receiver clock bias $\mathrm{d}t_\rx$ enters the receiver epoch as
$+\mathrm{d}t_\rx$ and the pseudorange as $+\sLight\,\mathrm{d}t_\rx$, it
cancels exactly, leaving a residual $(I+T+\varepsilon)/\sLight \approx
30$--$60$~ns of satellite displacement --- sub-millimetre, below any PPP
threshold.  This deliberately avoids a range-based fixed point: iterating
against $\geomrange^\sat_\rx$ would re-introduce an uncancelled
$\mathrm{d}t_\rx$ and drive a positive-feedback loop through the filter's
receiver-clock state.  The only second pass (the
\texttt{model\_corrections.refine\_emission\_time} block in
\texttt{Geometry::computeEmissionState}) re-runs the emission solve after
subtracting slant tropo and IF-combined ATX from the seed pseudorange ---
a pseudorange-cleanliness refinement, not a range-versus-position
iteration.

\subsection{Stochastic model}

Observation noise covariance is structured per epoch as a block
diagonal matrix with entries
\begin{equation}
\sigma^2_{\rx,\sat,\sigl}
\;=\;
\sigma^2_{0,\text{type}(\sigl)}\;\cdot\; w(\theta^\sat_\rx)^{-1}\;\cdot\;
\xi(\text{C/N}_0\text{ at } (\rx,\sat,\sigl)),
\label{eq:noise-model}
\end{equation}
combining a per-type baseline ($\sigma_{0,\text{code}}$,
$\sigma_{0,\text{phase}}$ from \texttt{filter.measurement\_noise}), an
elevation-dependent down-weighting factor $w(\theta) \in \{1, \sin\theta,
\sin^2\theta, \dots\}$ selected by
\texttt{filter.stochastic\_model}, and an optional carrier-to-noise
ratio scaling $\xi$ parameterised by \texttt{snr\_model\_params}.
Cross-correlations between observations are assumed zero in the
undifferenced parameterisation; differencing introduces structured
correlations handled in \Cref{sec:diff}.
