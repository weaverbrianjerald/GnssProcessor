\section{Observation Model Physics: Antenna, Attitude, Relativity, and Range Corrections}\label{sec:obsphys}
% ============================================================
%  Deterministic per-observation corrections beyond geometry
%  and atmosphere: antenna PCO/PCV, satellite attitude, carrier
%  wind-up, relativity, Shapiro, Sagnac, and group-delay/bias
%  realisation of the abstract B/b terms.
% ============================================================

% --- doc-local macros (defined in the body, never in a section title) ---
\newcommand{\bframe}{\bm{F}}                       % satellite body-frame matrix [i_s j_s k_s]
\newcommand{\isun}{\hat{\bm{x}}_\sat}              % sat body +X (toward Sun)
\newcommand{\jcross}{\hat{\bm{y}}_\sat}            % sat body +Y (cross-track)
\newcommand{\knad}{\hat{\bm{k}}_\sat}              % sat body +Z (nadir / boresight)
\newcommand{\esun}{\hat{\bm{e}}_\odot}            % unit Sun direction
\newcommand{\pco}{\bm{p}}                          % phase-centre offset vector
\newcommand{\dipS}{\bm{D}_\sat}                    % satellite effective dipole
\newcommand{\dipR}{\bm{D}_\rx}                     % receiver effective dipole
\newcommand{\winddphi}{\Delta\varphi}             % signed wind-up angle (rad)

The corrections in this document are the \emph{deterministic} part of the
observation model that lies between pure geometry and the atmosphere on
one side, and the streamed SSR overlay on the other.
\dcref{sec:obs}{The Foundations observation equations}
\dcref{eq:obs-code}{the undifferenced code equation} and
\dcref{eq:obs-phase}{the undifferenced phase equation} introduce the satellite and
receiver hardware biases $B^\sat_\sigl,\,b^\sat_\sigl,\,B_{\rx,\sigl},
\,b_{\rx,\sigl}$ \emph{abstractly}, and
\dcref{sec:obs-geometry}{the observation-geometry section} carries the
geometric range $\geomrange^\sat_\rx$ and its linearisation; this
document supplies every \emph{other} deterministic term that maps the
raw measurement to the modelled range: the antenna phase-centre
geometry, the satellite attitude frame on which it depends, the
carrier-phase wind-up, the relativistic clock and propagation
corrections, the Sagnac (Earth-rotation) frame correction, and the
deterministic realisation of the bias terms through broadcast group
delays and the precise \texttt{.BIA} product.  The streamed SSR overlay
that refines the \emph{same} terms in real time is the subject of
\dcref{sec:ssr}{the SSR corrections document}; the two mechanisms are
related but distinct, and the differences (frames, sign conventions,
gating) are flagged where they matter.

Every term below is assembled into a single modelled observable in
\texttt{model/PppModel.cpp} (\texttt{PppModel::computeBaseModeledValue},
the single shared helper called by both the iono-free-pair and the
uncombined observation paths), summed onto the geometric range that
\texttt{model/ModelGeometry.cpp} produces.  The dominant structure of
that sum, for the code observable, is
\begin{equation}
\hat{\code}^\sat_{\rx,\sigl}
=
\geomrange^\sat_\rx
\;-\;\sLight\,\mathrm{d}t^\sat
\;-\;\sLight\,\delta t_{\mathrm{rel}}
\;+\;T^\sat_\rx
\;+\;A^\sat_{\rx,\sigl}
\;+\;B_{\mathrm{grp}}
\;+\;S^\sat_\rx
\;+\;d_{\mathrm{atm}}
\;+\;d_{\mathrm{tide}},
\label{eq:obsphys-assembly}
\end{equation}
where $A^\sat_{\rx,\sigl}$ is the total antenna correction
(\Cref{sec:obsphys-antenna}), $\delta t_{\mathrm{rel}}$ the periodic
relativistic clock term (\Cref{sec:obsphys-rel}), $S^\sat_\rx$ the
Shapiro delay (\Cref{sec:obsphys-shapiro}), $B_{\mathrm{grp}}$ the
deterministic code/phase bias (\Cref{sec:obsphys-bias}), and
$d_{\mathrm{atm}},\,d_{\mathrm{tide}}$ the loading/site-displacement
terms documented in \dcref{sec:sitedisp}{the site-displacement
document}.  The Sagnac correction (\Cref{sec:obsphys-sagnac}) is folded
into $\geomrange^\sat_\rx$ itself, not added separately, for reasons made
precise below.  The signs in \eqref{eq:obsphys-assembly} are taken
verbatim from the return statement of
\texttt{PppModel::computeBaseModeledValue}
(\texttt{model/PppModel.cpp}): the satellite clock and relativistic terms
\emph{subtract}, the antenna, Shapiro, loading and tide terms \emph{add}.

% ============================================================
\subsection{Satellite attitude: the body frame}\label{sec:obsphys-attitude}
% ============================================================

The satellite antenna phase-centre offset, the antenna phase-centre
variation, and the carrier wind-up are all defined in the
\emph{spacecraft body frame}, so that frame must be constructed first.
The engine uses the IGS/ANTEX convention: a right-handed orthonormal
triad $(\isun,\jcross,\knad)$ with $+Z$ toward the Earth's centre
(nadir / antenna boresight), $+X$ in the Sun--satellite--Earth plane
pointing toward the Sun, and $+Y$ completing the triad along the solar-panel
(cross-track) axis (\texttt{model/SatAttitude.cpp},
\texttt{model/Antenna.cpp}).

\paragraph{Nominal yaw-steering.}
With $\satpos$ the satellite ECEF position and
$\esun=(\bm{r}_\odot-\satpos)/\lVert\bm{r}_\odot-\satpos\rVert$ the unit
direction to the Sun, the nadir axis and the cross-track axis are
\begin{equation}
\knad = -\,\frac{\satpos}{\lVert\satpos\rVert},
\qquad
\jcross = \frac{\knad\times\esun}{\lVert\knad\times\esun\rVert},
\qquad
\isun = \jcross\times\knad,
\label{eq:obsphys-yaw-frame}
\end{equation}
the exact operand ordering of \texttt{SatAttitude::compute}
(\texttt{model/SatAttitude.cpp}), to which the antenna path delegates
(\texttt{Antenna::getAntennaCorrection}) rather than rebuilding the frame
inline; the one genuine inline duplicate of this construction lives in the
wind-up path and is flagged in \Cref{sec:obsphys-windup} below.  Forming
$\jcross$ first and then
$\isun=\jcross\times\knad$ is algebraically the Gram--Schmidt
orthogonalisation of the Sun direction against nadir: by the
$\bm{a}\times(\bm{b}\times\bm{c})$ identity,
\begin{equation}
\isun = \jcross\times\knad
      = (\knad\times\esun)\times\knad
      = \esun - \knad\,(\knad\cdot\esun),
\label{eq:obsphys-xsun-gramschmidt}
\end{equation}
i.e. $\isun$ is the component of $\esun$ orthogonal to nadir, normalised
--- the $+X$ axis points half-toward the Sun, as required by the ANTEX
convention.  The body-frame matrix collecting the three columns,
\begin{equation}
\bframe = \big[\,\isun\;\;\jcross\;\;\knad\,\big],
\label{eq:obsphys-bodyframe-matrix}
\end{equation}
is orthonormal, so its inverse is its transpose --- the property exploited
below to rotate a body-frame offset into ECEF.

\paragraph{Sun elevation and the mode switch.}
For non-nominal attitude (eclipse seasons, the noon/midnight yaw turn) the
relevant geometric quantity is the Sun's elevation above the orbital
plane, $\beta$.  With the orbit angular-momentum (normal) unit vector
$\hat{\bm{n}}=(\satpos\times\satvel)/\lVert\satpos\times\satvel\rVert$,
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\beta = \arcsin\!\big(\hat{\bm{n}}\cdot\esun\big),
\qquad
\hat{\bm{n}} = \frac{\satpos\times\satvel}{\lVert\satpos\times\satvel\rVert},
\label{eq:obsphys-beta}
\end{empheq}
signed by the projection of the Sun direction onto the $+(\satpos\times
\satvel)$ normal (\texttt{SatAttitude::computeBeta},
\texttt{model/SatAttitude.cpp}; the sign is noted in-code as unvalidated
against an external attitude product).  The mode selector
(\texttt{SatAttitude::determineMode}) keeps GPS, GLONASS and Galileo in
yaw-steering unconditionally, and switches BeiDou and QZSS to
orbit-normal attitude below a constellation-specific $\beta$ threshold
($4.0^\circ$ BeiDou, $20.0^\circ$ QZSS,
\texttt{model/SatAttitude.cpp}).  In orbit-normal mode the $+Y$ axis is
pinned to the orbit normal, $\jcross=\hat{\bm{n}}$, and the triad is
re-orthogonalised $\isun=\jcross\times\knad$, $\jcross=\knad\times\isun$.
The legacy default path additionally forces the hard-coded BeiDou-2 GEO
PRN list (C01--C05, C59, C60) into orbit-normal mode irrespective of
$\beta$; the $\beta$-driven table is active only when
\texttt{enableOrbitNormalAttitude()} is set, and is constructed so the
flag-off result is bit-identical to the historical inline frame
(\texttt{model/Antenna.cpp}, \texttt{model/SatAttitude.cpp}).

\paragraph{Eclipse gating.}
The body frame above presumes a known yaw angle, which is exactly what
fails during Earth-shadow passes: the actual spacecraft yaw during
eclipse and the post-shadow recovery is block-specific and not modelled
here, so the antenna PCO/PCV and the wind-up are unreliable for an
eclipsing satellite.  When the user opts in
(\texttt{processing.exclude\_eclipsing\_satellites}), the satellite is
dropped at the geometry stage by a \emph{cylindrical umbra} test
(\texttt{Planetary::isSatelliteEclipsing}, \texttt{model/Planetary.cpp},
called from \texttt{model/ModelGeometry.cpp}).  With
$\hat{\bm{u}}_\odot=\bm{r}_\odot/\lVert\bm{r}_\odot\rVert$ the Earth-to-Sun
direction, the satellite's along-axis and perpendicular distances are
$s_\parallel=\satpos\cdot\hat{\bm{u}}_\odot$ and $\bm{s}_\perp=\satpos -
s_\parallel\,\hat{\bm{u}}_\odot$, and the predicate is
\begin{equation}
\text{eclipsed}
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
\big(s_\parallel < 0\big)
\;\wedge\;
\big(\lVert\bm{s}_\perp\rVert < \kappa\,R_\oplus\big),
\label{eq:obsphys-eclipse}
\end{equation}
i.e. the satellite is on the anti-Sun side \emph{and} inside the shadow
cylinder of radius $\kappa R_\oplus$, with $R_\oplus = 6\,371\,230$\,m
(\texttt{common/Constants.hpp}) and $\kappa$ the user shadow scale
(\texttt{processing.eclipse\_shadow\_scale}, default unity; cited rather
than reproduced as a tuning knob).  This is a deliberately conservative
geometric umbra, not a photometric penumbra model.

\subsection{Antenna phase-centre corrections}\label{sec:obsphys-antenna}
% ============================================================

The range measurement is realised between the satellite and receiver
\emph{antenna phase centres}, but the estimated states refer to the
satellite centre of mass and the receiver antenna reference point (ARP).
The antenna correction $A^\sat_{\rx,\sigl}$ reconciles the two, summing
four geometric terms plus the wind-up:
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
A^\sat_{\rx,\sigl}
\;=\;
-\,\delta_{\mathrm{PCO}}^{\sat}
\;+\;\delta_{\mathrm{PCV}}^{\sat}
\;+\;\delta_{\mathrm{PCO}}^{\rx}
\;+\;\delta_{\mathrm{PCV}}^{\rx}
\;+\;\varphi_{\mathrm{wu}},
\label{eq:obsphys-antenna-total}
\end{empheq}
the exact accumulation of \texttt{Antenna::getAntennaCorrection}
(\texttt{model/Antenna.cpp}).  The line-of-sight used throughout is the
\emph{satellite-to-receiver} unit vector,
$\los = (\rxpos_\rx-\satpos)/\lVert\rxpos_\rx-\satpos\rVert$
(\texttt{Antenna.cpp}; note this is the negative of the receiver-to-satellite
$\los^\sat_{\hat{\rx}}$ of \dcref{sec:obs-geometry}{the geometry section}
in \dcref{eq:los}{its line-of-sight definition}).  The sign conventions are derived once and reused for
each term below.

\paragraph{Satellite PCO (subtracted).}
The satellite phase-centre offset $\pco^\sat_{\mathrm{body}}$ is published
in the body frame (millimetres in ANTEX).  It is rotated to ECEF by the
body-frame matrix --- because $\bframe$ collects the body axes as
\emph{columns}, $\bframe$ itself maps body $\to$ ECEF (and the orthonormal
$\bframe^\Tr=\bframe^{-1}$ maps ECEF $\to$ body) --- and projected onto the
LOS:
\begin{equation}
\delta_{\mathrm{PCO}}^{\sat}
=
\big(\bframe\,\pco^\sat_{\mathrm{body}}\big)\cdot\los .
\label{eq:obsphys-sat-pco}
\end{equation}
Because the antenna phase centre sits \emph{nearer the receiver} than the
centre of mass, the true range is shorter than the CoM range; the
correction is therefore \emph{subtracted} in
\eqref{eq:obsphys-antenna-total} (\texttt{correction -= sat\_pco\_corr},
\texttt{model/Antenna.cpp}).  One real-time exception omits this term
entirely: when an applied SSR orbit correction is itself APC-referenced
(the corrected orbit already points at the phase centre), the
\texttt{skip\_satellite\_pco} path of \texttt{getAntennaCorrection} drops
the satellite-PCO range term to avoid double-counting it, gated by
\texttt{realtime.honor\_ssr\_reference\_point} and default-off; the SSR
reference-point dialect that drives it is documented with
\dcref{sec:ssr}{the SSR corrections document}.

\paragraph{Satellite PCV (added).}
The satellite phase-centre variation is a function of the \emph{nadir
angle} --- the angle between the boresight $\knad$ and the line of sight ---
and of the azimuth in the body frame.  With $\cos\theta_{\mathrm{nad}} =
\knad\cdot\los$,
\begin{equation}
\theta_{\mathrm{nad}} = \arccos\!\big(\knad\cdot\los\big),
\qquad
\alpha_{\mathrm{body}} = \operatorname{atan2}\!\big((\bframe^\Tr\,\los)_y,\,
(\bframe^\Tr\,\los)_x\big),
\label{eq:obsphys-nadir}
\end{equation}
and the PCV value is read by bilinear interpolation of the ANTEX grid
over (zenith/nadir ring, azimuth sector), the look-up argument being
$\theta_{\mathrm{nad}}-\mathtt{zen1}$ in grid units
(\texttt{Antenna::interpolatePcv}, \texttt{model/Antenna.cpp}).  The
satellite PCV is \emph{added} to the range correction.

\paragraph{Receiver PCO (added).}
The receiver PCO is published as a North/East/Up (NEU) triplet relative to
the ARP.  It is reordered to ENU, rotated to ECEF by the local
ENU$\to$ECEF rotation $\bm{R}_{\mathrm{enu}}(\phi,\lambda)$ at the
receiver geodetic latitude/longitude
(\texttt{Coordinates::enuToEcefMatrix}), and projected on the LOS:
\begin{equation}
\delta_{\mathrm{PCO}}^{\rx}
=
\Big(\bm{R}_{\mathrm{enu}}(\phi,\lambda)\,
\big[\,p_E\;\;p_N\;\;p_U\,\big]^\Tr\Big)\cdot\los,
\qquad
\big[\,p_E\;p_N\;p_U\,\big] = \big[\,p_{\mathrm{NEU},2}\;\;
p_{\mathrm{NEU},1}\;\;p_{\mathrm{NEU},3}\,\big],
\label{eq:obsphys-rx-pco}
\end{equation}
where the index swap $(N,E,U)\!\to\!(E,N,U)$ is explicit in
\texttt{model/Antenna.cpp}.  It is \emph{added} to the correction.

\paragraph{Receiver PCV (added).}
The receiver PCV is indexed by \emph{zenith angle} $z = 90^\circ -
\mathrm{el}$ (and, optionally, azimuth corrected for the antenna mounting
orientation when \texttt{enableRxAntennaOrientation()} is set), with the
same bilinear interpolation over $z-\mathtt{zen1}$
(\texttt{model/Antenna.cpp}).  It is \emph{added} to the correction.

\paragraph{ATX frequency fallback.}
ANTEX entries frequently omit the newest carriers (L5/E5a/B2a on older
spacecraft, and many receiver antennas published before triple-frequency
tracking).  Rather than dropping the antenna correction to zero --- which
leaves a several-centimetre vertical bias --- the engine walks a
\emph{nearest-carrier} substitution chain when the requested ANTEX
frequency key is absent (\texttt{pickAtxFrequencyKey},
\texttt{model/Antenna.cpp}).  The chains are ordered by carrier-frequency
proximity, e.g. for GPS $\mathtt{G05}\to\{\mathtt{G02},\mathtt{G01}\}$ and
for Galileo $\mathtt{E05}\to\{\mathtt{E08},\mathtt{E07},\mathtt{E06},
\mathtt{E01}\}$, since cross-frequency PCO drift on one physical antenna
is typically below a centimetre.  The PCO and PCV chains are walked
\emph{independently} (an antenna may carry the PCO but not the PCV at a
given key).  For the \emph{satellite} antenna only the intra-system chain
is permitted --- borrowing another constellation's spacecraft entry would
be meaningless.  For the \emph{receiver} antenna, after the intra-system
chain is exhausted and only when
\texttt{enable\_receiver\_cross\_system\_atx\_fallback} is set, a
\emph{cross-system} nearest-GPS-band chain is allowed
(\texttt{pickReceiverAtxFrequencyKey}), because the receiver phase pattern
is a function of frequency on shared hardware: the load-bearing mappings
are exact-carrier matches ($\mathtt{E01}\!\to\!\mathtt{G01}$,
$\mathtt{E05}\!\to\!\mathtt{G05}$ at $1575.42$ and $1176.45$\,MHz), the
remainder $\sim$10--70\,MHz approximations
(\texttt{model/Antenna.cpp}).  Each substitution raises a coverage flag and
emits a fallback log line; this is the same graceful-fallback policy used
for biases rather than excluding the measurement.

\subsection{Carrier-phase wind-up}\label{sec:obsphys-windup}
% ============================================================

A right-hand-circularly-polarised GNSS signal accrues a phase shift equal
to the relative geometric rotation between the transmitting and receiving
antenna dipoles as either rotates about the line of sight \cite{wuebbena1985software}.
This wind-up is identical on every signal in cycles (hence a range term
proportional to wavelength) and is computed in
\texttt{GnssCorrections::computePhaseWindUp}
(\texttt{corrections/GnssCorrections.cpp}).  With $\bm{k}$ the unit
line-of-sight, $(\isun,\jcross)$ the satellite body $\pm X,+Y$ axes and
$(\bm{x}_\rx,\bm{y}_\rx)$ the receiver local North/East axes, the
\emph{effective dipoles} are
\begin{equation}
\dipS = \isun - \bm{k}\,(\bm{k}\cdot\isun) + \bm{k}\times\jcross,
\qquad
\dipR = \bm{x}_\rx - \bm{k}\,(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}_\rx) + \bm{k}\times\bm{y}_\rx,
\label{eq:obsphys-windup-dipoles}
\end{equation}
the standard Wu construction --- each dipole is the antenna $X$ axis
projected into the plane orthogonal to $\bm{k}$, plus the $\bm{k}\times Y$
quadrature term (\texttt{corrections/GnssCorrections.cpp}).  The receiver
North and East axes are built directly from the geodetic latitude
$\phi$ and longitude $\lambda$,
$\bm{x}_\rx = (-\sin\phi\cos\lambda,\,-\sin\phi\sin\lambda,\,\cos\phi)$ and
$\bm{y}_\rx = (-\sin\lambda,\,\cos\lambda,\,0)$
(\texttt{GnssCorrections.cpp}).  The signed wind-up angle is the
$\operatorname{atan2}$ of the cross- and dot-products of the unit
dipoles, which recovers the full $(-\pi,\pi]$ range and avoids the
information loss of an $\arccos$ near $0$ and $\pi$:
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\winddphi
=
\operatorname{atan2}\!\Big(
\bm{k}\cdot\big(\hat{\dipS}\times\hat{\dipR}\big),\;
\hat{\dipS}\cdot\hat{\dipR}
\Big),
\qquad
\varphi_{\mathrm{wu}}
=
\winddphi\,\frac{\lambda_\sigl}{2\pi},
\label{eq:obsphys-windup-angle}
\end{empheq}
with $\hat{\dipS},\hat{\dipR}$ the normalised dipoles and $\lambda_\sigl$
the signal wavelength (\texttt{corrections/GnssCorrections.cpp}); the
$\bm{k}\!\cdot\!(\hat{\dipS}\times\hat{\dipR})$ argument fixes the sign of
the rotation about the line of sight.  The result enters
\eqref{eq:obsphys-antenna-total} as $\varphi_{\mathrm{wu}}$ on phase
observables only (the code branch passes zero wavelength).

\paragraph{Fractional vs.\ accumulated modes.}
The raw $\operatorname{atan2}$ returns the wind-up wrapped to a single
cycle.  In the default \emph{fractional} mode this wrapped angle is used
directly: the float ambiguity $N^\sat_{\rx,\sigl}$ absorbs any
integer-cycle component, so wrapping is harmless.  For integer ambiguity
resolution the ambiguity must stay a constant integer across the arc, so
the engine offers an \emph{accumulated} (unwrapped) mode
(\texttt{config.accumulatePhaseWindUp()}) that unwraps each epoch
relative to the previous stored angle,
$\winddphi_t = \winddphi_{t-1} + \operatorname{wrap}_{2\pi}(\winddphi_t -
\winddphi_{t-1})$, provided the inter-epoch gap is below the
$300$\,s continuity threshold; a larger gap (or a backward time step)
restarts the accumulator from the raw angle
(\texttt{corrections/GnssCorrections.cpp}).  This $300$\,s reset is the
wind-up analogue of an ambiguity-arc boundary: a gap long enough to
invalidate the unwrap reference is treated as a fresh arc.

\paragraph{Known frame duplication.}
The satellite body frame feeding the wind-up dipole $\dipS$
\eqref{eq:obsphys-windup-dipoles} is built \emph{inline} inside
\texttt{computePhaseWindUp} (\texttt{corrections/GnssCorrections.cpp})
rather than calling the shared \texttt{model/SatAttitude.cpp} helper used
by the antenna path \eqref{eq:obsphys-yaw-frame}.  The two
constructions are deliberately bit-for-bit identical (same operand
ordering, same BeiDou-2 GEO orbit-normal special case, same velocity
fallback), and both source files note the duplication explicitly.  It is
flagged here because the invariant is a \emph{maintenance} obligation: a
change to one frame must be mirrored in the other or the antenna PCV and
the wind-up would silently diverge on the same satellite.

\subsection{Periodic relativistic clock correction}\label{sec:obsphys-rel}
% ============================================================

A clock on an eccentric orbit runs at a time-varying rate relative to a
clock on the geoid; the constant and secular parts are absorbed into the
broadcast/precise clock polynomial, but the \emph{periodic} part driven by
orbital eccentricity must be applied separately.  The standard expression
\cite{subirana2013gnss}, in
\texttt{GnssCorrections::computeRelativisticCorrection}
(\texttt{corrections/GnssCorrections.cpp}), is
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\delta t_{\mathrm{rel}}
\;=\;
-\,\frac{2\,(\satpos\cdot\satvel)}{\sLight^{2}}
\quad[\text{s}],
\qquad
\text{range term } = -\,\sLight\,\delta t_{\mathrm{rel}},
\label{eq:obsphys-rel}
\end{empheq}
i.e. $-2\,(\bm{r}\cdot\bm{v})/c^2$ seconds, applied to the range by the
$-\sLight\,\delta t_{\mathrm{rel}}$ term of the assembly
\eqref{eq:obsphys-assembly} (\texttt{model/PppModel.cpp}).  Two points are
worth making precise.  First, the dot product $\satpos\cdot\satvel$ is
\emph{invariant} to whether the position/velocity pair is expressed in the
inertial or the co-rotating ECEF frame: the ECEF velocity differs from the
inertial velocity by the frame-rotation term
$\satvel_{\mathrm{ecef}} = \satvel_{\mathrm{inertial}} -
\bm{\omega}_\oplus\times\satpos$, and
$\satpos\cdot(\bm{\omega}_\oplus\times\satpos)=0$ because the
cross-product is orthogonal to $\satpos$; so the same scalar results from
either velocity, and the broadcast-ephemeris ECEF velocity may be used
directly.  Second, the periodic term is computed \emph{here}, separately,
because the IGS precise CLK product by convention carries only the
constant-plus-drift clock and excludes the periodic eccentricity term ---
applying \eqref{eq:obsphys-rel} restores the missing piece consistently
for both precise and broadcast clock sources.

\subsection{Shapiro (gravitational-delay) correction}\label{sec:obsphys-shapiro}
% ============================================================

The signal path bends and slows in the Earth's gravitational potential;
the resulting Shapiro delay is a few centimetres
\cite{subirana2013gnss}.  With $r_\sat=\lVert\satpos\rVert$,
$r_\rx=\lVert\rxpos_\rx\rVert$ the geocentric radii and
$r_{\sat\rx}=\lVert\satpos-\rxpos_\rx\rVert$ the satellite--receiver
distance, the delay (in metres) is
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
S^\sat_\rx
\;=\;
\frac{2\,GM}{\sLight^{2}}\,
\ln\!\frac{r_\sat + r_\rx + r_{\sat\rx}}
          {r_\sat + r_\rx - r_{\sat\rx}},
\label{eq:obsphys-shapiro}
\end{empheq}
with $GM = 3.986005\times10^{14}\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$
(\texttt{Constants::GRAVITATIONAL\_CONSTANT}, \texttt{common/Constants.hpp})
and the formula verbatim at \texttt{GnssCorrections.cpp}.  Because
$r_{\sat\rx}<r_\sat+r_\rx$ the logarithm is positive, so $S^\sat_\rx>0$
and the term is \emph{added} to the modelled range in
\eqref{eq:obsphys-assembly} (\texttt{model/PppModel.cpp}).  Its magnitude
is $\sim$1--2\,cm for a typical MEO geometry.

\subsection{Sagnac (Earth-rotation) correction}\label{sec:obsphys-sagnac}
% ============================================================

The pseudorange is formed between the satellite position at \emph{signal
emission} and the receiver position at \emph{signal reception}, but both
are needed in a single, consistent ECEF frame.  During the
$\sim$70\,ms transit the ECEF frame itself rotates with the Earth, so the
emission-time satellite coordinates must be rotated forward into the
reception-time frame.  The engine applies the \emph{rigorous} rotation,
not a scalar approximation: with the geometric time-of-flight
$\tau = \lVert\satpos_{\mathrm{em}}-\rxpos_\rx\rVert/\sLight$ and the
Earth rotation rate $\omega_\oplus = 7.2921151467\times10^{-5}$\,rad/s
(\texttt{common/Constants.hpp}), the emission-time position
$\satpos_{\mathrm{em}}$ is rotated by $\theta=\omega_\oplus\tau$ about the
$Z$ axis,
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\satpos
=
\bm{R}_z(\omega_\oplus\tau)\,\satpos_{\mathrm{em}},
\qquad
\bm{R}_z(\theta)
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\;\;\cos\theta & \sin\theta & 0\\
-\sin\theta & \cos\theta & 0\\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:obsphys-sagnac}
\end{empheq}
after which $\geomrange^\sat_\rx=\lVert\satpos-\rxpos_\rx\rVert$ and the
line of sight are computed from the rotated $\satpos$
(\texttt{model/ModelGeometry.cpp}, the \texttt{rotateAndComputeTopo}
lambda).  The Sagnac correction is thus \emph{folded into the geometric
range itself} rather than added as a separate term --- which is why
\eqref{eq:obsphys-assembly} carries no explicit Sagnac entry.  An
equivalent scalar form,
$(\omega_\oplus/\sLight)\,(x_\sat y_\rx - y_\sat x_\rx)$, exists in the
codebase as a \emph{diagnostic} only
(\texttt{GnssCorrections::computeSagnacCorrection},
\texttt{corrections/GnssCorrections.cpp}); it is the first-order expansion
of the rigorous rotation and is not on the modelling path.  The
\dcref{sec:products}{Orbit and Clock Product Evaluation document} cross-references
\emph{here} for the Sagnac frame rotation; the derivation is kept in this
document.  The same $\bm{R}_z(\omega(t_{\mathrm{node}}-t_{\mathrm{query}}))$
rotation is reused at SP3 interpolation time to pre-rotate ephemeris
nodes into the query-epoch frame
(\texttt{model/ModelGeometry.cpp}), a numerical-conditioning use of the
identical rotation documented with the products.

\subsection{Group delays and inter-frequency biases}\label{sec:obsphys-bias}
% ============================================================

The abstract bias terms of the Foundations observation equations --- the
satellite code/phase biases $B^\sat_\sigl,\,b^\sat_\sigl$ and the receiver
biases $B_{\rx,\sigl},\,b_{\rx,\sigl}$ --- are realised deterministically by
two products, which this section ties to the equation symbols.

\paragraph{Broadcast group delay.}
The broadcast navigation message carries a per-system inter-frequency
group delay (GPS TGD, Galileo BGD, BeiDou TGD), referencing each signal's
code hardware delay to the ionosphere-free combination used to generate
the broadcast clock.  In the uncombined parameterisation these are absorbed
into the deterministic code bias applied through
\texttt{model/PppModel.cpp}'s bias term; they are the broadcast-grade
realisation of $B^\sat_\sigl$, used when no precise bias product is
available.

\paragraph{Precise OSB / DCB biases.}
When a precise bias product is loaded, the deterministic correction is the
observable-specific bias (OSB) / differential code bias (DCB) entry for the
exact (\sat,\sigl) pair, time-windowed against its validity interval and
returned by \texttt{GnssCorrections::getBiasCorrection}
(\texttt{corrections/GnssCorrections.cpp}).  This single scalar realises
$B^\sat_\sigl$ (code) or $b^\sat_\sigl$ (phase) for that signal:
\begin{equation}
B_{\mathrm{grp}} = \mathrm{bias}(\sat,\sigl,t),
\qquad
t_{\mathrm{start}} \le t \le t_{\mathrm{end}},
\label{eq:obsphys-bias-window}
\end{equation}
with the application gated by
\texttt{enable\_code\_phase\_bias} (\texttt{model/PppModel.cpp}).  Two
exclusions are baked into the bias index
(\texttt{GnssCorrections::buildBiasIndex}): \emph{zero-valued} code entries
(e.g. MGEX placeholder $\mathtt{C2I}/\mathtt{C6I}$ for BeiDou) are treated
as \emph{absent} so the model does not both apply a null correction and
suppress the per-(\sat,\sigl) estimated bias state, leaving the real
tens-of-metres delay unmodelled; and \emph{GLONASS code} entries are
likewise treated as missing because the FDMA per-channel receiver delay
cannot be captured by a single value-per-(\sat,\sigl) bias, forcing those
signals onto the estimated-state path
(\texttt{corrections/GnssCorrections.cpp}).

\paragraph{Estimated vs.\ deterministic biases.}
Whatever the deterministic bias leaves uncorrected --- principally the
receiver hardware delay $B_{\rx,\sigl},\,b_{\rx,\sigl}$, and any satellite
delay not covered by a product --- is handled by the estimator.  The
receiver biases are common to all satellites on a signal and so collapse
into the receiver clock / inter-system bias states or cancel under
between-satellite differencing
(\dcref{sec:diff}{the differencing section}); the per-(\sat,\sigl)
satellite code bias may be \emph{estimated} as a state when no product
covers it (\texttt{estimation.estimate\_sat\_biases},
\texttt{measurement/MeasurementModel.cpp}).  The state-allocation gate
that prevents estimating a bias the products already supply ---
$\mathrm{hasBIA}(\sat,\sigl)$ from the static product, OR'd with
$\mathrm{hasSsrCB}(\sat,\sigl)$ from the live stream --- is documented with
the SSR overlay (\dcref{sec:ssr}{the SSR corrections document},
\Cref{eq:ssr-satbias-gate} there); the present document supplies only the
\emph{deterministic} half of that OR.

\paragraph{Relation to the SSR overlay.}
The streamed SSR code/phase OSB (\dcref{sec:ssr}{the SSR corrections
document}) refines the \emph{same} $B^\sat_\sigl,\,b^\sat_\sigl$ terms in
real time, summed onto the predicted observable as \texttt{ssr\_bias\_term}
in \texttt{measurement/MeasurementModel.cpp} alongside the deterministic
$B_{\mathrm{grp}}$ here.  The two are distinct mechanisms and must not be
conflated: the SSR bias is a per-(\sat,\sigl) scalar like
\eqref{eq:obsphys-bias-window}, but the SSR \emph{orbit} delta lives in a
radial/along/cross (RAC) delta frame
(\dcref{sec:ssr}{the SSR document}'s \Cref{eq:ssr-rac-frame}) that is
\emph{not} the satellite attitude body frame
\eqref{eq:obsphys-bodyframe-matrix} of this document --- the RAC frame is
built from the broadcast position and velocity to express an orbit
correction, whereas the body frame is built from the Sun direction to
orient the antenna.  They coincide neither in construction nor in purpose.

\subsection{Provenance and assembly}\label{sec:obsphys-provenance}
% ============================================================

All deterministic corrections converge in
\texttt{model/PppModel.cpp}: \texttt{computeBaseModeledValue}, the single
shared helper for both the iono-free-pair and uncombined paths, returns the
sum \eqref{eq:obsphys-assembly}, calling
\texttt{model/ModelGeometry.cpp} for the Sagnac-rotated geometric range
\eqref{eq:obsphys-sagnac}, \texttt{model/Antenna.cpp} for the total
antenna correction \eqref{eq:obsphys-antenna-total} (which in turn calls
\texttt{model/SatAttitude.cpp} for the body frame and
\texttt{corrections/GnssCorrections.cpp} for the wind-up), and
\texttt{corrections/GnssCorrections.cpp} for the relativistic
\eqref{eq:obsphys-rel}, Shapiro \eqref{eq:obsphys-shapiro} and bias
\eqref{eq:obsphys-bias-window} terms.  The Sun and Moon ephemeris feeding
the attitude frame \eqref{eq:obsphys-yaw-frame}, the Sun-elevation
\eqref{eq:obsphys-beta}, the eclipse predicate
\eqref{eq:obsphys-eclipse} and the wind-up
\eqref{eq:obsphys-windup-dipoles} is produced by
\texttt{model/Planetary.cpp} (the rigorous IAU precession/nutation Sun
position, cached per epoch) and is documented in
\dcref{sec:frames}{the Coordinate Frames and Time Systems document}.  Every term is invariant
physics; the only user-facing knobs are enable toggles and the
eclipse-shadow scale, all cited rather than reproduced because they tune
\emph{whether} a correction is applied, never its functional form.
