\section{Cycle-slip detection and repair}\label{sec:slip}
% ============================================================
%  Cycle-slip detection and repair
%  Linear combinations as test statistics + TurboEdit 2x2 repair.
%  Combination algebra only -- empirical thresholds (MAD / Welford /
%  EWMA cross-sat sigma) are pointers to code, not reproduced here.
%  Sources: src/core/slip/{MwSlipDetector,EwlSlipDetector,
%  GraphicSlipDetector,GfSlipDetector,IfPhaseSlipDetector,
%  TurboEditResolver}.cpp and the matching include/slip headers.
% ============================================================

The uncombined filter (\dcref{sec:obs}{the observation-equations
section of the Foundations document}) never forms a linear combination
on the measurement-update path: per-signal $\code^\sat_{\rx,\sigl}$ and
$\phaseo^\sat_{\rx,\sigl}$ enter \dcref{eq:obs-code,eq:obs-phase}{the uncombined code and phase observation equations}
directly.  Cycle slips are instead screened by a bank of
\emph{diagnostic} combinations, each constructed so that the geometric
range $\geomrange^\sat_\rx$, the clocks $\sLight(\mathrm{d}t_\rx -
\mathrm{d}t^\sat)$ and the troposphere $T^\sat_\rx$ cancel, leaving an
observable whose only between-epoch variation is measurement noise plus
any integer carrier discontinuity.  The active detectors are
Melbourne--W\"ubbena (\texttt{src/core/slip/MwSlipDetector.cpp}, the
primary) and GRAPHIC (\texttt{src/core/slip/GraphicSlipDetector.cpp});
the extra-wide-lane detector (\texttt{src/core/slip/EwlSlipDetector.cpp})
runs when a third frequency is present.  The geometry-free and
ionosphere-free phase detectors
(\texttt{src/core/slip/GfSlipDetector.cpp},
\texttt{src/core/slip/IfPhaseSlipDetector.cpp}) are staged --- present
in the tree but not compiled into the current build --- and their
algebra is recorded here for completeness.  The bank is composed at
run time from the config block \texttt{estimation.cycle\_slip}
(\Cref{sec:slip-registry}).

Throughout this section we suppress the receiver index $\rx$
(single-receiver PPP / PPP-RTK) and write $f_1 > f_2$ for the two
carriers of a band pair, sorted by frequency so that $f_1 - f_2 > 0$
and $\lambda_1 < \lambda_2$
(\texttt{src/core/slip/MwSlipDetector.cpp}, the band-frequency sort).
Carrier phase is read from RINEX in cycles and scaled by
$\lambda_\sigl = \sLight / f_\sigl$ before combining, so
$\phaseo^\sat_\sigl$ below is always in metres, consistent with
\dcref{eq:obs-phase}{the phase observation equation}.  We reserve the operator $\slipd{\,\cdot\,}$ for a
\emph{temporal} integer slip step (e.g.\ $\slipd N_\sigl$ is the change
in the ambiguity on signal $\sigl$ across the slip epoch); this is
deliberately distinct from the between-satellite single difference
$\bsd{\cdot}$ of \dcref{sec:diff}{the differencing section of the
Foundations document}, which also uses $\Delta$.

\subsection{Detector registry and composition}\label{sec:slip-registry}

The detectors are not hard-wired.  Each implements a narrow polymorphic
contract \texttt{ISlipDetector} (\texttt{include/slip/ISlipDetector.hpp})
--- a single per-epoch entry point
\begin{equation*}
\texttt{detect}(\text{SlipEpochContext}) \;\longrightarrow\; \{\,\text{SlipEvent}\,\},
\end{equation*}
plus lifecycle hooks (\texttt{resetSatellite},
\texttt{resetSatelliteSignal}, \texttt{enableTracing},
\texttt{setCorrections}).  A \texttt{SlipEvent} is the union of the
legacy per-detector result structs; its \texttt{granularity} field
distinguishes a \emph{per-signal} flag (GRAPHIC, GF, IF: one carrier)
from a \emph{band-difference} flag (MW, EWL: a wide-lane integer
$\slipd \Nwl = \slipd N_1 - \slipd N_2$ that, on its own, cannot
attribute the jump to one signal --- the two-unknown problem
\Cref{eq:te-mw} resolves downstream).  Inputs are bundled in
\texttt{SlipEpochContext} so the signature is uniform even though only
the staged IF detector consumes the extra 3D position sigma and
receiver ECEF it carries.

\texttt{SlipDetectorRegistry::createEnabled}
(\texttt{src/core/slip/SlipDetectorRegistry.cpp}) builds the
config-selected set from
\texttt{estimation.cycle\_slip.\{mw,graphic,ewl,gf,if\}} in a
deterministic order --- MW first (it is the primary and the sole source
of the wide-lane integers consumed by ambiguity resolution
(\dcref{sec:ar}{the ambiguity-resolution document}) and the BSSD pivot
machinery (\texttt{src/core/filter/PivotManager.cpp})), then
the auxiliaries.  MW defaults on; the auxiliary detectors default off,
preserving the historical MW-only behaviour.  A kind that is enabled in
config but \emph{unavailable} in this build --- the staged \texttt{gf}
and \texttt{if} detectors, whose \texttt{.cpp} is not compiled --- is
skipped with a warning rather than aborting, so a forward-looking config
degrades gracefully.  \texttt{CycleSlipManager} drives the whole set
through one loop and recovers the MW detector's richer API (WL-lock
queries, pivot-handover hints, per-satellite quality scoring) through a
typed down-cast (\texttt{asMelbourneWubbena}) rather than widening the
base interface.

\texttt{turbo\_edit} (\Cref{sec:slip-turboedit}) sits a level above the
registry: it is \emph{not} a detector kind but a \emph{coordination
mode} that, when the MW detector is enabled, pairs the MW wide-lane
integer with the geometry-free phase jump to recover per-signal
integers.  It is therefore documented separately from the detector bank
below.

\subsection{Test-statistic combinations}\label{sec:slip-combos}

\subsubsection{Melbourne--W\"ubbena (wide-lane)}\label{sec:slip-mw}

The Melbourne--W\"ubbena (MW) observable is the wide-lane carrier phase
minus the narrow-lane code.  Introducing the wide- and narrow-lane
wavelengths
\begin{equation}
\lamwl \;:=\; \frac{\sLight}{f_1 - f_2},
\qquad
\lamnl \;:=\; \frac{\sLight}{f_1 + f_2},
\label{eq:wl-nl-wavelength}
\end{equation}
the engine forms (\texttt{MwSlipDetector::computeMwMeters})
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\mwc^\sat
\;=\;
\underbrace{\frac{f_1\,\phaseo^\sat_{1} - f_2\,\phaseo^\sat_{2}}{f_1 - f_2}}_{\text{wide-lane phase}}
\;-\;
\underbrace{\frac{f_1\,\code^\sat_{1} + f_2\,\code^\sat_{2}}{f_1 + f_2}}_{\text{narrow-lane code}}.
\label{eq:mw}
\end{empheq}
The range-additive terms common to \dcref{eq:obs-code,eq:obs-phase}{the uncombined code and phase observation equations} ---
geometry, clocks, troposphere --- cancel between the two bracketed
combinations.  The slant ionosphere also cancels: the wide-lane phase
carries the ionospheric coefficient $-(f_1\mu_1 - f_2\mu_2)/(f_1-f_2)$
and the narrow-lane code carries $+(f_1\mu_1 + f_2\mu_2)/(f_1+f_2)$,
and with $\mu_\sigl = f_1^2/f_\sigl^2$ (\dcref{eq:iono-scaling}{the ionosphere-scaling relation}) these
two coefficients are equal, so the difference \eqref{eq:mw} removes the
ionosphere exactly to first order.  What remains is a near-constant
equal to the wide-lane ambiguity plus hardware bias,
\begin{equation}
\mwc^\sat
\;\approx\;
\lamwl\,\Nwl^\sat \;+\; b_{\mathrm{MW}}^\sat \;+\; \varepsilon_{\mathrm{MW}},
\qquad
\Nwl^\sat = N^\sat_{1} - N^\sat_{2},
\label{eq:mw-content}
\end{equation}
so a wide-lane slip $\slipd \Nwl = \slipd N_1 - \slipd N_2$ appears as a
step of $\lamwl\,\slipd \Nwl$.  For GPS L1/L2,
$\lamwl \approx \SI{0.861}{m}$ while the dominant error is the
metre-level narrow-lane \emph{code} noise; with code noise
$\sigma_\code \sim \SI{0.3}{m}$ the MW noise is
$\sigma_{\mathrm{MW}} \approx \sigma_\code / (\sqrt{2}\,\lamwl) \sim
0.25$ wide-lane cycles, i.e.\ code-noise-limited.  The integer-ready
(BIA-corrected) variant subtracts the satellite MW bias
$b_{\mathrm{MW}}^\sat$, which has the same algebra as \Cref{eq:mw}
applied to the OSB phase and code biases of \dcref{eq:obs-code,eq:obs-phase}{the uncombined code and phase observation equations}.

The level series $\mwc^\sat(k)$ is screened against a robust adaptive
gate whose deviation statistic is configurable via
\texttt{estimation.cycle\_slip.mw\_detect\_reference}.  The
recommended --- and replay-default --- choice is the first time
difference $\mathrm{TD1}(k) = \mwc^\sat(k) - \mwc^\sat(k-1)$, gated
against the median-absolute-deviation of its own history; the second
time difference $\mathrm{TD2}(k) = \mwc^\sat(k) - 2\,\mwc^\sat(k-1) +
\mwc^\sat(k-2)$ is available (\texttt{mw\_td\_order}) for curvature
immunity at a $\sqrt{6}$ noise penalty versus the $\sqrt{2}$ of TD1 (the
white-noise scaling of each statistic is the single source of truth in
\texttt{MwSlipDetector::activeStatScale}).  A windowed local median and
the legacy Hatch-mean reference are retained for A/B comparison
(\texttt{local\_median}, \texttt{hatch\_mean}).  The threshold itself is
fully data-driven --- no stochastic-model $\sigma$ enters --- combining
a per-arc MAD, an EWMA-smoothed cross-satellite MAD floor and an
elevation $1/\sin\theta$ shape, with a breadth cascade (band-pair
$\rightarrow$ constellation $\rightarrow$ global-no-GLONASS) for sparse
or cold-start arcs; the empirical construction lives in
\texttt{MwSlipDetector::computeAdaptiveThreshold} and is intentionally
not reproduced here.

\subsubsection{Extra-wide-lane (EWL)}\label{sec:slip-ewl}

For three-or-more frequency satellites the same MW algebra
(\Cref{eq:mw}) is applied to a pair of \emph{non-L1} carriers
(\texttt{EwlSlipDetector::computeEwlMwMeters}; band digit $\ge 2$).
Because the two carriers are spectrally close --- $f_1 - f_2$ small ---
the extra-wide-lane wavelength
\begin{equation}
\lamewl \;=\; \frac{\sLight}{f_1 - f_2}
\label{eq:ewl-wavelength}
\end{equation}
is very long: GPS L2/L5 gives $\lamewl \approx \SI{5.86}{m}$ and Galileo
E5a/E5b $\approx \SI{9.77}{m}$.  Metre-level code noise then maps to
$\lesssim 0.05$ EWL cycles, so the EWL integer $N_{\mathrm{EWL}} = N_2 -
N_5$ resolves in a single epoch and a slip $\slipd N_{\mathrm{EWL}}$ is
sized with near-certainty.  Together with the L1/L2 wide-lane
(\Cref{eq:mw-content}) this yields two independent band-difference
constraints,
\begin{equation}
\slipd \Nwl = \slipd N_1 - \slipd N_2,
\qquad
\slipd N_{\mathrm{EWL}} = \slipd N_2 - \slipd N_5,
\label{eq:wl-ewl-system}
\end{equation}
narrowing the per-signal triple $(\slipd N_1, \slipd N_2, \slipd N_5)$
to a single remaining degree of freedom \cite{geng2013triple}.

\subsubsection{GRAPHIC (single-frequency half-sum)}\label{sec:slip-graphic}

The GRAPHIC combination averages the code and phase on a \emph{single}
band (\texttt{GraphicSlipDetector::computeGraphic}):
\begin{equation}
\graphicc^\sat_{\sigl}
\;=\;
\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\code^\sat_{\sigl} + \phaseo^\sat_{\sigl}\bigr),
\label{eq:graphic}
\end{equation}
with $\phaseo^\sat_\sigl$ in metres.  Because the slant ionosphere
enters $+\mu_\sigl I^\sat_{\rx,1}$ on the code and $-\mu_\sigl
I^\sat_{\rx,1}$ on the phase of the \emph{same} signal
(\dcref{eq:obs-code,eq:obs-phase}{the uncombined code and phase observation equations}), the half-sum cancels it to first
order --- GRAPHIC is ionosphere-free without a second frequency, at the
cost of retaining half the code noise.  The combination still contains
the geometric range, clocks and troposphere plus the constant
half-ambiguity $\tfrac12 \lambda_\sigl N^\sat_\sigl$, so the
between-epoch first difference
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{d}\graphicc^\sat_\sigl(k)
\;=\;
\graphicc^\sat_\sigl(k) - \graphicc^\sat_\sigl(k-1)
\label{eq:graphic-diff}
\end{equation}
removes the constant ambiguity (and cancels range and clocks down to
receiver-motion / code-noise level).  A $\slipd N$-cycle slip on signal
$\sigl$ therefore produces a jump of $\tfrac12 \lambda_\sigl\,\slipd N$,
and the integer estimate is $\slipd N = \operatorname{round}\!\bigl(2\,
\mathrm{d}\graphicc / \lambda_\sigl\bigr)$.  With $\sigma_\code \sim
\SI{0.3}{m}$ the post-difference noise is $\sim\SI{0.3}{m}$, so
sensitivity is noise-limited to a few L1 cycles; in practice a hard
floor \texttt{kMinThresholdM} $= \SI{0.5}{m}$ governs on quiet arcs
($\approx 5$ L1 cycles), keeping GRAPHIC a coarse backstop rather than a
sub-cycle detector \cite{yunck1993coping,banville2013mitigating}.

\subsubsection{Geometry-free phase (staged)}\label{sec:slip-gf}

The geometry-free (GF) observable is the between-band phase difference
\begin{equation}
\gfc^\sat \;=\; \phaseo^\sat_{1} - \phaseo^\sat_{2}
\;\approx\;
(\mu_2 - \mu_1)\,I^\sat_{\rx,1}
\;+\; (\lambda_1 N^\sat_1 - \lambda_2 N^\sat_2),
\label{eq:gf}
\end{equation}
which removes geometry, clocks and troposphere exactly but retains the
\emph{difference} of the two ionospheric delays.  Since $I^\sat_{\rx,1}$
varies smoothly, a second-order time difference
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{TD2}_{\gfc}(k)
\;=\;
\gfc^\sat(k) - 2\,\gfc^\sat(k-1) + \gfc^\sat(k-2)
\label{eq:gf-td2}
\end{equation}
cancels the ionospheric trend up to quadratic order over the sampling
interval, leaving noise plus slip
(\texttt{GfSlipDetector::computeGfMeters} for \Cref{eq:gf}; the TD2
update is the level-jump test statistic).  The detector is intrinsically
high-rate ($\ge \SI{1}{Hz}$): at coarse rate the residual ionospheric
curvature defeats \eqref{eq:gf-td2}.  It is present in the tree but
excluded from the build; the same algebra backs the active
\texttt{src/core/slip/IonoVariabilityMonitor.cpp}.

\subsubsection{Ionosphere-free phase (staged)}\label{sec:slip-if}

The dual-frequency ionosphere-free (IF) phase combination uses the
coefficients
\begin{equation}
c_1 = \frac{f_1^{2}}{f_1^{2} - f_2^{2}},
\qquad
c_2 = \frac{f_2^{2}}{f_1^{2} - f_2^{2}},
\qquad
c_1 - c_2 = 1,
\label{eq:if-coeffs}
\end{equation}
giving $\phaseo^\sat_{\mathrm{IF}} = c_1 \phaseo^\sat_1 - c_2
\phaseo^\sat_2$ and the effective wavelength $\lamif = \bigl|c_1
\lambda_1 - c_2 \lambda_2\bigr|$
(\texttt{IfPhaseSlipDetector::computeIfWavelengthMeters}); for GPS L1/L2
$\lamif \approx \SI{0.107}{m}$.  Rather than screen the raw combination,
the staged detector forms the IF observed-minus-computed (OMC),
differences it between satellites against a high-elevation pivot
$\piv{g}$ to remove the receiver clock, the inter-system offset
$\sLight\,\delta t^{(g)}_\rx$ and the satellite phase clock, then
between epochs to remove the slowly-varying wet troposphere and the
constant ambiguity:
\begin{equation}
\dd{\phaseo^{\sat,\piv{g}}_{\mathrm{IF}}}(k)
=
\bigl[\phaseo^\sat_{\mathrm{IF}}(k) - \phaseo^{\piv{g}}_{\mathrm{IF}}(k)\bigr]
-
\bigl[\phaseo^\sat_{\mathrm{IF}}(k-1) - \phaseo^{\piv{g}}_{\mathrm{IF}}(k-1)\bigr],
\label{eq:if-dd}
\end{equation}
which is near zero for a clean arc and jumps by $\sim \slipd N\,\lamif$
on a slip.  The between-satellite step does not cancel the differential
geometric error $(\los^\sat - \los^{\piv{g}})^{\Tr} \delta\rxpos$, so
flagging must be gated by the filter's 3D position sigma; this restricts
the staged detector to a static site with a precise a priori position,
which is why \texttt{estimation.enable\_if\_slip\_detector}
is currently inert.

\subsection{TurboEdit dual-frequency resolution}\label{sec:slip-turboedit}

The TurboEdit resolver is a \emph{pure} MW+GF joint integer solver
(\texttt{src/core/slip/TurboEditResolver.cpp} and its header
\texttt{include/slip/TurboEditResolver.hpp}).  It owns the per-arc geometry-free jump history
and the integer arithmetic, and returns per-signal integer repairs plus
a reset-fallback set without mutating filter state; it is wired into
both the post-processing \texttt{CycleSlipManager} and the real-time
engine, and enabled by \texttt{estimation.cycle\_slip.turbo\_edit}.

\subsubsection{The 2$\times$2 system}\label{sec:slip-te-system}

For a dual-frequency phase pair $(\sigl_1, \sigl_2)$ on satellite
$\sat$, ordered higher-frequency first ($f_{\sigl_1} > f_{\sigl_2}$,
hence $\lambda_{\sigl_1} < \lambda_{\sigl_2}$), the resolver pairs two
scalar slip observables.  The first is the MW wide-lane integer of
\Cref{eq:mw-content}, taken \emph{as input} from the MW detector and
never recomputed (the empirically tuned MW path of
\Cref{sec:slip-mw} is the single source of $\slipd \Nwl$):
\begin{equation}
\slipd \Nwl \;=\; \slipd N_{\sigl_1} - \slipd N_{\sigl_2}
\qquad\text{(one equation, two unknowns).}
\label{eq:te-mw}
\end{equation}
The second is the GF phase jump in metres.  Writing the GF level as
$\gfc(t) = \lambda_{\sigl_1} L_{\sigl_1}(t) - \lambda_{\sigl_2}
L_{\sigl_2}(t)$ (carrier in cycles), a simultaneous slip steps its
ambiguity content by
\begin{equation}
\slipd \gfc
\;=\;
\lambda_{\sigl_1}\,\slipd N_{\sigl_1} - \lambda_{\sigl_2}\,\slipd N_{\sigl_2},
\label{eq:te-gf}
\end{equation}
estimated in-arc by the second-order time difference $\mathrm{TD2}(t) =
\gfc(t) - 2\,\gfc(t-1) + \gfc(t-2)$ of \Cref{eq:gf-td2}, which cancels
the ionospheric delay up to quadratic order so that at the slip epoch it
equals the integer jump plus phase noise only.  The TD2 chain requires
three \emph{consecutive} epochs; non-consecutive history forces a chain
re-seed (\Cref{sec:slip-te-bridge}).

Stacking \Cref{eq:te-mw,eq:te-gf} gives the linear map from the integer
slip pair to the two observed jumps:
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\begin{pmatrix} \slipd \Nwl \\[2pt] \slipd \gfc \end{pmatrix}
\;=\;
\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\[2pt] \lambda_{\sigl_1} & -\lambda_{\sigl_2} \end{pmatrix}}_{\bm A}
\begin{pmatrix} \slipd N_{\sigl_1} \\[2pt] \slipd N_{\sigl_2} \end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:te-2x2}
\end{empheq}
The determinant $\det \bm A = \lambda_{\sigl_1} - \lambda_{\sigl_2}$ is
non-zero for any genuine cross-band pair --- it is the cm-scale quantity
\texttt{denom} of \Cref{eq:te-solve}, $\approx \SI{-0.0539}{m}$ for GPS
L1/L2, \emph{not} the $\sim\!\SI{0.86}{m}$ wide-lane wavelength --- so
the system is always invertible:
\begin{equation}
\bm A^{-1}
=
\frac{1}{\lambda_{\sigl_1} - \lambda_{\sigl_2}}
\begin{pmatrix} -\lambda_{\sigl_2} & 1 \\[2pt] -\lambda_{\sigl_1} & 1 \end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:te-inv}
\end{equation}
The implementation does not invert the full matrix; it solves the second
row for $\slipd N_{\sigl_2}$ and back-substitutes the first
(\texttt{TurboEditResolver.cpp}):
\begin{equation}
\slipd N_{\sigl_2}
\;=\;
\frac{\slipd \gfc - \lambda_{\sigl_1}\,\slipd \Nwl}{\lambda_{\sigl_1} - \lambda_{\sigl_2}},
\qquad
\slipd N_{\sigl_1}
\;=\;
\slipd N_{\sigl_2} + \slipd \Nwl,
\label{eq:te-solve}
\end{equation}
with $\slipd \gfc$ instantiated either by the in-arc TD2 or, across a
data gap, by the bridged jump of \Cref{sec:slip-te-bridge}.  The
denominator $\lambda_{\sigl_1} - \lambda_{\sigl_2}$ (the variable
\texttt{denom} in the source) equals $\approx \SI{-0.0539}{m}$ for GPS
L1/L2, so the amplification factor on the wide-lane integer is
$\lambda_{\sigl_1} / (\lambda_{\sigl_1} - \lambda_{\sigl_2}) \approx
-3.53$: the GF equation must localise $\slipd N_{\sigl_2}$ to well
within $\pm 0.5$ cycle despite that lever, which is what the
split-capability gate below enforces.

\subsubsection{Integer rounding and validation}\label{sec:slip-te-round}

The float $\slipd N_{\sigl_2}$ from \Cref{eq:te-solve} is rounded and
its residual recorded:
\begin{equation}
d_2 = \operatorname{round}\!\bigl(\slipd N_{\sigl_2}\bigr),
\quad
f = \bigl|\slipd N_{\sigl_2} - d_2\bigr|,
\quad
d_1 = d_2 + \slipd \Nwl.
\label{eq:te-round}
\end{equation}
A candidate is \emph{accepted} as a repair only if the rounding is clean
($f \le 0.30$, the constant \texttt{kMaxFractional}; a $1 - 0.30 = 0.70$
cycle margin to the nearest wrong integer), the magnitudes are sane
($|d_1|, |d_2| \le \texttt{kMaxSlipCycles}$), and the pair is non-trivial
($d_1 \neq 0 \lor d_2 \neq 0$).  A non-trivial accepted candidate yields
\texttt{action="repair"}; $(d_1, d_2) = (0, 0)$ is resolved as no-slip
and the arc keeps rolling; otherwise the satellite is reset.

Before the joint solve is \emph{trusted}, the GF evidence must be sharp
enough to discriminate the integer lattice.  The float $\slipd
N_{\sigl_2}$ carries $\sigma_{\mathrm{TD2}} / |\lambda_{\sigl_1} -
\lambda_{\sigl_2}|$ cycles of noise, so the solve is permitted only when
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\mathrm{TD2}}
\;\le\;
\texttt{turbo\_edit\_split\_max\_sigma}\,\cdot\,
\bigl|\lambda_{\sigl_1} - \lambda_{\sigl_2}\bigr|
\qquad(\texttt{splitCapable}),
\label{eq:te-split}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_{\mathrm{TD2}}$ the arc's own MAD-derived $\sigma$ when
mature, else the cross-satellite tier floor, else NaN (NaN fails by
design --- ``no estimate'' is not evidence of sharpness).  The default
\texttt{estimation.cycle\_slip.turbo\_edit\_split\_max\_sigma} is
$0.2$ cycles.  This gate is the single mechanism making the resolver
rate-adaptive: millimetric $\SI{1}{s}$ noise passes trivially, while
coarse-rate ($\SI{30}{s}$) ionospheric residual passes only while the
arc is quiet.  On an MW-flagged pair failing the gate the resolver falls
back to a satellite reset; a GF-only exceedance on a split-incapable arc
is \emph{ignored}, not reset.  When $\slipd \Nwl = 0$ (an equal slip
$\slipd N_{\sigl_1} = \slipd N_{\sigl_2}$, to which MW is blind) a
GF-only flag fires on $|\mathrm{TD2}|$ exceeding an adaptive threshold
$\tau = \max(k_\sigma \hat\sigma_{\mathrm{TD2}}, \SI{0.04}{m})$, with
$k_\sigma$ the \texttt{turbo\_edit\_sigma\_multiplier}; the floor sits
just below $|\lambda_{\sigl_1} - \lambda_{\sigl_2}| \approx
\SI{0.054}{m}$ so a $\pm 1$-cycle equal slip remains detectable.

\subsubsection{MW-only / code-event null line}\label{sec:slip-te-codeevent}

The asymmetric case is MW flagged but the GF jump \emph{affirmatively
zero}.  For a fixed $\slipd \Nwl = k$, every integer pair $(\slipd
N_{\sigl_1}, \slipd N_{\sigl_1} - k)$ predicts, via \Cref{eq:te-gf}, a GF
jump
\begin{equation}
\slipd \gfc
\;=\;
\lambda_{\sigl_2} k - (\lambda_{\sigl_2} - \lambda_{\sigl_1})\,\slipd N_{\sigl_1},
\label{eq:te-nullline}
\end{equation}
whose zero crossing sits at $\slipd N_{\sigl_1} = \lambda_{\sigl_2} k /
(\lambda_{\sigl_2} - \lambda_{\sigl_1}) \approx 4.53\,k$ for GPS L1/L2
($\approx 3.95\,k$ for L1/L5).  Every small pair $|\slipd N_{\sigl_1}|
\le 3$ then predicts $|\slipd \gfc| \ge \SI{8}{cm}$ --- orders above
phase noise --- so a flat GF leaves only \emph{null-line} pairs of
$(4,3)$ / $(9,7)$-class magnitude: implausible multi-cycle simultaneous
slips on both signals with no loss of lock.  The Occam-simpler
explanation is a narrow-lane \emph{code} excursion (multipath, NLOS, DLL
re-lock), since $\mwc = \phaseo_{\mathrm{WL}} - \code_{\mathrm{NL}}$ and
one wide-lane cycle $\approx \SI{0.86}{m}$ of narrow-lane code.

Accordingly the pair is classified a code-domain artifact --- and the
caller \emph{skips} the phase reset --- when the GF jump is affirmatively
zero within a discrimination-bounded gate,
\begin{equation}
|\mathrm{TD2}|
\le
\underbrace{\texttt{kCodeEventZeroSigma}\cdot\hat\sigma_{\mathrm{MAD}}}_{\text{zero gate}}
\quad\wedge\quad
\text{zero gate}
\le
\underbrace{\texttt{kVetoZeroGateMaxLambdaFrac}\cdot|\lambda_{\sigl_1} - \lambda_{\sigl_2}|}_{\text{discrimination cap}},
\label{eq:te-codeevent}
\end{equation}
with $\texttt{kCodeEventZeroSigma} = 3.5$ ($\approx 99.95\%$ two-sided)
and $\texttt{kVetoZeroGateMaxLambdaFrac} = 0.5$.  The cap is the
rate-adaptive safeguard: a zero gate wider than half the smallest
GF-visible quantum cannot distinguish stillness from a small slip, so
the veto stands down and the MW mandate governs.  The classification is
further trust-gated --- veto enabled, MW flagged, GF not exceeding, no
post-slip cooldown, ionospherically stable constellation, no LLI on
either signal, and MAD maturity $\ge 10$ samples (stricter than
detection's $5$, because affirming stillness is a state-\emph{preserving}
decision).  The implied null-line pair is computed for the trace only,
never applied; \texttt{did\_slip} stays false so the GF chain keeps
rolling.  A genuine null-line slip is not lost: it jumps that signal's
Kalman phase innovation by $\ge |4\lambda_{\sigl_1}| \approx \SI{0.76}{m}$
this very epoch, far beyond any residual gate, so the residual-driven
reset corrects it one epoch late.  The behaviour is toggled by
\texttt{estimation.cycle\_slip.turbo\_edit\_mw\_only\_veto} (default on).

\subsubsection{Gap-spanning GF bridge}\label{sec:slip-te-bridge}

The TD2 chain needs three consecutive epochs; any per-signal dropout or
receiver gap re-seeds the chain, making the post-gap TD2 \emph{blind} to
a re-lock slip whose GF level jump lives \emph{between} the two chains.
When a chain break occurs within
\texttt{estimation.cycle\_slip.turbo\_edit\_max\_gap\_s}, the resolver
stashes the pre-gap GF level $\gfc_{\mathrm{ref}}$, its timestamp, the
arc's EWMA GF rate $\dot{\gfc}$ and the pre-gap spacing $dt_0$, and on
return forms the drift-corrected across-gap jump
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\slipd \gfc_{\mathrm{gap}}
\;=\;
\gfc_{\mathrm{now}} - \gfc_{\mathrm{ref}} - \dot{\gfc}\,\Delta t,
\qquad
\Delta t = t_{\mathrm{now}} - t_{\mathrm{ref}},
\label{eq:te-bridge}
\end{empheq}
which then plays the role of $\slipd \gfc$ in \Cref{eq:te-solve}.  Here
$\dot{\gfc}$ is an EWMA ($\alpha = 0.1$) of the per-epoch GF rate, chosen
so its noise does not amplify when projected over the gap.  Because the
un-cancelled ionospheric residual is random-walk over the gap, the
detection threshold and the code-event zero gate are widened by the
factor $w = \sqrt{\Delta t / dt_0}$ (for $\Delta t > dt_0$), and the
split-capability $\sigma$ becomes $\sigma_{\mathrm{bridge}} =
\hat\sigma_{\mathrm{MAD}}\,w$; the zero-gate \emph{discrimination cap}
stays un-widened, since the integer lattice of \Cref{eq:te-2x2} does not
stretch with the gap.  The bridge cap default is $\SI{15}{s}$ in code,
raised to $\SI{60}{s}$ in the bundled real-time and replay configs.

An MW-flagged pair across the gap is attributed three ways.  (i) A
\emph{code event} (\texttt{action="code\_event"}) when $\slipd
\gfc_{\mathrm{gap}}$ is affirmatively zero within the widened gate ---
the same null-line logic as \Cref{eq:te-codeevent}, applied across the
gap --- preserving phase through a re-acquisition DLL transient.
(ii) A \emph{repair} or \emph{moot} when the joint solve runs with
$\slipd \gfc = \slipd \gfc_{\mathrm{gap}}$; per signal it is dispatched
by observation-domain continuity, a signal observed at every epoch
through the gap kept its ambiguity (its nonzero $\slipd N$ is a genuine
\emph{repair}), whereas a signal that missed an epoch was
pruned-and-reseeded on return (its nonzero $\slipd N$ is \emph{moot} ---
already absorbed, mandate suppressed).  (iii) A \emph{reset} when the
across-gap $\sigma$ is too coarse for the split, or the candidate set
fits zero or two integers with no deferral.

Because the GF equation alone is degenerate along the null-line lattice
of \Cref{eq:te-nullline} --- $+\SI{0.735}{m}$ fits both $(0,-3)$ and
$(9,4)$ at millimetre level; only the integer $k$ separates them --- the
bridged solve demands a \emph{unique} surviving candidate after three
guards: the fractional gate of \Cref{eq:te-round}; a GF-separation guard
requiring the candidate's own predicted jump to clear the zero gate,
$|\lambda_{\sigl_1} d_1 - \lambda_{\sigl_2} d_2| > \texttt{zero\_gate}$,
lest a marginal noise exceedance mint a multi-cycle repair from noise;
and a sibling-pair zero affirmation, whereby a satellite's \emph{other}
GF arc that stayed chain-continuous and unflagged through the bridged
interval affirms $\slipd N = 0$ for both its signals and prunes any
candidate demanding a nonzero integer on an affirmed signal.  The
wide-lane integer feeding the bridged solve is taken in trust order: the
published MW integer $k$; failing that, at the flag epoch,
$\operatorname{round}(f) \pm 3$ around the (NL-code-biased) instantaneous
re-acquisition float; and on deferral epochs the settled across-gap MW
Hatch mean.  When the float wide-lane evidence is unsettled or the
lattice is ambiguous (two or more acceptable integers), the solve is
\emph{deferred} rather than guessed
(\texttt{estimation.cycle\_slip.turbo\_edit\_wl\_settle\_max\_s}),
terminating in a later repair, reset or timeout-reset.

\subsubsection{Atomic reconciliation}\label{sec:slip-te-atomic}

Per-satellite candidates are reconciled atomically: a cross-pair integer
disagreement on a shared signal demotes the whole satellite to reset,
and a satellite ending in the reset set has its code-event, moot, clean
and pending classifications \emph{withdrawn} --- contradictory evidence
always resolves to the conservative outcome.  Arc bookkeeping keeps the
TD2 validity precondition honest: a non-consecutive update partially
resets the chain but preserves the slowly-varying MAD calibration
window, an absolute gap beyond the configured maximum wipes the arc, and
an accepted slip re-seeds with a short cooldown to suppress the TD2 echo;
only clean (non-exceeding, non-bridged) TD2 samples feed the MAD window.
