\section{Worked example: estimability in a two-receiver network}
\label{sec:net-example}

This section makes the rank deficiency and minimum-constraint datum of
\dcref{sec:net-sbasis}{the minimum-constraint datum section of the Network-mode document} concrete on the smallest network that still shows
the structure: two receivers $\rxset = \{A, B\}$ and four satellites
$\sat \in \{1,2,3,4\}$. We strip everything that is not essential to the
estimability argument --- positions are known so the geometric range is
removed, a single frequency is used, the troposphere and ionosphere are
absent, and the data are noise-free --- so that every number below is
\emph{exact} and the gauge freedoms are visible by inspection. The point
is not the size of the system but its null space.

\subsection{Code only: clocks and one gauge freedom}
\label{sec:net-example-code}

With geometry removed, the single-frequency pseudorange of
\dcref{sec:obs}{the observation-equations section} collapses to a
difference of two clock lumps,
\begin{equation}
\code^\sat_\rx \;=\; a_\rx \;-\; g^\sat,
\qquad
a_\rx := \sLight\,\mathrm{d}t_\rx + B_\rx,
\quad
g^\sat := \sLight\,\mathrm{d}t^\sat + B^\sat,
\label{eq:ex-code-model}
\end{equation}
where $a_\rx$ is the receiver clock and $g^\sat$ the satellite clock. On
a single frequency the receiver and satellite \emph{code biases}
$B_\rx, B^\sat$ are inseparable from the respective clocks, so each
``clock'' here is already a clock\,+\,code-bias lump --- the
$\mathrm{IF}$-vs-$\mathrm{DCB}$ split of \dcref{sec:net-phasebias}{the satellite phase-bias section of the Network-mode document}
degenerates to nothing with one band. There are
$|\rxset| + |\{\sat\}| = 2 + 4 = 6$ unknowns and
$2 \times 4 = 8$ observations.

Take as ground truth (metres)
\begin{equation*}
a_A = 5,\quad a_B = 3,\qquad
g^1 = 2,\quad g^2 = -1,\quad g^3 = 4,\quad g^4 = 0.5,
\end{equation*}
which through \Cref{eq:ex-code-model} produces the eight pseudoranges
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
$\code^\sat_\rx$ & $\sat=1$ & $\sat=2$ & $\sat=3$ & $\sat=4$ \\
\midrule
$\rx = A$ & $3$ & $6$ & $1$ & $4.5$ \\
$\rx = B$ & $1$ & $4$ & $-1$ & $2.5$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{The deficiency, seen two ways.}
\emph{(i) Invariance.} Add any constant $\kappa$ to \emph{all six}
unknowns, $a_\rx \mapsto a_\rx + \kappa$ and $g^\sat \mapsto g^\sat +
\kappa$; the difference $a_\rx - g^\sat$, and therefore every
observation, is unchanged. The truth above and the shifted set
$\{15,13,12,9,14,10.5\}$ fit the data identically. Absolute clocks are
unknowable; only differences are.

\emph{(ii) Singular normals.} Ordering the unknowns
$\bm{x} = [\,a_A, a_B, g^1, g^2, g^3, g^4\,]^\Tr$ and using unit weights,
the normal matrix $\bm{N} = \bm{A}^\Tr\bm{A}$ is
\begin{equation}
\bm{N} =
\begin{bmatrix}
\phantom{-}4 & \phantom{-}0 & -1 & -1 & -1 & -1 \\
\phantom{-}0 & \phantom{-}4 & -1 & -1 & -1 & -1 \\
-1 & -1 & \phantom{-}2 & \phantom{-}0 & \phantom{-}0 & \phantom{-}0 \\
-1 & -1 & \phantom{-}0 & \phantom{-}2 & \phantom{-}0 & \phantom{-}0 \\
-1 & -1 & \phantom{-}0 & \phantom{-}0 & \phantom{-}2 & \phantom{-}0 \\
-1 & -1 & \phantom{-}0 & \phantom{-}0 & \phantom{-}0 & \phantom{-}2
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\bm{N}\,\bm{1} = \bm{0},
\label{eq:ex-code-normals}
\end{equation}
which is $6\times6$ but has rank $5$: the all-ones vector
$\bm{1} = [1,1,1,1,1,1]^\Tr$ is an exact null vector (every row sums to
zero), and it is precisely the common-shift direction of view~(i). The
system $\bm{N}\bm{x} = \bm{u}$ cannot be solved without one added
constraint --- a \emph{datum}.

\paragraph{Two datum choices, same fit.}
Pinning one clock removes the gauge. Two natural choices give
\emph{different} parameter values that fit the eight observations
\emph{equally and exactly}:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccccc@{}}
\toprule
datum (S-basis) & $a_A$ & $a_B$ & $g^1$ & $g^2$ & $g^3$ & $g^4$ \\
\midrule
$a_A \equiv 0$ (reference receiver) & $0$ & $-2$ & $-3$ & $-6$ & $-1$ & $-4.5$ \\
$g^1 \equiv 0$ (reference satellite) & $3$ & $\phantom{-}1$ & $\phantom{-}0$ & $-3$ & $\phantom{-}2$ & $-1.5$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Under $a_A \equiv 0$, every satellite-clock estimate comes out as
$\hat g^\sat = g^\sat - a_A^{\text{true}}$: the reference receiver's
clock is \emph{absorbed} into all of them. The estimated ``satellite
clock'' is not the physical clock; it is the physical clock plus the
datum we chose --- the \emph{lumping} that the user must add back. This
is exactly why a producer must \emph{declare} its datum
(\dcref{sec:net-closedloop}{the closed-loop section of the Network-mode document}).

\paragraph{What is invariant.}
The estimable quantities --- combinations annihilated by the null vector
$\bm{1}$, i.e.\ differences --- are identical in both bases:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
estimable quantity & $a_A \equiv 0$ & $g^1 \equiv 0$ & (truth) \\
\midrule
$a_A - a_B$  & $2$  & $2$  & $2$ \\
$g^2 - g^1$  & $-3$ & $-3$ & $-3$ \\
$g^3 - g^1$  & $2$  & $2$  & $2$ \\
$a_B - g^4$  & $2.5$ & $2.5$ & ($=\code^4_B$) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Only the absolute labels are convention; the differences are physics.
This is the S-system of \cite{teunissen1985generalised} in six
parameters.

\begin{remark}[Why the producer cannot difference]
Form the double difference of this code-only model,
$\dd{\code^{\sat,1}_{AB}} = (\code^1_A - \code^\sat_A) - (\code^1_B -
\code^\sat_B)$. The between-satellite step gives $g^\sat - g^1$ for
\emph{both} receivers, so the between-receiver step yields exactly
$\bm{0}$. Double differencing annihilates the entire model, because DD
is built to delete clocks and here clocks are all there is
(\dcref{sec:diff}{the differencing section}). A DD user would estimate
nothing; the network producer instead \emph{keeps} the clocks and pins
one datum. That degeneracy is the producer's reason for existence in
miniature.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Adding carrier phase: the satellite phase bias needs the network}
\label{sec:net-example-phase}

Now add the single-frequency carrier phase. With the same clock lumps it
reads
\begin{equation}
\phaseo^\sat_\rx \;=\; a_\rx - g^\sat \;+\; b^\sat \;+\; N^\sat_\rx,
\label{eq:ex-phase-model}
\end{equation}
where $b^\sat$ is the satellite phase bias --- the producer's target ---
and $N^\sat_\rx$ is the float ambiguity (in metres). The receiver phase
bias and the receiver code-minus-phase offset are constant per receiver
and have been absorbed into $N^\sat_\rx$; we keep $b^\sat$ explicit
because it is what the producer must emit. Extend the truth with
\begin{align*}
b^1 &= 0.10, & b^2 &= -0.20, & b^3 &= 0.05, & b^4 &= 0.15, \\
N^\sat_A &: 10,\ -5,\ 20,\ 8, & & & N^\sat_B &: 12,\ -3,\ 18,\ 9, & &
\end{align*}
giving the eight carrier-phase observations
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
$\phaseo^\sat_\rx$ & $\sat=1$ & $\sat=2$ & $\sat=3$ & $\sat=4$ \\
\midrule
$\rx = A$ & $13.10$ & $0.80$ & $21.05$ & $12.65$ \\
$\rx = B$ & $13.10$ & $0.80$ & $17.05$ & $11.65$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Counting: one receiver vs.\ two.}
The clocks are fixed by the code, so per satellite the phase contributes
$b^\sat + N^\sat_\rx$. With \emph{one} receiver this is a single equation
$b^\sat + N^\sat_A$ in two unknowns: the satellite phase bias has no
separate existence at all --- it is inseparable from that one
receiver's ambiguity, and not even redundant. With \emph{two} receivers
there are two equations,
\begin{equation}
b^\sat + N^\sat_A, \qquad b^\sat + N^\sat_B,
\label{eq:ex-phase-pair}
\end{equation}
in three unknowns $(b^\sat, N^\sat_A, N^\sat_B)$: rank $2$, one
deficiency per satellite. The estimable two-dimensional space now
contains the \emph{between-receiver single-difference ambiguity}
$\brd{N^\sat_{AB}} = N^\sat_A - N^\sat_B$ --- a quantity that simply does
not exist with one receiver, and is exactly the integer a user resolves
(\dcref{sec:diff}{the differencing section}). The second receiver is
what gives $b^\sat$ an identity shared across stations.

\paragraph{Datum and solution.}
The joint code\,+\,phase system has a $1$-dimensional clock gauge (as
before) plus one phase-bias$\leftrightarrow$ambiguity gauge per
satellite: $b^\sat \mapsto b^\sat + c_\sat$, $N^\sat_\rx \mapsto
N^\sat_\rx - c_\sat$ for all $\rx$. The minimum-constraint datum used
here is the clock pin $a_A \equiv 0$ --- S-transform-equivalent to the
reference-\emph{satellite} clock pin $g^1 \equiv 0$ that the estimator's
S-basis actually declares
(\dcref{sec:net-sbasis}{the minimum-constraint datum section of the Network-mode document}: the shared-block gauge pins a satellite clock
per constellation, not a receiver clock) --- plus the reference-receiver
ambiguity pins $N^\sat_A \equiv 0$ for every $\sat$ (the local datum on
the pivot receiver, folded into station $A$'s rows). Solving gives
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
& $\sat=1$ & $\sat=2$ & $\sat=3$ & $\sat=4$ \\
\midrule
emitted phase bias $\bar b^\sat = b^\sat + N^\sat_A$ & $10.10$ & $-5.20$ & $20.05$ & $8.15$ \\
$\hat N^\sat_A$ (datum) & $0$ & $0$ & $0$ & $0$ \\
$\hat N^\sat_B = N^\sat_B - N^\sat_A$ & $2$ & $2$ & $-2$ & $1$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
(the clocks are unchanged from
\Cref{sec:net-example-code}). One checks the fit directly, e.g.\
$\hat{\phaseo}^3_B = a_B - g^3 + \bar b^3 + \hat N^3_B
   = -2 -(-1) + 20.05 + (-2) = 17.05$, matching the observation.

\paragraph{Reading the result.}
Two things are worth dwelling on.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{The emitted bias is dominated by the lump.} The physical
      satellite phase bias of satellite~$1$ is $b^1 = 0.10$~m, but the
      \emph{emitted} estimable bias is $\bar b^1 = 10.10$~m: it has
      absorbed receiver~$A$'s ambiguity ($10$~m) for that satellite.
      This is Odijk's $\bar b^\sat = b^\sat + (\text{pivot-receiver
      ambiguity})$ (\dcref{sec:net-phasebias}{the satellite phase-bias section of the Network-mode document}, Eq.~4/9) made arithmetic.
      A consumer recovers integers only by removing the \emph{same}
      lumped quantity --- producer datum and consumer integer recovery
      must be one S-system.
\item \textbf{The network unlocks the integer.} The estimable
      $\hat N^\sat_B = N^\sat_B - N^\sat_A = \brd{N^\sat_{AB}}$ is the
      between-receiver single-difference ambiguity, the very quantity an
      RTK user fixes to an integer. It is identically absent in a
      single-receiver problem; the second station is what brings it into
      existence. The satellite phase bias becomes a definite,
      transferable number for the price of pinning one reference
      ambiguity --- and that pin is what it carries.
\end{itemize}

This four-satellite, two-receiver instance is, up to the geometry and
the second frequency it suppresses, exactly the synthetic network the
estimator core is validated on: the
\texttt{RecoversSyntheticTruthUpToSTransform} test
(\texttt{tests/test\_network\_estimator.cpp}) builds rows of exactly
this shape --- at four receivers and five satellites, from a random
truth --- pins the reference-satellite clock datum $g^1 \equiv 0$
(S-transform-equivalent to the $a_A \equiv 0$ used above) through
\texttt{NetworkEstimator::solve}, and asserts
recovery of the satellite-side parameters \emph{up to the declared
S-transform} --- i.e.\ recovery of the lumped $\bar b^\sat$ and the
single-difference ambiguities above, not of the unobservable physical
$b^\sat$.
