This document covers the \emph{measurement} side of the estimator's error
budget: the strategies that populate the observation-noise covariance
$\Rmat$, and the a-posteriori quality-control (QC) layer that validates an
epoch's residuals against that $\Rmat$.  The $\Rmat$ produced here is the
block-diagonal skeleton of \dcref{eq:noise-model}{the observation-equations
noise model}; this document supplies the per-strategy variance interiors of
that skeleton.  The QC layer is built on the same post-fit residual statistics
the filter already computes for outlier rejection, but reframes them as the
classical geodetic detection theory of Baarda and Teunissen
\cite{teunissen2017handbook}.

Two scopes are deliberately \emph{out} of this document and only
cross-referenced.  First, the standardised-residual outlier path
($|\sres|>6$), the Huber re-weighting, and the EWMA $\Rmat$-inflation that
react to a detected blunder live in
\dcref{sec:kf-gating}{the Kalman-filter gating section} --- this document
explains how the QC statistics feed an \emph{alternative} gate but does not
re-derive the dispatch.  Second, the per-state process-noise covariance
$\Qmat$ (clock random walk, ZWD, ambiguity, IMU bias) is purely a
\emph{dynamics}-side quantity and lives with the smoother
(\dcref{eq:kf-Q-iono}{the process-noise section}); everything below is
measurement noise only.

\subsection{The stochastic-model dispatch}\label{sec:stoch-dispatch}

Every undifferenced observation $(\rx,\sat,\sigl)$ is assigned a scalar
variance by a strategy object selected from the
\texttt{StochasticModelType} enum (\texttt{include/config/GnssConfigTypes.hpp},
the four-way \{\texttt{Constant}, \texttt{Elevation}, \texttt{SNR},
\texttt{SatelliteBlock}\} set).  The dispatch lives in
\texttt{src/core/model/StochasticModel.cpp}; each variant returns a variance in
$\mathrm{m}^2$ that becomes one diagonal entry of $\Rmat$.  The three
geometry-aware strategies share a common low-elevation guard: when
$\sinel \le 10^{-6}$ the elevation amplification is suppressed and the
un-amplified variance is returned, so a satellite at the horizon does not
inflate to a numerically infinite weight (\texttt{computeElevation}).  The
\texttt{Constant} strategy has no elevation term at all --- it returns its
a-priori variance directly from the dispatch (\texttt{computeConstant}) and
never reaches the guard.

\paragraph{(a) Constant.}  The trivial strategy returns the a-priori variance
of unit weight unchanged,
\begin{equation}
\sigma^2_{\rx,\sat,\sigl} = \sigma^2_{0,\mathrm{type}(\sigl)},
\label{eq:qc-constant}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_0$ drawn from \texttt{filter.measurement\_noise}
(\texttt{computeConstant}).  This is the reference against which the other
strategies are multiplicative.

\paragraph{(b) Elevation.}  The classical elevation-dependent model scales the
zenith-referenced variance by an inverse power of $\sinel$,
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\sigma^2_{\rx,\sat,\sigl}
  = \zenvar \,\bigl(\sinel^{\sat}_{\rx}\bigr)^{-2p_{\mathrm{type}}},
\qquad
p_{\code} = p_{\phaseo} = 1.0 .
\label{eq:qc-elevation}
\end{empheq}
The shipped defaults of \texttt{filter.code\_elevation\_power} and
\texttt{filter.phase\_elevation\_power} are both unity, so the delivered model is
\emph{symmetric}: code and phase alike follow the legacy $\csc^2\elev$ variance
law (\texttt{computeElevation}).  The exponent in code is $-2p_{\mathrm{type}}$
because the strategy works in variance, not $\sigma$:
$\bigl(\sinel\bigr)^{-2p}$ is exactly $\bigl(\sinel^{-p}\bigr)^2$.  Rows whose
observable code does not begin with \texttt{L} --- everything but carrier phase,
including Doppler and the empty-\texttt{obs\_type} fallback paths --- take the
code power.

Both exponents are configurable per observable class, and the asymmetric
setting $p_{\phaseo}=0.5$ is the one alternative with a physical argument behind
it, so it is worth recording why it is an \emph{option} rather than the default.
It gives a $\csc\elev$ variance ($\csc^{1/2}\elev$ in $\sigma$), a markedly
gentler amplification at low elevation.  The argument for it is that phase noise
near the horizon is dominated by carrier-loop SNR and by antenna phase-centre
miscalibration, neither of which follows the $\csc^2\elev$ mapping that governs
code multipath and pseudorange path-length error; and that the milder
amplification keeps low-elevation phase inside the useful range of the
standardised-residual cycle-slip test instead of deweighting it into
irrelevance.  The argument against it is comparability --- the code branch, and
every archived benchmark, run at unity --- and that is the position the loader
now ships (\texttt{GnssConfig::getPhaseElevationPower}, whose documentation
carries the amplification ratios at a $10^{\circ}$ mask).

\paragraph{(c) SNR (SIGMA-$\varepsilon$).}  When carrier-to-noise density is
available the strategy uses the Hadás--Bosy variance-additive form
\cite{subirana2013gnss}.  Writing the measured (or synthesised) density as
$\cnzero$ in $\mathrm{dB\,Hz}$,
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\sigma^2_{\mathrm{SNR}}
  = a^2 + b^2 \, 10^{-\cnzero/10},
\label{eq:qc-snr}
\end{empheq}
where $a$ is the high-SNR floor ($\sigma\!\to\!a$ as $\cnzero\!\to\!\infty$)
and $b$ scales the SNR-dependent contribution; both have separate code/phase
values (\texttt{snr\_params\_.code\_a/b}, \texttt{phase\_a/b}).  The
\emph{variance}-additive composition in \Cref{eq:qc-snr} is mathematically the
correct combination of two independent noise sources.  The legacy
$\sigma$-additive alternative
$\sigma = a + b\,10^{-\cnzero/10}$ is explicitly rejected in
\texttt{computeSnr} because squaring it produces an
unphysical $2ab\,10^{-\cnzero/10}$ cross-term that has no noise-process
interpretation --- it would couple the two contributions as if they were
correlated when they are not.

When no \texttt{S\dots} observable is present the model does not switch
functional form; instead it \emph{synthesises} a density from elevation so the
noise curve stays continuous across the boundary
(\texttt{synthesizeCn0FromElevation}):
\begin{equation}
\cnzero(\elev) = 50 - 15\,\bigl(1 - \max(0,\sinel)\bigr) \;\; [\mathrm{dB\,Hz}],
\label{eq:qc-snr-synth}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ a $50\,\mathrm{dB\,Hz}$ zenith maximum dropping $15\,\mathrm{dB}$ to the
horizon.  The density is then clamped to the configured working range
$[\texttt{snr\_floor\_dbhz},\,\texttt{snr\_ceil\_dbhz}]$.  Two refinements
follow: on code rows a per-band scale $s_{\mathrm{band}}^2$ from the
SatelliteBlock code table multiplies $\sigma^2_{\mathrm{SNR}}$ (wider-bandwidth
signals such as L5/E5a get $\sim\!\sqrt{10}$ less correlator-noise $\sigma$,
hence a factor $\sim\!1/10$ in variance), while phase stays band-flat because
PLL noise is governed by carrier-loop bandwidth, not chip rate.  Finally the
Wieser hybrid step pipes the SNR variance through \Cref{eq:qc-elevation} so
that elevation-correlated multipath --- which pure SNR weighting
under-weights, since the carrier loop tracks cleanly even while the range is
corrupted --- is still captured.

\paragraph{(d) SatelliteBlock.}  The most detailed strategy keys the zenith
$\sigma$ on the transmitting satellite's ANTEX block type, then applies
observable-specific frequency-band and tracking-attribute scales
(\texttt{computeSatBlock}):
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\sigma^2_{\rx,\sat,\sigl}
  = \bigl(\sigma_{\mathrm{block}(\sat),\,\mathrm{type}}\,
          s_{\mathrm{band}}\, s_{\mathrm{attr}}\bigr)^2
    \bigl(\sinel\bigr)^{-2p_{\mathrm{type}}} .
\label{eq:qc-satblock}
\end{empheq}
Here $\sigma_{\mathrm{block}(\sat),\mathrm{type}}$ is looked up by
substring-matching the satellite's ATX type string against
\texttt{block\_code\_std\_m} / \texttt{block\_phase\_std\_m}
(falling back to \texttt{default\_code\_std\_m} / \texttt{default\_phase\_std\_m}
when no block matches); $s_{\mathrm{band}}$ is the per-band factor keyed on the
RINEX band character (\texttt{obs\_type[1]}); and $s_{\mathrm{attr}}$ is the
tracking-attribute factor keyed on \texttt{obs\_type[2]} via a
\emph{per-system} table, because RINEX-3 attributes carry constellation-specific
meaning (a \texttt{C} attribute is the noisy GPS C/A but the high-precision
Galileo E1C pilot).  The attribute lookup order is
\texttt{[system][attr]}~$\to$~\texttt{[default][attr]}~$\to$~$1.0$.  Code and
phase carry separate band/attribute tables
(\texttt{SatBlockNoiseParams::ObservableScale}) since chip-rate calibration
governs code while carrier-tracking calibration --- a much smaller band
gradient --- governs phase.  As with the other geometry-aware strategies the
block variance is finally amplified by \Cref{eq:qc-elevation}; phase only
reaches this path when \texttt{apply\_to\_phase} is set, otherwise it takes the
flat \texttt{default\_phase\_std\_m} route in the top-level dispatch.

All four strategies populate diagonal entries only; the undifferenced $\Rmat$
is therefore block-diagonal, exactly the skeleton of
\dcref{eq:noise-model}{the observation noise model}.  Structured off-diagonal
correlation appears only after differencing (\Cref{sec:stoch-fullR}).

\subsection{A-posteriori variance factor}\label{sec:stoch-vf}

The entire Kalman-side QC layer described in this and the following three
sections is a \emph{default-off} diagnostic.  \texttt{computeResidualQc\_} is
called from \texttt{KalmanFilter::update} only when
\texttt{estimation.enable\_variance\_factor} or
\texttt{estimation.enable\_data\_snooping} is set, and the correlation-aware
kernel of \Cref{sec:stoch-fullR} additionally requires
\texttt{estimation.enable\_full\_r\_kernel}; all three default to false.  With
them off the filter's only outlier gate is the fixed $|\sres|>6$ path of
\dcref{sec:kf-gating}{the gating section}, the variance factor keeps its
initialised value of unity, and none of the redundancy numbers, $w$-tests or
minimal detectable biases below are computed at all.

Once an epoch has been updated, the post-fit residuals $\residvec$ provide an
internal-consistency check on whether the assumed $\Rmat$ was correctly scaled.
The a-posteriori \emph{variance factor} (estimated variance of unit weight) is
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\varfac = \frac{\wssr}{\dof},
\qquad
\wssr = \residvec^{\Tr}\Rmat^{-1}\residvec
      = \sum_i \frac{\hat v_i^{\,2}}{R_{ii}}\;\bigg|_{\,\Rmat\;\mathrm{diag}},
\qquad
\dof = \operatorname{tr}\redmat,
\label{eq:qc-varfac}
\end{empheq}
where $\wssr$ is the weighted sum of squared residuals and the degrees of
freedom $\dof$ is the trace of the redundancy matrix $\redmat$
(\Cref{eq:qc-redundancy} below), i.e.\ the \emph{effective} redundancy of a
recursively-filtered epoch rather than the naive $m-n$ of a single batch
(\texttt{computeResidualQc\_}, \texttt{src/core/filter/KalmanFilter.cpp}).
Because $\Rmat$ is carried in absolute units, the a-priori
variance of unit weight is exactly $\sigma^2_0 = 1$; a well-scaled model
therefore yields $\varfac \approx 1$, with $\varfac \gg 1$ signalling
under-weighted (optimistic) noise and $\varfac \ll 1$ pessimistic noise.

The variance factor is a \emph{reporting-only} rescale: it never feeds back
into the propagated covariance $\covM$.  When \texttt{enableVarianceFactor()}
is set, the filter \emph{logs} a copy of the position $\sigma$ scaled by
$\sqrt{\varfac}$, i.e.\ $\sigma_{\mathrm{pos}} \to \sigma_{\mathrm{pos}}\,
\sqrt{\varfac}$, leaving the filter state untouched
(\texttt{validatePostFitResiduals}, \texttt{src/core/filter/KalmanFilter.cpp}).

The same quantity has a batch incarnation in the normal-equation accumulator
of the PPP NEQ-export path (constructed only under \texttt{enableNeqExport()}
in \texttt{Application::run}).  Given normal matrix $\bm{N}$,
right-hand side $\bm{u}$, and the pre-reduced $\bm{l}^{\Tr}\bm{P}\bm{l}$ term,
the accumulator forms (\texttt{NeqAccumulator::solve})
\begin{align}
\wssr &= \bm{l}^{\Tr}\bm{P}\bm{l} - \bm{u}^{\Tr}\hat{\stateV},
&
\dof &= n_{\mathrm{obs}} - (n_{\mathrm{params}} + n_{\mathrm{local}}),
\label{eq:qc-neq-wssr}\\[2pt]
\varfac &= \frac{\wssr}{\dof},
&
\sigma_i &= \sqrt{\varfac\,\bigl(\bm{N}^{-1}\bigr)_{ii}} .
\label{eq:qc-neq-sigma}
\end{align}
Here $\hat{\stateV} = \bm{N}^{-1}\bm{u}$ is the least-squares solution, the
Schur-reduced local parameters $n_{\mathrm{local}}$ are subtracted from the
redundancy alongside the retained parameters $n_{\mathrm{params}}$, and each
retained parameter's formal $\sigma$ is the $\varfac$-scaled square root of the
inverse normal matrix diagonal --- the batch analogue of
\Cref{eq:qc-varfac}'s recursive $\dof$.

Neither of these is the network producer's \emph{operational} variance factor.
The undifferenced network estimator does not form $\wssr/\dof$ at all; it
estimates a robust unit-weight $\sigma$ as a scaled median-absolute-deviation
over the weighted phase-leveling residual rows
$\tilde v_i = |\hat v_i|\,\sqrt{w_i}$,
\begin{equation}
\hat\sigma_{0,\mathrm{net}}
  = \max\!\bigl(\texttt{raim\_sigma0\_floor},\;
                1.4826\,\operatorname{median}_i \tilde v_i\bigr),
\label{eq:qc-sigma0-net}
\end{equation}
with the $1.4826$ consistency factor recovering a Gaussian $\sigma$ from the
MAD and \texttt{raim\_sigma0\_floor} (default $1.0$) flooring it.  Unlike the
Kalman variance factor of \Cref{eq:qc-varfac}, $\hat\sigma_{0,\mathrm{net}}$ is
emphatically \emph{not} reporting-only: it drives a within-epoch RAIM
adaptation loop whenever it exceeds \texttt{network.raim\_sigma0\_trigger}, a
whole-epoch and per-constellation product withhold above
\texttt{network.raim\_withhold\_sigma0}, and the scale on the emitted URA.
Those three responsibilities live in three separate seams:
\texttt{networkSolveGlue} (\texttt{filter/NetworkSolveGlue}) owns the
within-epoch loop and both withhold gates, the per-constellation one through
\texttt{NetworkEstimator::decidePerConstWithhold}; \texttt{networkPostFitRaim}
(\texttt{filter/NetworkRaim}) owns the post-fit pass and the
exponentially-weighted fold of $\hat\sigma_{0,\mathrm{net}}$ into the emitted
URA scale; and \texttt{networkSolPrep} (\texttt{filter/NetworkSolPrep}) owns the
emission-side prune that actually removes a withheld constellation's satellites.
\Cref{sec:stoch-net-raim,sec:stoch-net-pools} develop the adaptation loop and
the pooled statistics; the emitted-product side is not treated here.

A third, non-operational variance-factor instrument exists on the network path.
\texttt{NetworkCarryVce} estimates Floor and Rate variance components of the
carry-row stochastic model by residual-form least-squares variance-component
estimation at fixed weight, purely to check the hand-set carry floors against a
measured $\hat\sigma$.  It is default-off (\texttt{network.carry\_vce}), never
feeds back into the solve, and the feedback path \texttt{network.carry\_vce\_apply}
is a hard configuration-load error --- the class has no apply method at all.

\subsection{Redundancy numbers, the Baarda $w$-test, and the MDB}
\label{sec:stoch-baarda}

The detection theory rests on the post-fit residual covariance $\srmat$
(the covariance of $\residvec$),
\begin{equation}
\srmat \;=\; \Rmat - \jacH\,\covM^{+}\,\jacH^{\Tr},
\label{eq:qc-srcov}
\end{equation}
where $\covM^{+}$ is the \emph{updated} (post-fit) state covariance, so that
$\srmat$ is the covariance of $\residvec$ propagated through the gain.  Its
diagonal $\srmat_{ii}$ is built per row in \texttt{KalmanFilter::update} as
$R_{ii} - (\jacH\covM^{+}\jacH^{\Tr})_{ii}$; the full matrix is formed only
under the off-diagonal kernel of \Cref{sec:stoch-fullR}.

\paragraph{Redundancy number.}  The (local) redundancy of observation $i$ is
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\redno_i \;=\; \frac{\srmat_{ii}}{R_{ii}}
            \;=\; \bigl(\redmat\bigr)_{ii},
\qquad
\redmat = \srmat\,\Rmat^{-1},
\qquad
\redno_i \in [0,1],
\label{eq:qc-redundancy}
\end{empheq}
clamped to $[0,1]$ for numerical safety in both branches of
\texttt{computeResidualQc\_}.  $\redno_i \to 1$ means the observation is fully
checked by the geometry (a blunder lands almost entirely in the residual);
$\redno_i \to 0$ means it is \emph{uncheckable} (a blunder is absorbed into the
estimate and leaves no residual signature).  The sum $\dof = \sum_i \redno_i =
\operatorname{tr}\redmat$ is the effective number of degrees of freedom used in
\Cref{eq:qc-varfac}.

The network producer reimplements \Cref{eq:qc-redundancy} in the batch/arrowhead
algebra of the Schur-conditioned shared covariance,
$\redno_i = 1 - w_i\,\bm{a}_i^{\Tr}\operatorname{Cov}(\stateV)\,\bm{a}_i$
with $\bm{a}_i = [\,\bm{a}_{s,i}\mid\bm{a}_{r,i}\,]$ and the same $[0,1]$ clamp
--- algebraically the batch twin of $\redno_i = \srmat_{ii}/R_{ii}$
(\texttt{NetworkNormalEquations::rowRedundancy}, gated by
\texttt{network.raim\_redundancy\_normalize}, default off).  Rows falling below
the \texttt{kRedFloor} redundancy floor are dropped from the
$\hat\sigma_{0,\mathrm{net}}$ pool of \Cref{eq:qc-sigma0-net} as uncheckable ---
the network analogue of the KF snooping gate's \texttt{kUncheckableRedundancy}
cutoff below, at a much smaller constant because it guards only the
$\sqrt{\redno_i}$ standardisation, not a reset decision.

A structurally \emph{exact} instance of $\redno_i = 0$ governs the network
producer's entire phase-side QC design, and it is worth stating as a theorem
rather than as a numerical caveat.  Whenever each (station, satellite, signal)
arc carries its own free ambiguity --- the configuration in force whenever
\texttt{network.phase\_leveling} is off --- every phase row is fitted
\emph{exactly} by its own parameter.  The post-fit phase residual then vanishes
identically, $\redno_i$ is exactly zero, and no phase fault is detectable from
the residuals at any magnitude: an undifferenced network with per-arc float
ambiguities carries \emph{no} phase-side redundancy whatsoever.  Only when the
ambiguities are stiffened by a carried prior do the phase rows acquire
redundancy, and therefore fault-detection power --- which is why the producer's
phase RAIM (\Cref{sec:stoch-net-raim}) is gated on phase-leveling in the first
place, rather than merely working less well without it.  The measure-only
instrument that reports the collapse is \texttt{networkPostfitSigma0}
(\texttt{filter/NetworkFrameDiag}), whose pooled phase variance factor is
identically zero in that configuration; the zero is the finding, not a defect.
Its pools deliberately exclude two row classes on the same reasoning: rows
touching a shared spherical-harmonic column, which are pseudo-observations
rather than data (\Cref{sec:stoch-iono-deweight}), and the carry-coupling rows,
which are a statement about the carry rather than about the epoch's
measurements.

\paragraph{$w$-test.}  Baarda's data-snooping statistic standardises each
residual by its own post-fit standard deviation.  The diagonal (uncorrelated)
form is
\begin{equation}
\wtest_i \;=\; \frac{\hat v_i}{\sqrt{\srmat_{ii}}}
\label{eq:qc-wtest-diag}
\end{equation}
(the diagonal branch of \texttt{computeResidualQc\_}), which --- crucially ---
divides by $\sqrt{\srmat_{ii}}$, the
\emph{true} residual standard deviation, not by $\sqrt{R_{ii}}$.  This is the
Studentised residual proper; under the null hypothesis $\wtest_i \sim
\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, whereas the $\sres$ used by the fixed gate floors the
denominator at $R$ and is therefore conservative.  When the residual
covariance is non-diagonal the correlated Teunissen generalisation is used
instead (the \texttt{use\_full} branch of the same routine):
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\wtest_i \;=\;
  \frac{\bigl(\srmat^{-1}\residvec\bigr)_i}
       {\sqrt{\bigl(\srmat^{-1}\bigr)_{ii}}} .
\label{eq:qc-wtest-corr}
\end{empheq}
\Cref{eq:qc-wtest-corr} reduces to \Cref{eq:qc-wtest-diag} when $\srmat$ is
diagonal, but accounts for the off-diagonal correlation among residuals
introduced by differencing
\cite{teunissen2017handbook,teunissen1985generalised}.

\paragraph{Minimal detectable bias.}  The smallest blunder on observation $i$
that the $w$-test will detect, at the configured significance and a
conventional $80\%$ test power, is
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
\mdb_i \;=\; \frac{\sigma_i\,\noncentral}{\sqrt{\redno_i}},
\qquad
\noncentral = \texttt{dataSnoopingCritical()} + 0.8416 ,
\label{eq:qc-mdb}
\end{empheq}
with $\sigma_i = \sqrt{R_{ii}}$ the a-priori observation $\sigma$ (both branches
of \texttt{computeResidualQc\_}).  The non-centrality $\noncentral$ is the
critical value plus $\Phi^{-1}(0.80)=0.8416$ (the $20\%$ false-negative
quantile, i.e.\ $\beta=0.20$ power); for the default critical $k=3.29$ this
gives $\noncentral \approx 4.13$.  The $1/\sqrt{\redno_i}$ factor is the
geometric leverage: a poorly-checked observation ($\redno_i \to 0$) has an
arbitrarily large MDB --- the code returns $+\infty$ when $\redno_i$ falls below
\texttt{kRedFloor} --- so a blunder there is by construction undetectable from
the residual alone.  The MDB is a Kalman-filter QC quantity only: the network
producer implements no minimal detectable bias, relying instead on the robust
$\hat\sigma_{0,\mathrm{net}}$ RAIM loop of \Cref{eq:qc-sigma0-net}.

\subsection{Data-snooping gate override}\label{sec:stoch-snooping}

The QC statistics are not merely reported; when \texttt{enableDataSnooping()}
is set they drive an \emph{alternative, parallel} outlier gate that can
reselect the row the fixed $|\sres|>6$ path missed.  Both gates share the same
downstream dispatch inside \texttt{KalmanFilter::validatePostFitResiduals};
only the \emph{statistic, index, and critical value} differ.  By default the
gate is seeded from the fixed-$\sigma$ triple
$(\,\max_i|\sres_i|,\,\arg\max,\,6\,)$ so the dispatch is byte-identical when
snooping is off.

When snooping is enabled the gate is instead reseeded from the $w$-test over
\emph{checkable} rows only: rows whose redundancy falls below the
\texttt{kUncheckableRedundancy} cutoff are skipped, because their residual is
unreliable and resetting the corresponding ambiguity would discard good
information.  (The same cutoff screens the summary log, so what is reported and
what is gated agree.)  Over the surviving rows the gate fires on
\begin{equation}
\max_{\{i\,:\,\redno_i \ge \texttt{kUncheckableRedundancy}\}} |\wtest_i|
  \;>\; \texttt{dataSnoopingCritical()},
\label{eq:qc-snoop-gate}
\end{equation}
and the worst checkable $|\wtest_i|$ row becomes the handled index.  Because
$\wtest_i$ uses the true residual $\sigma$
(\Cref{eq:qc-wtest-diag}) rather than the $R$-floored $\sres$, this gate can
catch a blunder on a high-leverage row whose floored $\sres$ stayed below $6$,
and it overrides the $\sres$ pick: the downstream
Melbourne--Wübbena tiebreaker still operates in the $\sigma$ domain, so when
the $w$-test selected a row the $\sigma$ cut missed, no $\sigma$-candidate is
collected and the $w$-selected index is handled directly.  This is the
complement of the fixed standardised-residual path in
\dcref{sec:kf-gating}{the gating section}, which this document does not
re-derive.

\subsection{Correlation-aware full-$\Rmat$ kernel}\label{sec:stoch-fullR}

The diagonal forms above assume $\Rmat$ and $\srmat$ are diagonal, which holds
for undifferenced PPP.  For BSSD and baseline-DD epochs the differencing
operator $\bm{D}$ of \dcref{sec:diff}{the differencing transformations} maps
the diagonal undifferenced covariance into a \emph{dense} one,
$\Rmat_{\mathrm{SD/DD}} = \bm{D}\,\Rmat_{\mathrm{ZD}}\,\bm{D}^{\Tr}$, carrying
the structured SD/DD cross-covariance between observations that share a pivot
or reference.  Under the off-diagonal kernel
(\texttt{estimation.enable\_full\_r\_kernel}, default off, which additionally
requires that a dense $\Rmat$ was actually supplied for the epoch --- the
\texttt{dense\_r\_supplied} test in \texttt{KalmanFilter::update}) the full
residual covariance \Cref{eq:qc-srcov} is built densely and symmetrised, and the
QC quantities follow without approximation:
\begin{align}
\redmat &= \srmat\,\Rmat^{-1},
  & \redno_i &= \bigl(\redmat\bigr)_{ii},
  & \dof &= \operatorname{tr}\redmat,
\label{eq:qc-full-red}\\[2pt]
\wssr &= \residvec^{\Tr}\Rmat^{-1}\residvec,
  & \wtest_i &= \frac{\bigl(\srmat^{-1}\residvec\bigr)_i}{\sqrt{(\srmat^{-1})_{ii}}},
  & \mdb_i &= \frac{\sqrt{R_{ii}}\,\noncentral}{\sqrt{\redno_i}} .
\label{eq:qc-full-w}
\end{align}
$\Rmat^{-1}$ and $\srmat^{-1}$ are obtained by $\mathrm{LDL}^{\Tr}$ solves
against the identity, exploiting the symmetric positive-definiteness of both.
The kernel is consistent by construction: the diagonal of the full $\redmat$ and
of the full Teunissen $\wtest$ equal the per-row quantities of
\Cref{sec:stoch-baarda} whenever the off-diagonals vanish, so the
\emph{outlier-handling path is unchanged behind the flag} --- it only gains
correctness on differenced epochs where the residual correlations are real.
Note the narrow scope of the flag: the gain and Joseph-form update already
honour a dense $\Rmat$ whenever one is supplied, independently of it; the flag
decides only whether the \emph{post-fit residual} covariance is formed densely.
This is the measurement-side counterpart to the structured correlation
\dcref{sec:diff}{the differencing section} introduces on the model side.

\subsection{Producer-side RAIM: within-epoch adaptation}\label{sec:stoch-net-raim}

The network producer's response to a tripped $\hat\sigma_{0,\mathrm{net}}$ is
not a single exclusion but a choice of \emph{granularity}, made from a robust
two-way decomposition of the epoch's standardised residuals.  Taking the largest
standardised residual $m_{\rx\sat}$ of each (station~$\rx$, satellite~$\sat$)
carried-ambiguity cell as a sparse two-way table and applying a Tukey median
polish,
\begin{equation}
m_{\rx\sat} \;\approx\; \mu \;+\; \alpha_{\rx} \;+\; \beta_{\sat}
             \;+\; \gamma_{\rx\sat},
\label{eq:qc-medpolish}
\end{equation}
splits the contamination into a grand effect $\mu$, a per-station row effect
$\alpha_{\rx}$, a per-satellite column effect $\beta_{\sat}$, and an
isolated-arc interaction $\gamma_{\rx\sat}$ (\texttt{medianPolish2Way},
\texttt{filter/NetworkRobustStats}).  Sweeping medians rather than means is what
makes the split usable: a minority of very large cells --- the actual outliers
--- does not distort the additive effects, so a bad satellite lighting up a whole
column is separable from a bad station lighting up a whole row, and genuinely
isolated arc slips are left in the interaction term.

Each effect is a candidate culprit, and each must clear
$\text{effect}/\hat\sigma_{0,\mathrm{net}} > k_{\mathrm{RAIM}}$ against the
configured gate (\texttt{network.raim\_k\_sigma}) to be eligible at all.  The three are
then ranked surgical-first: on equal score an
arc beats a satellite beats a station, and the station branch is additionally
de-rated by a bias factor (\texttt{network.raim\_station\_drop\_bias}, active
under \texttt{network.raim\_surgical\_station}) so the coarsest response --- which
discards good arcs along with bad --- is taken only when it dominates by that
margin.  A consensus rule short-circuits arc-by-arc peeling: when at least
\texttt{network.raim\_min\_stations} of one satellite's arcs are over the gate in
a single iteration, the arc candidate is redirected to the whole satellite,
because a corrupted \emph{shared} clock or phase bias is a network-wide fault
rather than a stack of independent local ones.

The response itself distinguishes a bad \emph{measurement} from a stale
\emph{parameter}, and that distinction is the load-bearing design decision.  A
whole-station culprit is excluded --- its data are bad, and deweighting or
exclusion is the right tool for bad data.  A satellite or arc culprit, by
contrast, is overwhelmingly a cycle slip, and a slipped ambiguity does not need
less weight, it needs a new \emph{value}.  The epoch is therefore replayed over a
freshly rebuilt normal-equation system with the culprit's ambiguities
\emph{refloated}: their measurements retained at full weight, so the local block
stays positive-definite and no drop-induced singularity is introduced, but their
carried prior withheld, so the re-solve is free to find the new value and absorb
the slip locally.  The loop iterates until $\hat\sigma_{0,\mathrm{net}}$ falls
below the trigger or a small fixed pass cap is reached; only the rare,
already-contaminated epochs pay for a re-solve, and any failure reverts to the
last good solution (\texttt{networkSolveGlue}).

Arming honesty: \texttt{network.raim} and \texttt{network.raim\_within\_epoch}
both default to false, and \emph{both} tiers are additionally gated on the
phase-leveling carry being warm --- the within-epoch loop inside
\texttt{networkSolveGlue}, the post-fit pass at the \texttt{networkPostFitRaim}
call site.  With \texttt{network.phase\_leveling} off there are no carried
ambiguities to refloat and, by the redundancy-collapse argument of
\Cref{sec:stoch-baarda}, no phase-side redundancy to test at all, so the
producer's RAIM is inert by construction rather than by policy.

\subsection{Pooled variance factors and the per-constellation carve-out}
\label{sec:stoch-net-pools}

\Cref{eq:qc-sigma0-net} pools only the carried-ambiguity \emph{phase} rows.  Two
extensions widen or partition that support without altering the estimator; both
are default-off.

\paragraph{Code and iono-constraint pools.}  In the historical arrangement the
code rows and the per-line-of-sight\,$\leftrightarrow$\,spherical-harmonic
iono-constraint rows are screened by no tier whatsoever.  Under
\texttt{network.raim\_we\_code\_iono} the same scaled-MAD estimator is applied to
each of them over its own disjoint row support,
\begin{equation}
\hat\sigma_{0,\mathrm{code}}
  = 1.4826\,\operatorname{median}_{i\in\mathcal{C}} \tilde v_i,
\qquad
\hat\sigma_{0,\mathrm{iono}}
  = 1.4826\,\operatorname{median}_{i\in\mathcal{I}} \tilde v_i,
\label{eq:qc-sigma0-pools}
\end{equation}
each with its own trigger, and each row assigned to exactly one of the three
supports.  Neither pool carries the floor of \Cref{eq:qc-sigma0-net}, and
neither displaces the phase pool as the driver of the whole-epoch ceiling.  The
adaptation gains two response classes matched to what these rows can actually
diagnose: an epoch-scoped station exclusion, and a network-wide
\emph{deweighting} of one satellite's code rows --- a fallback, not a drop, in
keeping with the project's standing preference for degrading a measurement over
discarding it.

\paragraph{Per-constellation carve-out.}  When the global ceiling trips, the
default response withholds the whole epoch's product.  Under
\texttt{network.per\_const\_withhold} the same pool is instead partitioned by the
ambiguity's satellite constellation and each part judged on its own,
\begin{equation}
\hat\sigma_{0,c}
  = \max\!\bigl(\texttt{raim\_sigma0\_floor},\;
        1.4826\,\operatorname{median}\{\tilde v_i : i \in \mathcal{P}_c\}\bigr),
\label{eq:qc-sigma0-perconst}
\end{equation}
over exactly the rows $\mathcal{P}_c$ that the global statistic pools --- same
statistic, same ceiling, restricted support.  A constellation is published if and
only if $\hat\sigma_{0,c}$ clears that ceiling \emph{and} $|\mathcal{P}_c|$ meets
a minimum row count, since too few rows cannot certify cleanliness inside a
contaminated epoch (\texttt{NetworkEstimator::decidePerConstWithhold}).

The approximation is stated rather than hidden.  The residuals entering
\Cref{eq:qc-sigma0-perconst} are evaluated at the \emph{joint} solution, so a
contamination in one constellation perturbs the shared per-station nuisances
(receiver clock, tropospheric delay, slant ionosphere) and leaks into another
constellation's residuals; and each row's redundancy is its share of the joint
system's redundancy, not of a hypothetical per-constellation solve.
$\hat\sigma_{0,c}$ therefore under-separates cross-constellation coupling, and
the outcome ``every constellation individually clean while the global statistic
tripped'' is the honest edge of that approximation --- it is published with a
warning and counted, not silently absorbed.  The rank and observability gate is
deliberately left with whole-epoch semantics: a rank deficiency invalidates the
declared datum itself, so the product would be a meaningless least-norm split and
no healthy subset exists to carve out.

\subsection{Station-relative deweighting of the ionosphere constraint}
\label{sec:stoch-iono-deweight}

A single-layer spherical-harmonic VTEC surface structurally cannot represent
per-line-of-sight slant structure: the large majority of LOS residuals against
the fitted field exceed $3\sigma$ at any degree and at every network scale.  That
exceedance is a chronic, roughly common-mode background, so an \emph{absolute}
$3\sigma$ gate would engage every station and diagnose nothing.  The genuinely
hostile sites --- equatorial-anomaly and scintillation-belt stations whose true
slant ionosphere lies beyond the field's representability --- stand out only as a
\emph{relative} excess.  That is the reason the gate below is network-relative
and robust rather than absolute (\texttt{NetworkIonoDeweightGate}).

Each station's per-epoch misfit statistic $m_{\rx}$ is first smoothed by an
exponentially-weighted moving average with forgetting factor $\phi = 2^{-1/W}$,
i.e.\ a half-life of $W$ epochs, then standardised against the network's own
robust centre and scale.  Writing $\mathcal{M}$ for the set of mature,
non-statically-listed stations,
\begin{equation}
z_{\rx} \;=\; \frac{\bar m_{\rx} - \mathrm{med}}{1.4826\,\mathrm{MAD}},
\qquad
\mathrm{med} = \operatorname{median}_{\rx'\in\mathcal{M}} \bar m_{\rx'},
\qquad
\mathrm{MAD} = \operatorname{median}_{\rx'\in\mathcal{M}}
               \bigl|\bar m_{\rx'} - \mathrm{med}\bigr| .
\label{eq:qc-idw-z}
\end{equation}
The median centring removes the common-mode background; the MAD scale tolerates
a poison minority, its $50\%$ breakdown point being precisely the property an
absolute threshold lacks.  A minimum mature population is required before the
robust centre is formed at all, and statically-listed stations are excluded from
it so that a known-bad site cannot mask the detection of others.

Detection is hysteretic --- a station engages at
$z_{\rx} > \sigma_{\mathrm{hi}}$ and releases at
$z_{\rx} < \sigma_{\mathrm{lo}}$, with $\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}}$ forced below
$\sigma_{\mathrm{hi}}$ --- and while engaged the target weight follows a smooth
IGG-style ramp, unity at the release threshold and equal to a configured floor
$w_{\min}$ at and above the engage threshold, quadratic between:
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}
w^{\ast}_{\rx} = w_{\min} + (1-w_{\min})\,(1-t)^2,
\qquad
t = \frac{\operatorname{clamp}\bigl(z_{\rx},\,\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}},\,
          \sigma_{\mathrm{hi}}\bigr) - \sigma_{\mathrm{lo}}}
         {\sigma_{\mathrm{hi}} - \sigma_{\mathrm{lo}}} .
\label{eq:qc-idw-ramp}
\end{empheq}
The realised weight slews toward $w^{\ast}_{\rx}$ by at most a fixed increment
per clean epoch and is clamped to $[w_{\min},1]$, which keeps the gate from
oscillating against its own effect on the field fit.

The action is purely on $\Rmat$.  The factor $w_{\rx}$ multiplies the
\emph{information} of that station's spherical-harmonic\,$\leftrightarrow$\,slant-iono
pseudo-observation rows, equivalently $\sigma^2 \mapsto \sigma^2/w_{\rx}$.
Because those rows are one joint pseudo-observation coupling the station's
per-LOS slant ionosphere to the shared field in both directions, a single
$w_{\rx}$ relaxes both directions uniformly; $w_{\rx} \to 0$ decouples that
station's ionosphere entirely
--- it then floats on its own geometry-free combination, still observable, so no
rank hole opens --- while the station continues to serve the shared clocks,
biases, datum graph and position.  It is the surgical alternative to dropping a
hostile station, and it is a weighting change only: no state is removed and no
datum is altered.

Two honesty notes.  Datum-change epochs, pivot handover included, are excluded
outright --- the gate is neither fed nor advanced on them, because their
residual spikes are datum artefacts rather than station misfit --- so every
weight is simply held across them.  And the adaptive path is default-off
(\texttt{network.iono\_constraint\_deweight\_adaptive}); with it off and the
static station list empty, every $w_{\rx}$ is unity and the producer is byte-identical
to the ungated build.  The gate is advanced once per epoch from the serial
post-solve harvest in \texttt{networkShHealth}
(\texttt{filter/NetworkShHealth}) and read (const, race-free) during the parallel
per-station assembly.

\subsection{State-domain emission gates}\label{sec:stoch-emission-gates}

Everything above tests \emph{residuals} against $\Rmat$.  The producer also
carries two gates that test the \emph{solved state} and act on the emitted
product rather than on the estimation.  A reader who meets them as ``QC'' should
know both that they are a different kind of test and where they live --- in
neither case the seam whose name most suggests it.

\paragraph{Spherical-harmonic field health.}  Under
\texttt{network.sh\_health\_gate} (default off) the solved VTEC coefficients are
read back out of the shared solution, the dual-layer degree-zero terms are summed
to a total mean VTEC, and that value is tested against a physically admissible
band together with a ceiling on the RMS of all coefficients.  An out-of-band
epoch must repeat for a configured number of consecutive epochs before the gate
engages, which debounces the single-epoch excursion.  The verdict is
\emph{emission-only} by construction: the solve, the carry stash and every QC
tier continue to run while emission is withheld, so an era lasting hours cannot
expire the carry the way a skip-epoch response would.  A failed coefficient
lookup leaves the gate state unchanged rather than fabricating a release.  The
gate has no release-or-degrade policy --- a sick era withholds for its full
duration --- and that is an open policy item, not a design conclusion.  The
verdict is formed in \texttt{networkSolveGlue}, the only pre-emission seam
holding the solved shared vector; \texttt{networkShHealth}
(\texttt{filter/NetworkShHealth}) owns the health \emph{probe}, the deweight fold
of \Cref{sec:stoch-iono-deweight} and the carry variance-component update, not
this verdict.

\paragraph{Clock-runaway quarantine.}  Under
\texttt{network.clock\_runaway\_check} (default off) the epoch's satellite clock
solutions are grouped by constellation, and a constellation is dropped from the
emission when the median of $|\text{clock} - \operatorname{median}_{\mathrm{all}}|$
exceeds a magnitude ceiling, or the median $|\text{drift}|$ exceeds a rate
ceiling.  Like the carve-out of \Cref{sec:stoch-net-pools}, this degrades scope
rather than the whole product.  The code documents an inversion hazard instead of
defending against it: the magnitude test is referenced to the global median over
\emph{all} clock satellites, so if the runaway constellation supplies half or more
of them the comparison inverts --- the healthy minority trips the ceiling and is
quarantined while the runaway passes.  The single-constellation degenerate case
is warned at configuration load.  The quarantine lives in
\texttt{networkSolPrep} (\texttt{filter/NetworkSolPrep});
\texttt{networkClockQc} (\texttt{filter/NetworkClockQc}) is a different tier
entirely --- the two-state oscillator model with its normalised-innovation fault
test, the decoupled emit-clock-rate estimator, and the per-satellite
carried-clock sanity monitor.
