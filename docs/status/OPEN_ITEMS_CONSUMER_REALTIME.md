- **Real-time gap tolerance (fixed in ff1c464, branch fix/realtime; gated 2026-10-17).** With `processing.data_gap_tolerance_s` 0, stepTimeAndDetectGap used 1 s, so every 30 s epoch was a gap and re-seeded the clock from code. It now uses 1.5 times the median measured epoch spacing, never below the former 1 s (which is also the value until a spacing is measured). Without the floor the threshold would be 0.15 s at 10 Hz and 0.75 s at 2 Hz, so one dropped epoch would overwrite the Kalman clock with a single-epoch code clock, a tightening the old rule never made. A stream with a nominal interval of 0.667 s or less (1.5 Hz and faster) therefore keeps the old rule exactly. Above that the threshold is 1.5 intervals, so at 1 Hz it rises from 1 s to 1.5 s and a late epoch (1 s < dt <= 1.5 s) no longer re-seeds the clock. Pinned by unit tests (`tests/test_epoch_cadence.cpp`, `tests/test_realtime_defects.cpp`). - **Consumer latency.** The loop is same-epoch with no latency injection; an artificial-latency A/B of ZOH staleness and ssr_max_age is owed before service claims. [SsrCorrectionStore.hpp](../../include/products/SsrCorrectionStore.hpp)
- **Held SSR weighting.** 1 Hz consumers reuse one correction realization inside a hold interval; count it once per update, not per epoch (the current weighting is optimistic). [SsrCorrectionStore.hpp](../../include/products/SsrCorrectionStore.hpp)
- **SSR coverage freshness (fixed on branch fix/ssr-live).** `SsrCorrectionStore::coverageFor` counts code and phase biases on presence alone, so a record that is never refreshed (or a misdecoded id) still counts as coverage; an age gate like the orbit and clock counts would have shown the SSR-PB-HDR decode defect as low coverage instead of a slow ramp. Fixed: a code- or phase-bias record counts only when it holds at least one bias and is fresh within the limit of the orbit and clock counts; before it, the BKG and GFZ streams, whose phase-bias blocks are empty, read 18 of 18 and 16 of 18 satellites covered (2026-11-03). [SSR-PB-HDR](../record/cards/realtime/SSR-PB-HEADER.md), [SSR-BIAS-COV](../record/cards/realtime/SSR-BIAS-COVERAGE.md)
- **SSR clock without its orbit (fixed on branch fix/ssr-live).** On a stream that sends orbit and clock corrections in separate messages (CAS), a clock that reached the store before its orbit (after an outage, at start-up) was applied on the uncorrected broadcast orbit and the satellite counted as corrected: 6 epochs after the 2026-11-03 outage (17 clocks, 8 orbits) and 5 at a session start. A decoded clock now waits for a fresh orbit of its IOD-SSR; network clocks are unchanged. [SSR-CLK-ORB](../record/cards/realtime/SSR-CLOCK-ORBIT.md)
- **A short correction gap costs a satellite its phase for a minute.** When one satellite's correction lapses for a few epochs, the engine holds its phase signals as a tracking gap; with no across-gap check the hold times out after 61 s and resets the ambiguities. G16 on CNES (2026-11-03): a 5 s gap cost 61 s of phase, three ambiguities and 56 epochs on code only; E13 on CAS (2026-11-10): 5 epochs with its clock not applied, then the same 61 s time-out and reset of four ambiguities. The receiver tracked throughout; why the corrections lapsed is not verified (an ephemeris issue change is likely). [SSR-TIME §7](../record/cards/realtime/SSR-TIMING.md)
- **CAS dates BeiDou corrections in BeiDou time.** CAS BeiDou corrections arrive 14.8 to 15.7 s after their stated epoch against 0.8 to 1.7 s for its GPS and Galileo (two nights): the 14 s of BeiDou time. CNES shows no such offset. The decoder reads every IGS-SSR epoch as GPS time, so it would date CAS BeiDou corrections 14 s early (reference time and content age). No session has used BeiDou. [SSR-TIME §7](../record/cards/realtime/SSR-TIMING.md)

[SSR-TIME §7](../record/cards/realtime/SSR-TIMING.md)
