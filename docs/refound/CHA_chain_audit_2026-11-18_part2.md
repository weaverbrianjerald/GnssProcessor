## 4. Defects and inconsistencies, in priority order

The first three are the background's; each is restated with what the code shows now.

**P1. A re-host kept on a held integer enters the tree as a known step without the entry check, and is certified for the settled flags.** The keep-carry path decides "kept, step 0" from four facts only: the link is held, its monitor is not tripped, neither band is latched, and the new host's carry sits within 1e-4 cycle of the held integer (`chainHeldInteger` CD:1481-1488, `keepOnHeldInteger` CD:1531-1543). A kept edge is not a release, so the ring fix builds no cut for it (it reads only `obs_rec->releases`, RFS:599, 608-689), and `ringFixEntryVerdict` (gap measured, gap shifted, near-null signature) is never asked; step 3 of the hold epoch then marks the link `to_tree` and inserts it in `certified_edges` (RFS:782-796). This is the path of card NRF section 103: a held link one near-null step off enters the tree at its held integers, the satellite's level follows it, and the satellite's other held links change their frame truth (103, corrected in 103.1; the audit's frame, 103.3). It is also where a held pair carried across an unchecked gap would be kept if the re-host is not a return. One map read a related claim, that a refused entry is still certified; it does not hold: a refused entry's cut is unknown and `ringFixApplyCuts` releases every held link crossing it, the entry included (RF:751-777), before step 3. Test: a unit case of keep-carry onto a link with a shifted gap resumption, and one with a step signature, each expected refused once the entry check owns the decision (step S8). Repair: S8.

**P2. Whether a held entry's step is known has three deciders.** The chain's returning row sets the step unknown unconditionally (CD:1469) and the walk then re-references the moved side's satellites and erases its carries (CD:2072, 2508-2598); the ring fix takes a usable held entry as a known step by default and re-expresses the held links through the cut (RFS:643-686, RF:736-798); keep-carry decides known with no verdict (P1); and the walk's held-first block chose the entry beforehand (AG:608-648). The two transforms act on different things (the chain's carries, the ring fix's held links re-born at their re-expressed integers through the hold rows), which is why the books balance in the runs of card NRF section 107.6, but no counter ties the two verdicts, and section 107.6's failure was exactly their disagreement. `network.ring_fix.entry_revalidate` (adopted scoped) makes one function own the verdict for released entries only. Repair: S8.

**P3. A re-host pins the satellite's level through one link.** The rehost row keeps the draft's target, λ·round of the new edge's own carry (`draftPinTarget` CD:1115-1132, the rounding at CD:1125) or the seed round's (CD:1057-1100), with an unknown step (CD:1600-1614): the satellite's level is re-expressed through the one link that becomes its edge, so a young link's float or one receiver's code-set wide lane becomes the level every converged link is then refused against (NRF 100.1, 103.1), and a setting satellite re-hosted onto ending arcs re-pins at each (NRF 106.5). Nothing compares the new level with the satellite's other links. The refactor gives the target one owner (S3, S8); a target from the satellite's converged links is a rule change behind a key (card IRD, IRD-4 and IRD-5), not part of the plan.

**P4. The record the ring fix and the stream read is gated by a trace key.** The election builds its record only with `network.trace_chain_datum` on (CD:2869-2873), under a comment that says "nothing below reads it" (CD:2866-2868); the loader requires the trace for the ring fix (LDN:11106-11111) and logs "measure-only, no event row is armed" (LDN:3409-3413) even with every row armed; the stream's step declaration of released keys needs `chain_obs->armed` (`EmitStore.cpp:472-473`). With `network.disc_step_class_pairs` and the event rows armed but the trace off, released keys are silently not declared. The ledger lists the key as TRACE. Repair: S1.

**P5. The ambiguity key names a station's arc and a satellite band's state in the same code** (part 1, section 2.2: 13 sites, 22 counted by the maps). This blocks a member of a signal family from keeping its own arc name (card NRF section 112.3 (d)) and makes the row functions' parameter `amb` mean two things. Repair: S6 (with S4 and S5 first).

**P6. The phase-family intake has two latent defects and a field-blocking deferral.** A file that declares both the member and the identity keeps the member's own codes (RinexParser.cpp:384-388), so that receiver's member arcs keep their own name and their own satellite columns, and the stream's member records then overwrite the estimated member values with the identity's (`EmitStore.cpp:66-68`, unconditional). A `network.ring_fix.pairs` entry that names a member signal matches no chord, with no loader check (LDN:1539-1548). The streamed ingest is refused (`ApplicationNetworkSetup.cpp:250-252`; triaged field-blocking for a real-time network in NRF 112.3 (c)). The header `PhaseFamily.hpp` says a member "keeps its own codes and code biases, its own ambiguities", which the parse rename contradicts under `network.phase_family_codes` and for the ambiguity names. Test: a station whose header declares L1C and L1X. Repair: loader refusals now; S6 removes the rename.

**P7. The ring fix's state is declared as one opaque run field.** About twenty cross-epoch maps of `NetworkRingFix` (RFh:731-844: links, `last_tree_s`, `certified_edges`, `settled`, `settled_incumbent`, `settled_run`, `prev_targets`, `cascade_links`, the `untied_*` maps, `nn_mon`) sit behind one row, `carry.ring_fix`, ColdStart (`RunFields.cpp:44`); none has a `FieldDecl` row, so `nom_n4_verify.py` cannot see them, and no ring-fix key is a token of the snapshot fingerprint (`SnapshotFingerprint.cpp`), so a warm resume would start with nothing held while carries sit at held integers (inferred). The observatory's `ChainFrameMemory` lives in a counters base (`RunContext.hpp:2474`), which the rule allows for a measure-only reader, and is copied whole and never pruned (CF:512-558). Repair: S7.

## 5. The refactor plan

### 5.1 The controls of every step

- **Observed, 2 h, the adopted configuration:** `_rap_d24c32_n146` and `_rap_d24c32_n147` (`D:/GnssProcessorSupport/runs_nom`), reproduced byte for byte at `2fdfc29` (section 7) and rerun at `72b7b3b`.

## 6. What the plan enables, and what it does not

**Members of a signal family keeping their own arc names.** After S4 to S6 an arc keeps its member's signal (`E05_L1X` at a combined-mode receiver) while the satellite band's state, the band and the receiver's band cell take the family's identity through one map. What remains for a native single Galileo family beyond the plan: the receiver phase bias of a member receiver keyed by the band's identity; the ring fix's `pairConsidered` by the family's identity; the stream's member phase records (already emitted, then needed only where a record is missing); the chord table's families are already band-based (CCT:47-56). The parse rename and its defects (P6) go with it, and so does the refusal of the streamed ingest (`ApplicationNetworkSetup.cpp:250-252`), since nothing is renamed at the parse. Whether the codes are shared or apart stays a choice of the configuration, now expressible without renaming codes.

**Further constellations and channels.** BeiDou (the third golden's B1I and B3I: `chainPairFamilies` takes digit '1' as band 1, CCT:47-56, and `makeChainBand` takes the wavelength from the digit alone, CD:410-416, where BeiDou digit 1 is B1C); GPS L1C with L1W on one band (the table's first use beyond Galileo, NRF 109.1 amendment 1); QZSS. The typed band and family identities remove the letter and digit reads of part 1, section 2.1, that would misread them. Not enabled: GLONASS (FDMA groups are not rounded, CD:408-409, and integer fixing across frequency channels waits on the inter-frequency biases); a third band's integers on observed data (the cascades stay in the log mode, and the near-null closure needs the third frequency, NRF 108.2); the guard's integer pair for another family (P14, a small follow-up of S3).

**A per-receiver-make code term** (NRF 107.8: the Trimble combined-mode receivers' steady per-satellite term, 0.026 cycle rms). The plan neither builds nor blocks it: it is a station parameter (the recipe `docs/onboarding/08_adding_a_network_parameter.md`), outside the chain. What S6 adds is the arc's own signal inside the estimator, so such a term can select its rows by an arc's tracking rather than by the stations trace's text column.

**The single Galileo family: before or after its adoption in the parse-time form.** The recommendation is to decide the adoption of the parse-time form (`network.phase_families` with `network.phase_family_codes`) on card NRF section 118's evidence now, and to rebuild it natively after S4 to S6, as a second implementation of the same keys graded against the adopted form on the same bars (the truth audit on the two-mode beds, the slip bed, the two-run audit of DOY 146, the users of both signal types). The parse-time form is confined to the intake (`ApplicationNetworkSetup.cpp:240-282`, `RinexParser.cpp:379-401`) and the stream's member records, whose contract (identity and member records with equal values, flags and counters) a native build must also meet, so users see no difference; its latent defects can be closed at the loader before an adoption (refuse a station whose file declares both names; refuse a `ring_fix.pairs` entry naming a member). A native build will not be byte-identical with it: the arc keys change and with them the order of maps and summations, so it is graded, not diffed. Its trigger is a real-time network, which the parse-time form refuses.
