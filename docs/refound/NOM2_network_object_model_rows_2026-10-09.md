## 1. The contract

Card NOM section 17 is the contract and is not repeated here. Two additions fixed before the build:

- The tags are plain data: a row tag is a `ContributionKind` with the row's satellite and signal (the N0a types), held in a vector aligned with the station block's rows; a shared fold carries its kind. No row or fold object owns a matrix.
- The user-side measurement header (`include/measurement/MeasurementModel.hpp`) stops including network headers: the absorbed-instance record of step N1b moves to its own small header.

## 4. 2026-10-09: N3 specified (pre-registered before the build)

N3 makes one object answer "which signal stands for which band at this station, and in what role". Card SID built that object, the per-station signal roster (`include/app/SignalRoster.hpp`, `network.signal_roster`, default off): for every (station, constellation, band) the tracked, modelled (copied from the canonical latch), companion, excluded (with a reason) and datum signals, with counters of every disagreement with the sites that decide the same question. Its own audit found 128 such sites under at least nine rules. Card NOM section 18 lists the band-role and signal-choice sites the N0b migration met (groups A to G). N3 does not choose new rules: it moves the readers onto the roster where the rules already agree, and lists the rest as decisions.

**N3a, the roster always built and printed (trace class).** The roster and its census are built on every run (the knob becomes the trace switch only, or goes), and a "signals as planned" table is logged after the first emitted epoch and at run end: per constellation and band, the modelled phase and code signals and how many stations model each, the companions, the datum pair, the excluded signals by reason, and one line per decision site with its disagreement count against the roster. Gate: byte-identical products, consumer trace and epoch status on the four arms (the roster is an observatory); cost within 2 % on an idle machine.

**N3b, the readers on the roster, one site at a time.** For each site of section 18's groups A to G: a mismatch counter between the site's own answer and the roster's runs first on the four arms and the family smokes; a site whose counter is 0 everywhere reads the roster, byte-identical; a site whose counter is not 0 keeps its rule and goes on the decision list with its counts. The arc ledger becomes the owner of the arc identity the slip tracker, the gap re-entry response and the wind-up restart use (card GRK's local rule is the reference).

**Decisions N3 will put to the author** (known before the build, from section 18 and card SID): the definition of a channel family (the attribute letter, or "X against the rest"; two definitions in the code disagree for Galileo C1C/C5Q); the pin pair (the BIA clock-reference pair, or the lexically first pair on distinct frequencies; they agree on bands and disagree on signals); the signal that stands for a band where several rules choose (smallest folded variance, majority census, lexical order); GPS as the inter-system-bias and time reference; BeiDou digit 1.

Gates: as card NOM section 6 with section 10's bar; the decision list with its counts is a deliverable of N3b, and no decision is taken by the build.

## 6. 2026-10-09: step TD, the run-end reports (pre-registered before the build)

**The defect.** The author's question (2026-10-09): is `NetworkTeardown` an example of bad organization? It is. `networkTeardown` (`src/core/filter/NetworkTeardown.cpp:76-7264`, main `3c097ca`) is one function of 7 189 lines with 44 positional parameters and 511 log call sites; it is the run-end report of every mechanism the producer has. INC-12 cut it out of the driver character-exact, so the move kept its shape, and since then every card has appended its lines at the bottom (the CLAUDE.md routing rule says "new run-end telemetry goes to `filter/NetworkTeardown`"). A read-only inventory (scripts and tables in the session scratchpad, `teardown/`) measured:

- **The body.** A 74-declaration alias preamble (2 aliases unused, 56 used in one section only), then 117 sections in the order cards added them. The sections report on 59 owners (the files whose state they read), and 15 owners print at more than one place (the carry driver and the slip QC at 5 places each, RAIM and the resume path at 3). 18 run-end functions already live beside their subsystems (card NOM's tables, the inject, roster, clock-clone and multi-GNSS run-ends, ...) and take 2 to 5 parameters, because their state is in the run context.
- **The parameters.** 5 are the run (context, configuration, logger, traces, setup). 15 can go: 5 duplicate what the teardown already holds (`synced` is used only for its size; `cl_truth` is `setup.cl_truth_arp`; `log_dir_` is `traces.log_dir_`; `idw_on` is `iono_deweight.active()`; `sh_dev_on` follows from the setup and the configuration), `kSubNames` is a constant table, `cl_truth_llh` is derived, and 8 flags are configuration values combined with `carry_invariant`, which `NetworkKnobs` can hold. The other 24 are the three groups the `NetworkRunContext.hpp` banner keeps out of the context: the run's engines, the section timers and the closed-loop consumer's tallies.
- **Hidden writes.** Seven sections sum per-station counters into `ctx.counters` while printing (one with a `+=` that is not idempotent); the exit code has three sources (no epoch emitted, the injection verdict, the ambiguity-block bar set in the middle of the file).
- **Readers of the text.** 35 consumers parse the run-end lines: 31 scripts, 3 launchers and one campaign script; 12 read "Network producer complete". Some depend on order: `pc_gate_verdict.py` takes "shared satellite product accumulated" as its done marker, `refound_grade.py` keeps the last 4 hits per pattern, `wup_grade.py` the first 2, `grade_slip_agreement.py` the last 12 slip lines, and the manifest's `complete` record must stay last. Others count `[error]` lines or split on the `[time] [logger] [level]` prefix.
- **The same disease elsewhere.** 32 network functions exceed 500 lines (49 % of the 83 918 network lines). Besides the teardown: `networkPassB_` 2 997, `runNetwork` 2 948 (1 701 of them in the per-epoch lambda; the CLAUDE.md figure of 2 359 is out of date), `networkSolveGlue` 2 686, `networkHarvestLocalCarries` 2 577. The driver makes 13 calls with more than 15 arguments (`networkBuildCarryDdPriors` 50, `networkTeardown` 44, `networkInstallCarry` 38, `networkEmitStore` 36).

**The design.** Each subsystem owns its run-end report, written beside its code; the teardown is a declared list of reports a reader scans like a table of contents.

1. **The run-end inputs (TD1a).** The 44 parameters become the run's five references and one const bundle, `NetworkRunEndInputs`, of named groups: the run's engines (estimator, carry driver, carry VCE, prior residual, slip QC, ionosphere deweight gate), the emit engines (emit rebase, phase-bias filter, clock rebase), the lane run (the lane counters and lanes), the run timing (loop start, section timers, assembly wall, profiled epochs) and the closed-loop tally. The eight derived flags move to `NetworkKnobs` (its fingerprint names its fields, so adding fields moves no fingerprint); the duplicates are read from where they live. The groups are driver-owned types in `filter/NetworkRunTypes.hpp`, not context members, as the context banner requires, and the same groups shorten `networkBuildCarryDdPriors`, `networkInstallCarry` and `networkEmitStore`.
2. **One report per concern, in its owner's file (TD1b).** The 117 sections are cut into about 40 reports, `int networkXRunEnd(const NetworkRunEndInputs&)`, each in the file that owns the state it reports (RAIM's three report sites in `NetworkRaim.cpp`, the gross line-of-sight screen's in `NetworkGrossLos.cpp`, the timing report in `NetworkRunTraces.cpp`, and so on); where no file owns the state (the leveling-carry census, the receiver code-bias carry, the resume path, the closed-loop harness), the step names a home. Every report gates itself and prints its INERT clause when armed with zero counts (invariant 4); it returns its exit-code contribution (0 for all but the ambiguity-block bar and the injection verdict). Reports are const: the seven counter sums become locals, and the one value another writer reads (`fr_if_refgate`) is computed explicitly before it. The alias preamble goes; each report declares what it reads. The teardown becomes a declared table, `kNetworkRunEndReports` (name, owner file, function), walked in order, then the exit code. The existing 18 run-end functions take the same signature and join the table. In this step the printed text and its order do not change.
3. **One report per subsystem (TD2).** The owners that print at several places get one report each, so the run-end log reads by subsystem, with one heading line per subsystem. This moves lines: each move is listed, and the order-dependent readers named above are updated in the same change.
4. **The routing rule.** CLAUDE.md's "new run-end telemetry goes to `filter/NetworkTeardown`" becomes "a new run-end line goes in its subsystem's report, beside its code; a new report is a row of the run-end table". The tour gains the table.

**Gates.**
