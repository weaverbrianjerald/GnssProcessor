### 124. 2026-11-18: why a short run differs from the day run from its first epoch, and why GPS fails in the short key-off run of DOY 146 (a read)

**Why.** Stage R's key-off control `ptRc_n146` (DOY 146, 5 h) departs from today's `d24_n146` at the first epoch and its GPS is poor (section 121.3); the managing session found the same departure in the 2 h run `d24c32_n146` (exe nrf32), so the engine is not the cause, and section 102.1 had noted it without tracing. Asked: (1) what makes the first epoch depend on the run's length, confirmed by a run pair, and what a streamed network would do; (2) why GPS is poor in the short key-off run and normal in the short key-on run.

**Method** (exe `gnss_processor_cha_fp1.exe`; scripts in `_irdu_grades/scripts_s20/`, outputs in `_irdu_grades/reads_2026-11-18/`). The run-start output of `d24_n146` and `ptRc_n146` compared as sets of lines with the counts and the durations normalised; the first epoch's records compared; the code read (`src/core/network/driver/ApplicationNetworkSetup.cpp`, `src/core/network/solve/Sbasis.cpp` on `chain/audit` at 91f36cf). Runs: `rl24_n146` (`d24_n146`'s configuration, 24 h) and `rl5_n146` (`ptRc_n146`'s, 5 h), each stopped by this session after 45 epochs (`stop_after_epochs.ps1`, log `_irdu_grades/stop_after_epochs.log`; the engine has no epoch cap that keeps the full parse), and `rl2e_n146` (`ptRc_n146`'s configuration for 2 h with `processing.exact_epoch_time` true; exit 0, 240 epochs). GPS reads: `gps_refusal_by_rx.py` (outputs `gps_refusal_by_rx.txt`, `gps_refusal_rl2e.txt`) and inline reads of `network_ring_fix.csv`, `network_chain_edges.csv` and `products.txt`.

**1. The first epoch depends on the configured length through the group-delay latch.**
- *The rule* (shown, code). With `network.sat_gf_datum` broadcast_tgd, the setup latches each satellite's broadcast group delay once per run for the geometry-free pin's right-hand side: the low median of the satellite's healthy navigation records (Galileo F/NAV preferred) whose clock epoch lies in the processing window [t0, t1) (`NetworkSbasis::latchBroadcastGroupDelays`, `Sbasis.cpp:121`; called at `ApplicationNetworkSetup.cpp:710`). The window ends at t0 + `max_duration_s`, or one second after the last synced epoch without a duration (`ApplicationNetworkSetup.cpp:357-361`). The same window feeds the broadcast health series (line 394), the broadcast clock a priori (line 607) and the product files named in the S contract (line 1707); in these configurations only the latch's line differs between the runs.
- *The difference* (shown). Of the run-start lines, as sets with counts normalised, `d24_n146` and `ptRc_n146` differ only in the latch's window and its result (and in the counts of synced epochs and removed observations): window [TOW 172800, 259200) against [172800, 190800); over 24 h, 22 Galileo satellites latch a value other than the file's first record (+0.0698 to +0.4886 m: E03, E06, E15, E30 +0.4886; E02, E04, E12, E13, E16 +0.4188; ...), over 5 h none does.
- *Its effect at the first epoch* (shown). The first epoch's Galileo code-bias records of the 5 h run minus the 24 h run equal minus the latch difference on the E1 codes (C1C, C1X) and minus 1.793 times it (f1²/f5²) on the E5a codes (C5Q, C5X): E04 −0.4188 and −0.7510 m, E03 −0.4886 and −0.8762 m, E31 −0.0698 and −0.1252 m; satellites whose latch did not change differ by at most 4 mm (E29 C1X 0.0039 m). The GPS records of the first epoch differ by at most 6.4 mm (no GPS latch changed).
- *The run pair* (shown). `rl24_n146` stopped at 45 epochs equals `d24_n146` over the first 20 epochs, data line for data line (10 357 lines); `rl5_n146` stopped at 45 epochs equals `ptRc_n146` over the same 20 epochs; the two differ from each other at the fourth data line (C E04 −0.2619 against −0.2555). What sets the first epoch is the configured length, not the number of epochs processed; and the key's exe reproduces exe nrf32's day run.
- *A streamed network* (code, no run). With `network.streaming.enable` the epochs come from the live front end (`ApplicationNetwork.cpp:322-345`), but the setup still forms the window from the configured files' synced epochs and `max_duration_s` before the stream starts, so the latch would follow whatever span those give. A network streamed without end has no window end: the rule as written cannot be causal there. The latch already has a causal fallback, the healthy record in force at the window start (used for a satellite with no record in the window). A day run, by contrast, latches with records up to 24 h after its first epoch.

**2. GPS in the short key-off run of DOY 146.**
- *Where* (shown, hours 3 to 5). GPS rows refused for their fraction: `ptRc_n146` 24.4 %, `ptR_n146` 2.1 %, `d24_n146` 1.6 % (held 65.5, 87.3, 87.9 %). In `ptRc_n146` 41 of 55 receivers have more than 10 % of their GPS rows refused for the fraction (BOR100POL, the GPS datum receiver in all three runs, 75 %; NYA200NOR, FFMJ00DEU 62 %; CEBR00ESP, REYK00ISL 57 %), with median |frac_if| 0.26 to 0.40 cycle and median |frac_wl| 0.02 to 0.17: the ionosphere-free direction, not the wide lane. By satellite, 9 of 32 have more than 30 % of their rows refused (G25 71 %, G09 66 %, G03 66 %, G32 54 %, G28 52 %, G27 50 %, G12 49 %, G08 37 %, G29 31 %), each with a common ionosphere-free offset of its unheld links between +0.28 and +0.39 cycle (resultant length 0.68 to 0.90); links such as YKRO00CIV–G25 and KOKV00USA–G09 are refused at every epoch of the three hours. In the arm one satellite exceeds 30 % (G04, 40 %), in the day run none.
- *When* (shown). GPS fraction refusals by hour 1 to 5: control 32.8, 26.7, 27.3, 24.6, 21.0 %; arm 28.5, 3.4, 2.6, 2.0, 1.8 %; day run 30.0, 0.9, 1.3, 1.3, 2.1 %. All three start alike; the arm and the day run converge in hour 2, the control does not.
- *The same in other short runs* (shown). `d24c32_n146` (exe nrf32, 2 h, exact epoch time off) in hour 2: 26.7 % against `d24_n146`'s 0.9 %, BOR100POL 74 %, 39 of 55 receivers over 10 %. `d24c32_n147` (DOY 147, 2 h, exact epoch time on) in hour 2: 0.6 %, equal to `d24_n147`'s, 0 receivers over 10 %, although its latch window also differs (16 Galileo satellites change over the day). `rl2e_n146` (DOY 146, 2 h, the control's configuration with exact epoch time on): 1.8 % in hour 2, 3 of 55 receivers over 10 %.
- *The arm's GPS side* (shown). The control and the arm emit identical GPS records at the first epoch and keep BOR100POL as the GPS datum receiver throughout; their GPS trees, like the day run's, part from 0.78 h (each pair of the three runs identical at 94 of 600 epochs). The key changes only Galileo's trees; GPS converges with it and does not without it.
- *Reading* (inferred). The failing GPS levels need three conditions together on these runs: a short window (the latch at the files' first records), exact epoch time off, and the per-signal Galileo trees. Each run that lacks one of them converges on GPS (the day run, the 2 h run with exact epoch time on, the key-on run). The only setup difference between the short and the day run is the latch, so the latch is the condition the run's length contributes; how a Galileo code datum and the epoch-time handling reach several GPS satellites' narrow-lane levels through the per-signal trees is not traced.

**What follows for the work** (inferred from the above).
- A short run is not a cut of the day run: the latch gives it another Galileo code datum (up to 0.49 m on E1, 0.88 m on E5a for single satellites on DOY 146), so short runs compare with short runs of the same length and start, and day runs with day runs.
- Short runs of DOY 146's configuration with exact epoch time off are not representative on GPS; with exact epoch time on (the adopted configuration, and every run of tonight's five days) the 2 h runs of DOY 146 and 147 converge on GPS as the day runs do. The 3 h saved-state tests should use exact epoch time on.
- Nothing here changes how the five days run: they are 24 h runs with exact epoch time on. The day runs' latch uses navigation records from the whole day, which a real-time network cannot; a causal latch (the record in force at the window start, or a latch that steps a declared generation when the broadcast value changes) would make short, long and streamed runs share one code datum. Not built; a rule change behind a key if wanted.
