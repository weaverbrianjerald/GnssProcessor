# XRS: an exact warm resume, what it must carry, its cost and the options (paper for ruling 12)

**STATUS**

- **id:** XRS
- **topic:** network-vehicle
- **status:** open
- **summary:** Decision paper for rule change 12 (card CHA part 3) and item 16 of the night of 2026-11-18: the inventory of every cross-epoch member of a network station that a resumed run must carry, saved today or not, with its re-warm horizon, its reach to the integers and its size; the trap of processed-epoch indices that restart at 0 in a resumed run, with its remedy; options A (the exact discrete state), B (answer the restart on the held links and carries) and C (the whole solve with the per-station covariance), with their costs and evidence; the order of log lines under "identical"; and a recommendation. Nothing is built or adopted by this paper.
- **source:** card NRF sections 125 to 126 ([part 18](NRF_network_ring_fixing_2026-11-14_part18.md), [part 19](NRF_network_ring_fixing_2026-11-14_part19.md)); [card CHA part 3](CHA_chain_audit_2026-11-18_part3.md), rule change 12 and its wind-up note; the two reader reports and their scripts under `D:/GnssProcessorSupport/gates/day_program_2026-11-19/packages/chain-exact-resume-paper/` (`reader_state_inventory.md`, code read at `e7bc098`; `reader_grade_folder.md`, every count recomputed from `D:/GnssProcessorSupport/runs_nom/b2_s7br_grade/` and the legs); the day program's package `chain-exact-resume-paper` (`D:/GnssProcessorSupport/gates/day_program_2026-11-19/program.json`).

**Outcome.** A resumed run today restores the chain's memory and its held integers, but not the per-station state under them: the accumulated phase wind-up restarts at the principal value (28 arcs a whole cycle off on n147) and the slip detectors start cold (208 geometry-free flags against 42 in the first 20 epochs). The restored integers are then a turn off in the resumed run's frame, and over the leg the restored run holds more wrong integers (16.6 % and 23.5 % of held link-epochs on m1 and h40) than a null that restores none (9.9 % and 14.8 %). The null's own wrong integers sit mostly on the same arcs, through the restored carries, so releasing held links alone does not cure it. The missing discrete state is small (an estimated 21 kB per station in binary, about 6 MB for 300 stations at 1 s). Recommendation: option A in stages, the wind-up accumulator first, then the slip history with the epoch clocks continued; until A lands, both chain keys stay off.

## 1. The question

Card NRF section 125 asked whether the saved state of the chain and the ring fix (step S7 (b), keys `network.state_snapshot_chain_capture` and `network.resume_chain_restore`) lets a resumed run continue an uninterrupted one. Sections 125.5 to 125.8 graded it: the memory crosses the seam whole (B2a, missed only by the window line, section 125.8) and every held integer is held at the seam (B2b met on m1 and h40), but B2c is missed on all three beds, because the snapshot carries no accumulated phase wind-up and no slip-detector history. The manager's reading (section 125.6): the purpose of the step, replaying from a converged state in place of two hours of convergence, is not reached, and no bar on integers is read on a resumed leg in place of an uninterrupted run until the author rules on the exact resume (rule change 12). This paper prepares that ruling.

**Totals (estimates from the declarations and the n147 counts, not measured allocations).** About 33 kB per station live, about 96 kB with the never-pruned maps after a day and the full covariance block: 1.8 MB for the 55 stations of n147 (5.3 MB), and 10 MB (29 MB) for 300 stations at 1 s with the same signals. Of the 33 kB, the covariance block (about 10 kB) and the parameter set (about 2 kB) belong to option C; option A's members (the wind-up accumulator, the slip history and the seed marks) come to about 21 kB per station. No buffer holds a span of seconds (windows count samples, EWMAs count epochs), so the bytes do not grow at 1 s; horizons counted in samples shrink thirtyfold in time at 1 s. For comparison, text against text: the snapshot today is 17.5, 17.5 and 21.1 MB on m1, h40 and n147, 87 to 88 % of it the coupling regressions and 1.4 to 1.6 % the chain memory section (`reader_grade_folder.md`, section 4); in the snapshot's text form (about 2.5 times the binary) the whole missing state would add about 4.5 MB on n147, and option A's members about 2.9 MB.

**Network-level members on the solve path that are ColdStart today** (`reader_state_inventory.md`, section 3): the receiver phase-bias carry `rpb` and the phase-colour carry `pc` (both live on the beds), the gross line-of-sight state, the admission records and the arc ledger. The arc ledger is documented as unread by the solve, but the chain election's kept row reads its admitted generation and run start through `arcLifeStamp` (ChainDatum.cpp:1517), so it reaches the datum. An exact resume needs these as well.

**What a byte-exact continuation needs beyond values.** Each station's column order (StationParams.hpp:21-27), which sets the rounding of the solve; the per-station covariance block; the order of the network's shared columns and of every ordered container the solve walks (not inventoried); and members written through non-const references inside the model (an open point of the inventory).

## 4. The trap of epoch indices

Four processed-epoch clocks restart in a resumed run, and none is restored today (`reader_state_inventory.md`, Table 2): C1 `ctx.counters.n_loop_epochs` (first processed epoch 1), C2 the slip channel's `epoch_idx` (first epoch 1), C3 the roster's `epochs` handed to the arc ledger (first epoch 0), C4 each station's IF-detector call counter; and the monitors' sequence counters (C5). Members keyed or valued by them include `RawSlipHistory::Prev::epoch_idx` and `SigPrev::last_epoch` and `run_start` (C2), `GfArcJumpArc::last_epoch` (C2), the IF detector's last reference fire (C4), `LocalCarry::birth_ep` and `dropped_ep` (C1), the arc ledger's epoch stamps (C3) and the pinned-life stamps the chain section already saves (C1, C3).

Saved unchanged and restored into a restarted clock, an index misleads: the raw history's adjacency test (`prev >= j-1`, RawObservables.cpp:490) classes a true gap re-entry as adjacent for up to W epochs (W, the writer's clock, about 241 on the beds), so the re-entry is neither found nor refloated; a restored carry with a positive saved birth stamp reads young (`localCarryYoung`) until the resumed clock passes its own stamp plus the minimum age, where a maturity bar is armed; the arc ledger's `admittedGapEp` (ArcLedger.hpp:261-263), which has no guard, goes negative; the arc ledger recognizes an arc observed this epoch by a stamp equal to the clock, which a restored stamp can match by coincidence. The chain section's pinned lives are not compared with the resumed run's stamps today (the restored-life rule of section 125.2 judges them on the resumed run's own state), which avoids the mismatch at the cost of not seeing an event between the writer's last election and the snapshot.

Three remedies:
- **R1, continue the clocks** (recommended by the reader): the snapshot writes each clock's value at the snapshot epoch and the resume restores it, so every saved index keeps its meaning and the sentinels (-1 "before any", 0 "never stashed") stay valid. Writer: the N record for C1 and C5, the new station section for C2 to C4. Reader: `networkTryWarmResume` beside the N-record counters (WarmResume.cpp:1255-1267) for C1 and C5; a new seam with the run in scope after the setup restores (Run.cpp:335-354) for C2 to C4. With C1 and C3 continued and the arc ledger's records and the flag map restored as well, the chain's first election can use the ordinary life comparison and the restored-life special path can retire.
- **R2, rebase:** store index minus W and restore into the restarted clock. Exact, but a rebased last-epoch stamp of 0 or -1 collides with the sentinels at every reader.
- **R3, times:** store continuous GPS seconds and test adjacency against the previous processed epoch's time, which must then be restored as well; counts of missed epochs then need the processed-epoch grid.

Continuing C1 and C3 also changes what trace strides and run-end averages see (reporting only; the 69 reads of `n_loop_epochs` were not all audited).

## 5. The options

**Option A, the exact discrete state.** Save and restore the wind-up accumulator (per station and satellite: the unwrapped angle in radians, its last update and its arc start in seconds), the slip history (the geometry-free jump arcs, the raw history with its step scales and LLI credibility, the wide-lane arc statistics and group memories, the IF chains, counter and noise scale) and the epoch clocks (R1: C1 and C5 in the N record, C2 to C4 in the station section), in a station section of the snapshot with its own fingerprint in its header (as section 125.2 repaired the chain section, so the whole-resume fingerprint does not change), under a capture key and a restore key, both default off. A restored accumulator whose gap to the leg's first epoch exceeds its horizon restarts as today and enters the restart ledger, so the restart refloat sees it (an event at both edges). A keeps the wind-up convention the producer, the users and the simulator share (accumulated, frame `nominal-wu93`) across the seam: the resumed run continues the writer's accumulator instead of restarting it.
- Cost (estimated): about 21 kB per station in binary (about 52 kB in the snapshot's text form), so about 2.9 to 3.1 MB of text on the beds' 17.5 to 21.1 MB snapshots (+14 % on n147, +18 % on m1 and h40), and about 6 MB in binary at 300 stations and 1 s; code in the order of the chain section (writers, readers, round-trip unit cases with every member at a value of its own, the restore seam, loader interlocks, ledger rows); one engine change with its own identity controls.
- Expected effect, to be measured: the whole-turn class goes (all of B2c on m1 and n147 and 330 of h40's 714 pairs; on n147 the 50 pairs at (1, 1) are on PARK00AUS with E18, which rose after the resume with the same accumulator in both runs, so its turn reaches it through its cycle and its removal is an inference through the tree), the jump test's storm and the n147 edge of B2b go with its history, and the wide-lane burst goes with its statistics (`network.resume_slip_warmup_ep` is then not needed). What stays: the float differences of the solve (on these beds L's phase biases sit a median 0.5 to 0.8 mm from the seed's at the first epoch, section 125.5), because the covariance block and the column order are not carried; through them a decision taken near a gate's edge can still fall on another epoch. The 0.6 m departure of the phase biases at the second epoch on the sim beds stays with the warm-up (0.61 and 0.69 m, section 125.6) and has no cause attached; whether B2c reaches 0 under A depends on it.
- Staging (the critic's reshape of the build): S1, the accumulator alone (keyed by time, it needs no epoch clock, so the read after S1 measures one change); the jump-test and raw-history half of the slip history next, with the epoch clocks continued (R1); the wide-lane and IF state only if the reads after those still show their effect. Each stage reads B2c on m1 and h40 together with the holds and releases unmatched and the tree edges differing per epoch: B2c on m1 does not see the wide-lane burst (329 without the warm-up, 328 with it), which shows on m1 in the releases and the tree edges and on h40 as the 384 pairs of E25.

**Option B, answer the restart on the held links and the carries.** At a resume, release every held link and refloat the restored carries, as `network.cycle_slip.windup_restart_refloat` does for an accumulator's own restart.
- Cost: small (one resume rule and its counters).
- Effect on the integers, read from the matched null N, which restores no held link: the first hold comes at epoch 31 on every bed (the ring fix's 600 s window and 10 epochs of persistence), half the seed's held count at epochs 62, 62 and 57, and 0.816, 0.809 and 0.926 of it at the end of the hour; the truth audit grades 9.9 % (m1) and 14.8 % (h40) of N's held link-epochs wrong, against 0 % and 8.2 % in the seed. The integers re-converge over the better part of an hour, so the step's purpose is not reached. Releasing the held links alone does not keep the integers right either: N's wrong holds concentrate on the arcs whose restored carries are a turn off (section 2), so the carry refloat is the half of B that matters for the integers.
- N is not B in everything (`reader_grade_folder.md`, section 3): most of N's 21 271 differing pairs on m1 are a change of integer datum (18 781 are right in both runs' own frames), and N restores no part of the chain memory section (4 113 entity, 1 968 ring fix and 83 emitter lines on m1), so it declares a datum change at its first epoch and bumps 166 discontinuity counters on n147. No leg refloats the carries at the resume (the legs' wind-up restart events, where there are any, come later in the hour), so B's carry refloat has never run: it discards the floats' convergence along with the turn, and N's timeline is B's fastest case, not its measure. B with the chain section restored keeps the tree, the targets and the emitter's state, and loses the held integers and the carried levels. B leaves the shared wind-up convention unchanged and answers the restart by re-levelling.

**Option C, the whole solve.** Option A plus the per-station covariance block, each station's column order, the order of the network's shared columns and every ordered container the solve walks, and the ColdStart carries on the solve path (receiver phase bias, phase colour, gross line of sight, admission records, arc ledger).
- Cost in bytes: small. The block is about 10 kB packed per station (n about 50 carried columns); at the snapshot epoch n147 has a median of 77 local parameters per station and at most 86, and a covariance over all of the stations' local parameters, each stored as a lower triangle, would be about 154 000 numbers, about 3.5 MB of text at the snapshot's 22.66 bytes per number (arithmetic, not measured). No log line reports a snapshot's write time; from file times one copy takes 0.2 to 0.4 s and the whole snapshot step at most 0.8 to 1.7 s on the beds (`reader_grade_folder.md`, section 4).
- Cost in work: large and open-ended. Byte identity across a resume needs every piece of state the solve reads, including the order of containers and members written through references inside the model; one missed member and the leg differs again from its first epoch, and finding it means another inventory.
- Effect: when complete, a resumed leg equals the uninterrupted run record for record. Then B2c and the integer bars read as first asked, and a leg is usable for identical-output steps against an uninterrupted run (section 125.6 notes the weaker use available today: two legs from one seed are repeatable).

## 6. Item 16: the order of log lines under "identical"

B4's whole-log clause is missed only because the restored-signal audit lines ("Canonical signals audited for"; 120 on m1, 108 on n147 per leg) come in another order between two legs from one seed; as multisets they are equal, and the positions that differ (43, 51, 102 and 102) are exactly the gate's counts (`reader_grade_folder.md`, section 5). The lines are written by the audit pass of `PppModel::decideConstellationCanonicals` from the slip scan's per-station OpenMP loop (`passAScanStations`, PassASlipScan.cpp:106), so their order follows the thread schedule. A remedy makes the question moot: the model buffers the audit lines per station and the slip scan flushes them in station order after its loop; the other OpenMP regions of the network producer are scanned for log calls reachable from their bodies, and any unordered one is ordered the same way. Whether line order belongs to "identical" stays the author's ruling (card NRF section 125.4); with the remedy it no longer decides a bar.

## 7. Recommendation

1. **Option A, in stages,** on the refactored seams (package chain-resume-refactor first): the wind-up accumulator alone; then the jump-test and raw-history half of the slip history with the epoch clocks continued (R1); the wide-lane and IF state only if the reads still show their effect. Each stage reads B2c on m1 and h40 with the unmatched holds and releases and the differing tree edges. Bytes are no obstacle (an estimated +14 to +18 % of text on today's snapshots), and A targets the error the night measured: integers held and decided a turn off.
2. **Until A lands, both chain keys stay off** (they are default off and UNVALIDATED, so no adopted configuration is exposed). Option B is no safe interim: releasing the held links alone still lets integers be decided a turn off from the restored carries (the null's 39 % wrong on the affected links), and refloating the carries as well, never run, discards the floats' convergence with the turn. The wind-up mismatch reaches any warm resume that restores leveling carries under accumulated wind-up, not only the chain restore; whether a service resume in the meantime should refloat the carries is a question for the author.
3. **Option C as a later, separate decision,** taken after A is measured: cheap in bytes, expensive in completeness. Its value is a resumed leg equal to the uninterrupted run, which would make legs a byte-identity tool and every integer bar readable as registered. It gets its own card with byte identity of L against U as its single bar.
4. **The two small items with A's engine:** the latch's own window (the GW line restored as the latch's window, apart from the processing window, so B2a reads as registered) and the station-ordered audit lines (item 16).

## 8. Open points

- The seam comparison rests on one snapshot epoch at hour 2 of DOY 147, where 3.2 % of arcs were past half a cycle; later snapshots (11.7 % on average after hour 2) were not tested.
- Whether any reader of the wide-lane statistics reads an arc older than its 120 s gap rule; if none does, only live arcs need saving (13 kB instead of 48 kB per station).
- The second resumed epoch's 203 prior-residual rows against 35 and the first epoch's 93 MDB exceedances (n147) are not attributed to a member; the per-entry first fold of the covariance block is one candidate.
- The inventory covers the station column order but not the network's shared column order or the other ordered containers (relevant to option C only).
