## 108. 2026-11-17: the near-null guard (the managing session's night item 3; pre-registered)

**Why.** Sections 107.2, 107.3 and 107.7: on both networks and both days, the network holds wide lanes one or more near-null steps off on a few links, which the two-run audit sees only where the roots disagree and the users see as their near-null column. **The offline prototype** (`guard_proto.py`, outputs `guard_proto_*.txt`; design work before this registration). Per held link-hour the mean postfit code residuals of the link's two Galileo codes as geometry-free GF = r1 − r5 and ionosphere-free IF = (γ r1 − r5)/(γ − 1), each centred on the median of the satellite's held link-hours of the same mode, then on the receiver's own median. A near-null step k (the held pair k (3, 4) above the truth) shows GF near +0.448 k m and IF near 0; a link's own code bias shows in both. ## 109. 2026-11-17: design note for the single Galileo network inside the engine (the managing session's night item 6; no code)

**What the relabel test settled.** One Galileo family per band repairs what the separate families lost (section 107.2: the pilot receivers' links held 78 to 92 % in every hour, 5 lost satellite-samples a day against 437 and 390). Its failures were of the test, or general, not of the tie: the root-dependent near-null steps (107.2) are the defect the guard of section 108 addresses; HKSL's height (107.4) came from an unflagged slip the relabelled run's slip layer could not see, used as a re-attachment's step (107.6); the per-satellite term between the modes is small and goes with the receiver make (107.1, 107.8).

**What the build changes** (behind one key, default off, byte-identical off):
1. *One family per band by signal identity, not by renaming.* The roster maps the combined mode's E1 and E5a phases (L1X, L5X) onto the pilot family's ambiguity identity (L1C, L5Q) for the chain, the ring fix and the hold rows, so a receiver of either mode contributes links to one pair family. The code signals stay distinct (C1X, C5X against C1C, C5Q), each with its own satellite code bias, as today.
2. *The slip layer keeps each receiver's own tracking pair.* The raw observables, the Melbourne-Wübbena, geometry-free, gap re-entry and ionosphere-free time-difference tests of a combined-mode receiver use its phases with its own codes (L1X with C1X, L5X with C5X), and a re-entry frees the arcs the family holds. Section 107.6 is the failure this item removes: the relabel left 28 receivers' slip layer on E6, E5b and E5.
3. *The stream carries both signal types.* The satellite phase-bias records of L1X and L5X are emitted beside L1C and L5Q (the same value: one bias per satellite and band), and the code biases per signal as today, so a combined-mode user finds corrections. The relabel's held-out user, itself in the combined mode, could not be graded for want of them.
4. *A returning satellite's held-first entry is re-validated.* The ring fix takes an entry link's held integer as a known step only when that link passed the gap re-entry test on its own pair across the satellite's absence; otherwise the step is unknown, as the chain's returning row declares, and the crossing links are released (section 107.6, general to both configurations).
5. *No per-satellite term in the first build.* Section 107.8: a term per mode would average two makes that do not share one (Javad against Trimble r −0.02); the Trimble class's own term is 0.026 cycle rms (at most 0.07, E12), small against the decision's margin. A later step may add one constant per satellite for the Trimble class from the observation files' table, read at run start.

**Bars prepared for the build** (simulator first, then DOY 146 and 147 as one-change arms on today's configuration): off byte-identical; on the two-mode bed, every pair family's held integers right against truth and the relabel arms' gains reproduced (held share, lost satellites); on observed data, section 107's bars 1 to 6 with the near-null guard as decided, bar 5 (the two-run audit) judged with the guard on, and the held-out combined-mode user graded on the guarded stream (no wrong hold; accuracy not worse than on today's stream by more than 1 mm in any hour from 3).
