# OTL: ocean tide loading from the SIF registry, and the nodal corrections

**STATUS**

- **id:** OTL
- **topic:** network-vehicle
- **status:** open
- **summary:** BUILT 2026-10-16, default off, never armed: `model_corrections.otl_from_sif` serves ocean tide loading from the SIF registry alone and `model_corrections.otl_nodal_corrections` adds the nodal factors and a UTC Greenwich argument. The gate against IERS HARDISP (NGS M-PAGES v1.2) found the SIF horizontal columns read reversed by `processing.enable_station_info` and closes the radial gap from 4.9 to 2.5 mm at the worst of three stations. The 6 h field arm and the 16-site census are owed.

- **knobs / posture:** `model_corrections.otl_from_sif`, `model_corrections.otl_nodal_corrections` (ledger rows UNVALIDATED; both goldens keep `enable_ocean_tide_loading` false, so both are unaffected); the loader default of `enable_ocean_tide_loading` stays true
- **dates in the text:** 2026-10-16
- **hashes / run dirs:** branch `mpg/otl`; gate and probe output `D:/GnssProcessorSupport/gates/otl/` (`gate_result.txt`, `gate_result.json`, `residual_attribution.txt`, `station_handling.txt`); harness `scripts/otl_compare/`; tool `gnss_otl_diag` (`src/tools/otl_diag.cpp`)

**Outcome.** Ocean tide loading was off on both goldens, and the only SIF route to it
(`enable_station_info`) also replaced the a-priori position and the ocean pole tide source. A dedicated
key now takes the loading block from the SIF alone. The gate against HARDISP showed that the existing SIF
conversion adds 180 degrees to the horizontal phases, which reverses the horizontal vector (up to 32 mm
against the reference at Brest); the new key reads the SIF columns as the BLQ west and south rows, as
M-PAGES does and as 98 % of 568 stations confirm physically. With the nodal factors the model stays within
2.5 mm radial and 0.9 mm horizontal of HARDISP at three stations; the floor is the minor diurnal and
semidiurnal constituents. The field and census checks are owed; nothing is armed.

## 1. What was built

All of it is default off and leaves the original evaluation untouched when off.

- `model_corrections.otl_from_sif` (`GnssConfigTypes.hpp`, loader in `GnssConfigLoad.cpp`). With
  `enable_ocean_tide_loading` on, the loader reads the SIF registry (`files.station_info_path(s)`, else
  `<data>/static/sif/*.json`) even when `processing.enable_station_info` is off. `Geometry::loadData`
  (`ModelGeometry.cpp`) then takes the 11 constituents of the station's SIF record, ahead of BLQ and EOT20.
  `enable_station_info` keeps its other effects (a-priori position, ocean pole tide) and is not armed by this
  key.
- `model_corrections.otl_nodal_corrections` (`OceanTideModel`, `GnssCorrections::setOceanTideNodalCorrections`).
  Each constituent is scaled by the nodal factor f and its argument advanced by the correction u at the
  epoch's lunar node longitude; the Greenwich sidereal angle comes from the UTC Julian date instead of GPS
  time (GPS is 18 s ahead, 0.15 degrees of semidiurnal argument). UT1 - UTC (below 0.9 s) is neglected, as in
  HARDISP, whose day fraction is UTC.
- `otlCoefficientsFromSif(station, SifOtlHorizontal)` is the one conversion from a SIF record to model
  coefficients. `BlqRows` copies the east and north columns into the west and south rows; `LegacyShifted` is
  the reading `enable_station_info` has always used (section 3). `ModelGeometry.cpp` picks `BlqRows` under
  `otl_from_sif` and `LegacyShifted` otherwise, so every run that arms `enable_station_info` alone stays
  byte-identical.
- `OceanTideModel::nodalFactor(constituent, node longitude)`: f exp(i u) = 1 + sum r_k exp(-i n_k Omega) over
  the node side lines of the constituent in the HARDISP development (`ADMINT.F` tables; 24 rows regenerated by
  `scripts/otl_compare/nodal_audit.py`). It reproduces the closed forms usually quoted (M2: f = 1 - 0.0373 cos
  Omega, u = -2.14 degrees sin Omega) to 0.0005 in f and 0.1 degree in u for M2, N2, K1, O1, Q1, Mf, and
  the audit prints the S2, P1 and Ssa side lines that the closed forms drop (up to 0.012 in f for P1 and 0.03 for Ssa).
- `gnss_otl_diag` (`src/tools/otl_diag.cpp`): SIF lookup with the production prefix guard, coefficient table,
  and an ENU displacement series from the production `OceanTideModel`; it is the C++ half of the gate.
- Unit gate `tests/test_otl_sif_source.cpp` (suite `OtlSifSourceTest`, 14 cases): loader defaults and the
  registry gating, both mappings, the nodal series against the closed forms, the off path against an
  independent re-implementation of the original model (1e-9 m), the Mf amplitude ratio and the S2 leap-second
  shift, and a regression of the KOKB00USA day against the HARDISP reference values.

## 2. The gate against HARDISP

**Reference.** `scripts/otl_compare/hardisp_port.py` ports HARDISP as NGS M-PAGES v1.2 ships it (`HARDISP.F`,
`ADMINT.F`, `TDFRPH.F`, `SHELLS.F`, `SPLINE.F`, `EVAL.F`, `ETUTC.F`, `TOYMD.F`, `JULDAT.F`, `LEAP.F`) because
the machine has no Fortran compiler. The 342-line tables are parsed from `ADMINT.F`, not retyped. The port
reproduces the two test cases printed in the `HARDISP.F` header (Onsala and Reykjavik, 24 hourly epochs) to
5e-7 m, half a unit of the printed sixth decimal (`--selftest`). It is fed the SIF columns exactly as
`StationInfoFile::parseOtlBlock` and `OceanTideLoad::computeOtlDisplacement` feed the Fortran routine: UP,
EAST, NORTH to the up, west and south rows, ENU = (-west, -south, up), epoch seconds truncated to whole
seconds. Arithmetic is double precision where HARDISP keeps single precision in a few routines, a 1e-9 m
effect.

**Harness.** `scripts/otl_compare/otl_gate.py` runs `gnss_otl_diag series` (production code) and the port over
2024-129 UTC at 120 s (721 epochs) for three stations: KOKB00USA (Pacific island, diurnal-dominated, 45 mm
radial peak), BRST00FRA (macro-tidal, 64 mm radial peak) and WTZZ00DEU (the held-out consumer, 13 mm). Output
`D:/GnssProcessorSupport/gates/otl/gate_result.txt`.

## 3. The SIF horizontal convention

The SIF block is generated from BLQ files (its comment lines read "FES2014b", "Computed using CARGA at
SEGAL" and "RADI TANG"), whose rows are radial, west positive and south positive. The SIF names the columns
UP, EAST and NORTH. `processing.enable_station_info` has always converted them as true east and north
(`west = east`, phase + 180 degrees, same for south), which reverses the horizontal vector if the columns
hold the BLQ rows. The evidence, in order of strength:

1. **NGS reads them as BLQ rows.** `StationInfo::parseOtlBlock` puts UP, EAST, NORTH into columns 0, 1, 2 of
   the amplitude and phase arrays, and `OceanTideLoad::computeOtlDisplacement` hands those to `hardisp_` as
   its up, west and south rows and returns ENU = (-west, -south, up). M-PAGES' own grid reader
   (`Lib/OtlGrid/get_otl_grid.f`) derives the horizontals from radial gradients and labels them west and
   south. (M-PAGES' `OceanTideLoad::getJsonBlock` labels the three array columns north, east, up, the reverse
   of the SIF order; that writer feeds no processing path and is noted for NGS only.)
2. **The depression gradient.** Loading draws the crust toward the load, so a station's horizontal vector
   at the instant of maximum radial depression points toward increasing depression. The radial gradient is
   measured from neighbouring stations of the same SIF (plane fit within 60 km, at least 6 neighbours,
   `scripts/otl_compare/sif_horizontal_convention.py --gradient`). With the east and north columns read as
   BLQ rows the vector points up the gradient for 554 of 568 stations (98 %) for M2 within 60 km and 1254 of
   1318 (95 %) within 120 km; S2 189 of 189 and 337 of 343, N2 28 of 28 and 124 of 125, O1 328 of 343 (96 %)
   and 844 of 971 (87 %), K1 396 of 456 (87 %) and 937 of 1278 (73 %). The true-east-and-north reading is the
   complement. A coarser test (direction to the nearest point of the 110 m coastline, same script, default
   mode) gave 61 % for M2 within 100 km and is superseded: the coastline is too coarse for stations tens of
   kilometres from it.
3. **Onsala against the HARDISP.F header.** The header prints CSR4.0 BLQ rows for Onsala; the SIF holds
   FES2014b for ONSA00SWE (`scripts/otl_compare/sif_vs_blq_rows.py`). For the semidiurnal south row the
   phase differences are -9, 8 and -18 degrees (M2, S2, N2) under the BLQ-row reading against 171, -172 and
   162 under the shifted one; for the west row 47, 98 and 38 against -133, -82 and -142. Amplitude-weighted
   over the eight largest constituents, 44 against 136 degrees. Reykjavik is inconclusive (96 against 84
   degrees): the two ocean models disagree by up to 82 degrees on the radial row there.

**Consequence.** The `enable_station_info` conversion reverses the horizontal loading displacement. Left in
place, because changing it would move the output of every run that arms the subsystem (the one config that does
is `config/ppp_rtk_internal_AR.json`) and the standing rule is default-off and byte-identical; recorded in
`docs/status/OPEN_ITEMS.md`. The fix is `SifOtlHorizontal::BlqRows` at one call site in `Geometry::loadData`;
the unit suite pins both mappings.

## 4. Stations that the SIF does not serve

`scripts/otl_compare/station_handling.py` hands the RINEX MARKER NAME and APPROX POSITION of each station to
the production lookup (4-character prefix candidates are confirmed by position) and prints the record found.
Output `D:/GnssProcessorSupport/gates/otl/station_handling.txt`. 64 of the 75 stations (58 roster, the
WTZZ00DEU consumer, 16 census) resolve to a SIF record with an OTL block, and 11 do not.

- **Without a SIF OTL block (no record in the IGS20 or NCN20 SIF):** GAMB00PYF (the 4-character prefix
  candidate GAMB00USA is 7,688 km away and rejected), NYA200NOR, WARK00NZL, WTZS00DEU, GOP600CZE, LEIJ00DEU,
  FFMJ00DEU; census ANMG00MYS, UTQI00USA, ARHT00ATA, WUH200CHN.
- **STHL00GBR resolves:** the marker STHL matches STHL00SHN (the renamed monument) by prefix and position and
  carries a block, contrary to the premise of the item; so it is served with 19.6 mm M2 radial.
- **What the 11 get.** The chain after the SIF is BLQ (no file is configured on the goldens), then the EOT20
  grid (radial only; the goldens set no `data_download.nc_path`, so it is not read), then zero. They therefore
  carry no ocean tide loading under `otl_from_sif`, while the other 64 do. The EOT20 grid would give them
  these M2 radial amplitudes (mm): GAMB 19.2, NYA2 9.7, WARK 28.5, WTZS 4.7, GOP6 4.5, LEIJ 4.5, FFMJ 5.0,
  ANMG 11.9, UTQI 5.0, ARHT 1.9, WUH2 4.2. On stations that have both, EOT20 agrees with the SIF to a few mm
  at open-ocean sites and can differ by 10 mm near land (CEBR 24.4 against 15.2, HARB 26.0 against 15.6,
  MBAR 19.1 against 9.0), so it is offered as a second arm, not as the default.
- **The sim fixture has no OTL.** `runs/simnet60_quiet_col_24h` was generated without `corrections.ocean_tide`
  (its `corrections` block lists only phase wind-up, pole tide, relativity, satellite antenna, Shapiro, signal
  bias and solid Earth tide), and its markers (`S01000SIM`, ...) are not in any SIF. The sim golden is
  therefore unchanged by the keys, switched on or off.
- **Goldens.** Both goldens set `enable_ocean_tide_loading` false, so neither key does anything on them; the
  loader default of that switch is true, which is why the keys are inert-until-armed in every config that sets
  it false and active in the default PPP configs only through BLQ or EOT20.

## 7. Open decisions

- Arming is the author's decision after the check. Nothing in the goldens or the manifest changes here.
- The `enable_station_info` conversion (section 3) is a defect left in place for byte-identity; one call site
  fixes it.
- The admittance-interpolated minor constituents (section 2) are the remaining 2 mm radial. The 342-line
  evaluation is a bounded build (tables from `ADMINT.F`, a spline per band and component per station, cached per
  epoch); it is worth building only if the check shows the radial residual matters at the consumer.
- The 11 stations without a SIF block (section 4) are served by nothing under `otl_from_sif`; the EOT20 arm
  shows whether a radial-only fallback helps or hurts them.
