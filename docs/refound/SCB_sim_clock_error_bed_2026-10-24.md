## 1. The question

The simulated network bed is the producer's only absolute grade. Its golden fixture takes the COD final clock as the simulator's truth clock, and the producer takes the same product as its a priori, so the estimable satellite clock truth is identically zero and the temporal anchor (`network.clock_anchor_q` 2.9e-8, about 1 mm of permitted wander per 30 s) holds the estimate at the truth. The agreement of the estimated wire with the truth wire to within 0.9 cm (status report T2) therefore certifies the algebra and the biases, not the clock. On the field (status report T3, DOY 129), the wire's correction to COD against the GFZ final is 4.3 cm (GPS, hours 1 to 6), the precision of the IGS real-time combination, and is uncorrelated with COD's own error against GFZ or ESA: relative to its a priori the wire adds noise. Whether that is the estimator's information content or the inheritance built into the anchored posture cannot be told on the field, where no truth exists.

The question of this card: when the simulator's satellite clock carries an error of known statistics against the COD product, does the golden posture estimate it or inherit the a priori, how close to the truth do the self-contained regime and the native broadcast-clock posture come on the same bed, and what does each cost the held-out consumer against its floor?

| key | value | meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sat_clock.bias0_sigma_m` | 0.5 | a constant offset per satellite, drawn once (1-sigma, m) |

The simulator adds the error to the product clock in the forward model only (`SatClockError`, a pure function of satellite and time, pre-generated on the epoch grid from a dedicated hash-seeded stream) and writes the truth to `truth_satclock.csv` in every station directory. Every other stream (measurement noise, multipath, slips, troposphere, receiver clock, phase colour, ambiguities, biases) is unchanged by construction; gate F1 checks it. The constant offset tests whether the level is estimated (a free first epoch takes it on code) or inherited; the rate tests whether a slow systematic departure from the a priori is followed or fought by the anchor (its 0.3 mm per epoch is inside the golden anchor's permitted 0.93 mm per epoch, so a tracking estimator can follow it, and a prior-dominated one lags); the random walk is the physical clock behaviour the anchor's spectral density nominally models.

The fixture is otherwise the quiet one. The start artifact is not repaired here (the simulator loads one product day; a two-day load is a simulator change, recorded as owed), so the sim grading convention of 00:30:15 stands, and the clock fixture differs from the quiet fixture in the `sat_clock` block alone.

**The a priori and the truth.** On the COD a priori arms (`g`, `gg`, `c`) the wire's C record is the correction to the COD clock, so the clock error against truth is C minus the injected error, plus the datum share. On the native arm (`n`) the producer's internal a priori is the broadcast clock made continuous across cut-overs (card NBP), and the wire's C carries the cut-over offset O, so the estimate's absolute clock is the stamped broadcast record plus C (`logs/network_brdc_offset.csv` gives `brdc_m`, `offset_m`, `c_est_m`, `c_wire_m` per satellite and epoch) and the truth's absolute clock is the COD CLK value plus the injected error. The datum share is the per-satellite IF(C1C, C2W) combination of the truth OSB (`clklevel.datum_share`, card SCL2's convention: the pinned reference pair's bias share sits in the clock and is not error); for Galileo the same combination over (C1C, C5Q), the product's own reference pair.
