## (d) The decisions: options, evidence, cost

### Evidence and its limits

Rules A (a float watcher) and B (an output-only fix) were emulated offline with the float sibling in place of an in-engine float (`counterfactuals.csv`, `counterfactual_actions_7runs.csv`).

**Circularity.** On observed data the float sibling is also the grader. Rule A at T >= 0.25 therefore cannot release a right pair-epoch by construction. Rule B withholds every pair-epoch the grader calls wrong by construction as well. Their observed catch carries no independent information. The informative outputs are:

- the latency from the hold start;
- the actions on unverified holds;
- the availability cost;
- the false releases on the simulator, whose truth for Galileo rests entirely on a nearest-epoch consensus fallback.

**Other limits.**
- The float sibling is not the fixing user's own float before the write-back. It runs without wl_branch_check and shares the partner refloat (P8).
- The counterfactual part's RULES.md has a birth time (10:52:59) after two of its result files (calibration_D.csv 10:01, actions_7runs.csv 10:45). Its "written before any result" is unconfirmed (skeptic, other findings).

### Decision 1 (prerequisite): keep the release and reset lists on a failed attempt

- **Evidence.** A failed attempt drops both lists (AR:700-717). Failure is the normal outcome: 96.5 % of search epochs.
- **Effect today (inferred).** On today's observed users the change is inert: HOLDREL never fires, and the code-bias reset is applied only with estimate_rx_sat_code_bias, absent from all 105 arm.json files (loader default false, `src/core/config/GnssConfigLoad.cpp:1221-1222`; RTE:297-304). Partner refloats survive, because they are appended after resolveCore_ (AR:514-518).
- **Cost** (`options_cost.csv`). Est. 60-120 lines. Key `estimation.ambiguity_resolution.keep_lists_on_failure`, default false. No state, no runtime. Owner: user-hold-honesty, after user-resolver-split step 6.
- **Role.** Any release rule (2a, 2b, 2c) loses its refloat in 96.5 % of epochs without it.

### Decision 2: the quantity an independent release test reads (P1)

**2a. Shadow float in StateManager**
- *Catch of the 7 runs*, stand-in rule A at T 0.25 with gate G1: 5 of 7 runs (4 of 6 holds). Misses UTQI-143 and CHTI-G26: the sibling's wide-lane sigma never reached 0.05.
- *Latency from the hold start:* 7, 30, 55 and 126 epochs (median of the 5 runs 55 epochs, 27.5 min). With no gate: 0 to 11 epochs on all 7.
- *Actions on unverified holds, observed:* 22 918 with gate G1 (45 430 with no gate). On UTQI DOY 146 alone, 162 actions fall on four holds never graded wrong: E12 85, E19 43, E04 27, E15 7 (`scratch/skeptic/ruleA_146.txt`).
- *False releases, simulator:* primary Galileo 102 (strict 91), primary GPS 48, all GPS 3 495 of 58 394 right pair-epochs (6.0 %).
- *Cost:* est. 700-1200 lines; about 35 StateManager layout mutators to mirror; StateManager owns the shadow; at most +100 % of the filter's share of 0.128 s per epoch; 45 KB. Key release_from_shadow_float.

**2a'. Second float engine in the replay driver**
- *Evidence:* as 2a. It inherits the partner refloat (P8).

## (e) Recommendation

**Repair P1 first, with option 2d, and build decision 1 with it as its prerequisite.**
- *Order.* 2d is an output-only fix behind `estimation.ambiguity_resolution.output_only_fix`, default off. Decision 1 is `keep_lists_on_failure`. - *Defer.* Decision 3 stays at 3a. Decisions 4 and 6 are not built now.

**Reasons.**
1. **P1 is the one mechanism in every case and in every population.** No hold release fired in 105 observed fixing user-days or 454 simulator users (two searches each). Every wrong run ended by geometry or by the reference satellite leaving, never by a test. Repairing admission (P2) or lists (P3) alone leaves a wrong hold, once admitted, held to the horizon.
2. **2d removes the cause rather than adding a watcher of it.** The state is never pulled, so the existing hold test W3 reads a true float. P4 and most W14 rejections vanish by construction, which makes decisions 4 and 6 moot under 2d. It is the cheapest independent quantity: est. 120-250 lines, no runtime, no parallel state. 2a costs 700-1200 lines and about 35 mirror sites, where one missed site is a silent defect. 2b sees 3 of 7 runs. 2c has no data.
3. **Decision 1 is a prerequisite and costs little.** Any release path loses its action in 96.5 % of search epochs without it. It is inert on today's observed users (inferred), so its identity check is clean.
4. **Decision 3 has no separating evidence.** W1 only delays (540 s of uninterrupted code admission on E18-E28). W2 reaches a verdict in 40 of 8 240 events. The margin ranks the wrong admissions at percentiles 30 and 96. The parts proposed a refusal of young or re-seeded code admissions; the skeptic showed it has no measured separation, and it is dropped here as a remedy for these cases.
5. **Reference handling (P5) matters less under 2d.** The output integer is re-searched every epoch, so an orphaned memory loses only a release test, not a correction. Under 2a it is a prerequisite: a watcher keyed on sat_i|sat_ref loses every held pair of a group at each of 30 to 40 reference changes per user-day. It must key on satellite-level integers or wait for decision 5 (user-reference-switching).

**The price of 2d.**
- It gives up hold continuity. A user's fix then depends on every epoch's search.
- The offline lower bound of the availability loss is 4.1 % of right held pair-epochs on observed data. On the simulator, with partial fixing on, it is 33 to 41 %.
- Every user output moves, and the F6 grader must change.

If the author rules that fix-and-hold stays, the alternative order is decision 1, then decision 5 (or a satellite-keyed watcher), then 2a, then 6b with 4b, armed only behind 2a. That is the order the parts shared.

**Where this differs from the parts.** The parts and the skeptic named "a watcher keyed off reference changes, then 6b with 4b". This paper recommends 2d first because its cost is a fifth of 2a's and it needs neither 4b nor 6b. The evidence for either rests on the same float-sibling stand-in, so neither is preferred on catch.
