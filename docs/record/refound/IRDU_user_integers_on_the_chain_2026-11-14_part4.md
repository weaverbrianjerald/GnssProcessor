### 12.6 2026-11-14: the position floor that integers do not explain, user replays only (pre-registered before the replays)

**Assigned** by the requesting session in the author's absence after its re-grade of 12.5, whose own readings (its scripts) are: late-start users from 30 min, second hour, float 23 mm on the truth stream, 119 mm on Q1's and 95 mm without pins; S48000SIM and SZZ000SIM hold 92.2 and 92.4 % on Q1's stream against 92.5 and 91.2 % on the truth stream, at 45 and 69 mm against 8 and 18 mm; each satellite's ionosphere-free phase term against truth, less the epoch's median over satellites and the satellite's own mean, 2.9 mm rms on Q1's stream (p95 5.6 mm; 0 on the truth stream, 13.1 mm without pins), the same term on the code side about 22 mm. The cause is in neither the integers, the start-up nor the phase term that moves.

**Read before the replays** (streams and existing user logs only):

- x per satellite (card IRD section 8; `scripts/irdu_code_datum.py terms`): on Q1's stream and the reference row 0.83 to 4.48 m (median 1.95 m), constant to 0.1 mm over the run; 0 on the truth stream. Observed data give the same values: the broadcast T_GD against CODE's observable-specific code biases of the day (`irdu_code_datum.py observed`) reproduces x for all 32 satellites to the millimetre (G01 4.481 m, G19 0.827 m). The simulator's truth code biases are CODE's, and the network's code biases sit at the broadcast T_GD exactly as on observed data, so whatever x does here it does on observed data. Its common part (1.83 m over the satellites) falls on the receivers; the part specific to a satellite is 0.49 m (p50) and up to 2.65 m.
- r, the range-like code term: each satellite's mean against the epoch's median 3.5 cm (p50) and 9.5 cm (max) on Q1's stream.
- the users' ionosphere: one slant state per satellite (between-satellite single differences, the pivot's state frozen), seeded at 0 with a 50 m prior (the seed from the code needs code-bias coverage, which the replayed user's configuration leaves off, so it falls back to 0 with ten times the 5 m initial sigma), random walk 0.001 m^2/s scaled by the obliquity; the stream carries no ionosphere record and `consume_network_iono` is inert in a replay. By the algebra this user takes a constant x whole into its ionosphere states.
- satellites: each user tracks the same satellites on the truth stream as on Q1's (8.3 to 10.2 per 15 min bin); the accuracy records (U) are the same on the three streams (mean 0.096 m).
- the replay's resets: Q1's phase records carry no discontinuity counter, and a user on it resets ambiguities at 380 satellite-signal epochs from the early start, at 3 to 7 after a 30 min start (all between 31 and 35.5 min) and at none after a 45 min start; none on the truth stream. The 45 min start stalls like the 30 min one, so the resets do not set the floor. The wide-lane flag of the phase records (1 on the truth stream, 0 on Q1's) is parsed and read nowhere in the user.
- the users' wet delay (`scripts/irdu_user_zwd.py`: the absolute estimate in each user's `rt_engine.log` against `truth_tropo.csv`), median over the users per 15 min from 30 min after the start: the truth stream -11 to +10 mm (one bin -25); Q1's -13 to -34 mm at all three starts; the reference row -4 to -27 mm. The network's own wet delay on Q1's run is within 1 to 3 mm of the truth from 15 min (median over the stations). A height error of about 2.5 times a 2 to 3 cm wet-delay error is the floor's size.

**The cases** (one change each against Q1, or against the truth stream for the users' configuration; the five users of section 9, float and fixing):

1. The code datum (candidate 1): `_rap_irdu_cdx_nr3`, Q1's products with x removed (B'_b = B_b + mu_b x, P'_b = P_b - mu_b x: x' = 0, r and every E_b unchanged, so every pair keeps its integers), from the start and from 30 min (20 runs).

Additions of this session, the remaining terms one at a time, from 30 min only (30 runs): 4. `_rap_irdu_cdr_nr3`, r removed (B'_b = B_b + r: the code equals the truth's but for x); 5. `_rap_irdu_cdxr_nr3`, x and r removed (code corrections the truth's, phase corrections the truth's plus lambda_b E_b); 6. `_rap_irdu_cdxrf_nr3`, x, r and the fraction of E_b removed (phase corrections the truth's plus whole cycles only: the frame's integer offsets per satellite and band). **Rule (written before the replays).** A case removes the floor when its float median over the users is within 1.5 times the truth stream's (same user configuration) in each of the bins 45 to 60, 60 to 75 and 75 to 90 min after a 30 min start; it leaves the floor when within 1.25 times Q1's (same user configuration) in those bins; otherwise it removes part. From the start, the second hour's four bins are read the same way.

**Expectation.** The algebra predicts no floor at all: x is taken whole by the users' ionosphere states, the common modes cancel between satellites, every constant on the phase falls into a float ambiguity, and what moves is 3 mm on the phase. In every case the users' wet-delay error tracks the floor.

**Decisions in the author's absence.** (1) Cases 4 to 6 are added, from 30 min only. (2) Cases 1 and 2 run from both starts. (3) The free ionosphere is realized in the users' configuration only (initial sigma and random walk). (4) The facts above were read before the expectation was written; the wet-delay reading is an addition. (5) The observed-data comparison of x takes CODE's biases as the stand-in for the truth.

**2026-11-14, addendum to 12.6, written before its replays (additions of this session).** What the replays of 12.6 showed before this was written (results in 12.7): cases 1, 2, 4 and 5 leave the floor; case 6 as built removes most of it, but its construction missed its own definition: rounding E_b at each epoch flips the integer wherever the fraction crosses half a cycle (285 changes after 30 min on 22 satellites; |fraction| p50 0.17 and p95 0.46 cycle per band), so its phase carries whole-cycle jumps and its users reset (77 times at SZZ000SIM). Case 6' `_rap_irdu_cdxri_nr3`: x, r and every fraction removed against one integer per satellite and band (the rounded median of E_b over 30 to 120 min): phase corrections the truth's plus constant whole cycles. Case 7 `_rap_irdu_cdcm_nr3`: only each band's common drift removed from Q1's phase (P'_b = P_b + cm_b(t)), everything else kept, each satellite's own wander included. Both checked (deviation 4e-14 cycle; common drift left 1e-15 m). Users from 30 min (20 runs), the rule of 12.6.

Expectation: case 6' removes the floor (the fraction carries it, as the flawed case 6 suggests). Case 7: by the algebra a common drift cancels, so case 7 leaves the floor and the cause is each satellite's own wander; if case 7 removes the floor, the users do not cancel a drift common to every satellite, which is a property of the user (its pivot handling, or a state that reads one satellite's correction alone), not of the stream.

Decisions in the author's absence: (6) case 6 is rebuilt with constant integers, the definition 12.6 wrote ("the frame's integer offsets per satellite and band"), and its first build is reported as voided by its construction; (7) case 7 is added.

**Verdicts.** Case 1 leaves the floor, as expected: the users take x whole, from either start. Case 2 leaves it, and its expectation fails in part: the free ionosphere reads like the configured one on both streams (the configured users' ionosphere is already free in effect), so nothing slows. Case 7 removes most of it, against the expectation: with only the drift common to the satellites removed, the users read at the truth level from 45 to 60 min (70 against 76 mm), the excess over the truth stream at 60 to 90 min falls from 97 and 73 mm to 23 and 12 mm, the wet-delay bias from -13 to -33 mm to -9 to -18 mm, and three of the five users reach a sustained 5 cm (30.0, 59.0 and 46.0 min; on the truth stream 33.5, 50.5 and 54.0; on Q1 none). The rest (case 7 against case 6': 45 against 22 and 58 against 46 mm at 60 to 90 min) is the satellites' own wander.

**Candidate 3.** The users track the same satellites on every stream (8.3 to 10.2 per 15 min bin); their wet-delay error follows the floor in every case (-13 to -34 mm where the floor stands, -4 to -10 mm where it goes); on the truth stream their own second-hour positions are 11 to 36 mm at SZZ000SIM, S48000SIM and S40000SIM and 71 to 75 mm at S27000SIM and S32000SIM (either start; the two weaker users read 0.32 on N_1 even on the truth stream since section 12.1), so the bed supports centimetre users at three of the five.

**The drift.** Q1's phase errors, each satellite's less its own mean over 30 to 120 min, share a drift of 84 mm over 90 min (-37 mm at 30 to 45 min, +19 mm at 105 to 120 min; the reference row 94 mm), while the code's shared part moves 11 mm. It is not the simulator's: the root receiver's phase bias is constant there (66.9 mm on L1C and 24.0 mm on L2W at S30000SIM) and its phase colour, averaged over the root's satellites, moves a few millimetres. It is the network's phase datum moving against its code datum, a term that the users' single differences should remove. A least-squares projection of Q1's phase errors onto a user with an ambiguity per satellite, a clock per epoch, a wet delay and a position (`irdu_stream_projection.py`) moves the wet delay by 1.5 to 2.8 mm only: a free clock takes the drift, so what these users do with it is not what an ambiguity-and-clock float does.

**On observed data.** x is the same on observed data (12.6) and costs the users nothing (case 1). The network realizes its phase datum the same way on observed data, so a user that does not cancel a common phase drift meets the same cost there; the drift's size on observed data is not measured here.

**What it means for the author's question.** With every integer right, the stream is good enough: given the same stream less a drift that a user should cancel, the users read at the truth level from 45 min after a late start and within 25 mm of it after 60 min, and their integers are right. The floor that the integers do not explain is mostly the users' handling of a phase drift common to the satellites. Its cause in the user is not traced (candidates: the pivot handling of the single differences, a state that reads one satellite's correction alone, or a time constraint on a term common to the satellites).

**Read against the IRD family.** Card IRD section 8's stream algebra assumes that a user absorbs x and every term common to the satellites; the data confirm the first and refute the second for this user. The 0.1 m floor of 12.5 is therefore not evidence against the integer-recovering chain: on this bed the chain's integers deliver a truth-level stream up to its common drift and the satellites' wander.

**What B needs** (the all-integers case on r3c; not built, being larger than the two hours the assignment allows before stopping). The oracle pin needs the chain frame computed during the run, because r3c's links rise, set and re-host every few minutes: (1) a live frame mode, each epoch taking the chain's tree from the stations' pinned targets and every arc's true integer in the producer's wind-up convention (the simulator's slip-accumulated integer plus the producer's own wind-up branch of the arc), each chord's frame integer N~ = N(chord) + the sum over its tree path of c (N(link) - k(link)), every chord held at lambda N~; (2) event responses at both edges with counters and identities, at a true slip and at a cut release or re-host (the chords whose frame integer changes, and whether the chain's own new target equals the frame's); (3) a trace of every chord's frame integer per epoch; (4) a time scope that holds only the links born before a given time, for the steady-state reading; (5) the key default off, refused without the oracle pin, a ledger row UNVALIDATED, unit tests (path sums, a re-host, a slip, the scope), off byte-identical on both beds, Release and Debug builds. Estimate: four to six hours of build and tests, then about an hour of runs and grading on the adopted arm.

**Decisions in the author's absence, for review.** Those of 12.6 and its addendum, and: (8) the projection, the common-drift reading and the root receiver check were read after the replays and are not registered; (9) the floor's cause in the user is named, not traced or changed; (10) B is stopped at its sizing, as the assignment allows.

### 12.8 2026-11-14: why the users keep a term shared by the satellites, the streams with only that term held out, and where it comes from (pre-registered before the replays)

**Assigned** by the requesting session in the author's absence after its re-grade of 12.7, with its own truth-free reading of the shared drift (`stream_common.py`: per signal, the median over the satellites of the epoch-to-epoch change of code bias minus phase bias, summed over time). The term is in the network's holds-off emission on observed data too, and the emission hold is what removes it today.

**Read before the replays** (code and existing runs):

- The users' differencing (`measurement/BssdTransform`) forms the single difference on the innovations per constellation and signal against a pivot (z_i - z_p, H_i - H_p) and zeroes the receiver clock, the receiver code biases and the pivot's ambiguity, ionosphere and satellite-bias columns, so a term the same for every satellite cancels in every row. The pivot handover (`filter/PivotManager`, the ambiguity and ionosphere re-reference) is exact in state space: each state less the new pivot's, the pivot set to 0.
- Q1's users and case 7's (the shared drift removed) carry identical single-difference rows in their traces, but their ambiguity states first differ at G13 one minute after a 30 min start, the epoch at which the Q1 user reset G13. The replay reconstructs the producer's live discontinuity counters (a phase bias that moves by more than 0.05 m or 0.25 cycle between epochs bumps its counter, and the user resets that ambiguity): Q1's users reset 3 to 7 times between 31 and 35.5 min, case 7's not at all. The term's movement between epochs adds to each satellite's own, so it can carry a satellite over the gate.
- The truth-free shared drift (`irdu_code_datum.py`, truthfree_shared, the requesting session's definition) equals the truth-based shared drift of the phase against the code exactly while the truth biases are constant, and reproduces the requesting session's numbers (125 / 159, 158 / 201 and 156 / 238 mm; 0 on the truth stream and on the golden arm with the holds). ### 12.10 2026-11-14: the users' rule for satellites without a stream record (a default-off key), and the 27 resets (pre-registered before the runs)

**Assigned** by the requesting session in the author's absence after its re-grade of 12.9. **The rule.** Key `network.closed_loop.drop_unrecorded` (the closed-loop consumer), default false, copied into the consumer engine as `processing.ssr_drop_unrecorded`, which a standalone consumer sets directly. Armed, the consumer leaves out of its solution every satellite for which the stream holds no clock record at the epoch (`SsrCorrectionStore::getOrbitClock` at the model's own freshness limit, `getSsrMaxAge`, the test by which the model applies the correction), the simpler of the two forms: a satellite without a record is in no single difference with the stream's satellites because it is not in the solution. Placement: the measurement-set conditioning seam (`RealTimeEnginePrepare.cpp`), beside the existing drop of broadcast-grade satellites, before the model chooses its pivot and builds its rows; the innovation screen (`RealTimeEngineScreen.cpp`) runs inside the update after the pivot is chosen, where a satellite left out could already be the pivot. Edges: a satellite seen without a record (at its first sighting, or when its record is lost) is a drop event, and a dropped satellite whose record returns is a readmit event; each event is logged with the satellite and the time, and a run-end line gives the counters (drops, of them first sightings; readmits; satellite-epochs left out; satellites still out at the end). At the decoupling edge the model prunes the satellite's ambiguity and ionosphere states, as for a satellite out of view (`pruneAmbiguitiesExcept`, `pruneIonosphereStatesExcept`); at the re-coupling edge they are seeded afresh from that epoch's code, a new float, so nothing estimated against the a priori clock is carried into the stream's frame. The decisions are a pure gate (`realtime/UnrecordedSatGate`) with unit tests. Off, the outputs are byte-identical.

**Gates before any reading.** The gate's unit tests (edges and counters); byte identity with the key off: this branch's Release build before and after the change on the same user replays (Q1's stream from 30 min, five users, float and fixing; r3c as emitted from 30 min), every output file identical.
