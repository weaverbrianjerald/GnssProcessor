## 12. 2026-11-14: ring-by-ring integer fixing down the chain, true integers first (pre-registered)

**The author's words.** The idea, verbatim: "Why don't we fix the "one link" then fix "two links" from root and continue fixing down the chain?" The question as put by the requesting session: "Test ring-by-ring integer fixing on the simulator, with true integers first?" The answer, verbatim: "Yes, test it". What was described to the author before the answer: two cheap cases, neither of which builds an integer search: (1) give the second ring's links their true integers and check that the third ring becomes as good as the first (0.02 cycle), then the next ring; (2) let plain rounding pick the integers in that order and count the wrong ones against truth. It was said to be card IRD's step 5 done in a specific order, and to touch the do-not-build ruling. The author is away for several hours: "From the results of the test, assign work to Opus, while I am away for the next several hours." Choices the scope leaves open are made in the author's absence and listed as such below.

**The scope, as the requesting session reads it (a reading, not the author's words).** A simulator measurement on the adopted arm (the wet delay carried). Truth-fed pins and would-be rounding counted against truth. No integer search, nothing armed, no reference configuration or ledger verdict changed. The do-not-build on integer resolution in the network stands for anything beyond this measurement until the author rules on its results. Any key is sim-only, default off, with an UNVALIDATED ledger row, and byte-identical when off.

**The rings.** On the chain's tree the root receiver has depth 0, and each link joins a receiver and a satellite. A link's ring is the larger depth of its two vertices: ring k holds every link, tree link or chord, from a depth-k vertex up the tree, and ring 1 is the root's links, all of them tree links. On the bed without re-seats (`irdu_nr`, the third cut of section 11.1; one tree for the whole run, root S30000SIM, depth 5) the chords number 54, 130, 94 and 94 band rows in rings 2, 3, 4 and 5, beside 174 tree-link band rows (`config/sim/irdu/fpin_nr3_all.csv`). The requesting session's link counts, re-graded here to the digit: on that bed the receivers at depth 2 see a median of 2 satellites of ring 1 (1 to 4; two receivers see four or more), the satellites at depth 3 a median of 4 receivers nearer the root, the receivers at depth 4 a median of 3 satellites, the satellites at depth 5 a median of 9 receivers; on r3c at 110 min (473 arcs, 89 tree edges) the medians are 2 (1 to 7, 14 with four or more), 8, 6, 12, 7 and 16 down to depth 7. Strict depth order therefore starts weak: half the depth-2 receivers tie to the root's satellites through one or two links.

**The instrument: the oracle pin in the chain's frame** (`network.cycle_slip.oracle_pin_frame_file`; new, simulation only, default empty, ledger row UNVALIDATED). The simulation-only oracle pin (a `network.inject` event of kind `oracle_pin`, `docs/record/SPEC_oracle_pin.md`) holds float ambiguities at truth through one stiff diagonal prior row per pinned column (sigma 1e-4 m). Its existing target fits station offsets in the reference configuration's anchor frame. The chain needs no fit: every tree link is pinned at an integer target, so a chord's absolute ambiguity in the chain's frame is λ times the integer N~ = N_true(chord) + Σ c (N_true(path link) − k_link), which the simulator's truth and the tree's targets give offline (`scripts/ird_frame_census.py`, `frame_integer`). A chord's carried value over its wavelength equals the chord table's float to 5e-7 cycle (checked on `_rap_irdu_zw_nr3`), so a pin at λ N~ sits in the frame the chain estimates in. The new key names a file (`scripts/irdu_frame_targets.py`: station, sat, signal, frame_int, role, ring) and replaces the fit by it. Every listed link the chain pins this epoch is checked at λ N~: a chain pin at any other value means the frame has stepped, and every frame pin is released for good. Every other listed link in scope is held at λ N~. A link whose true integer changes (a true slip) is released for good. Counters and identities: the oracle pin's per-arc `pinned` and `released` event rows (both edges, standing invariant 7) and run-end counts of links checked at their integer, tree links of the file that the chain no longer pins (then held by the oracle), links released at a true slip, and the epoch of a frame step. Without the key the oracle pin is unchanged; without an `oracle_pin` event the pin object is never built, so a run without the event is byte-identical (gate: the adopted arm on the bed without re-seats and on r3c reproduces `_rap_irdu_zw_nr3` and `_rap_irdu_zw_r3c` whole on the new build, `gnss_processor_irdu_fp2.exe` (sha256[:16] `269fd459a98fb467`, this branch plus the key): IDENTICAL on both, `nom_td_gate.py --whole --mask-time`, runs `_rap_irdu_fp2off_zw_nr3` and `_rap_irdu_fp2off_zw_r3c`). Unit tests: `tests/network/test_oracle_pin_frame.cpp` (the parser, links pinned and checked, a re-host onto a link at its integer keeping the frame, a step latching the release, the release at a true slip); on the Debug build its 8 cases and the 22 existing oracle pin cases pass, 30 of 30, and the neighbouring suites (contributions, station build, inject) 127 of 127. The 0.1 h smoke of Q1 (`_rap_irdu_smk_q1_nr3`) arms the mode: 372 chord rows held, 2 604 prior rows injected, every tree link found at its integer, the frame unchanged, nothing released.

**The split of the float-user gap.** A user's phase correction is the stream's clock minus its phase bias, and its code correction the clock minus the code bias (the wire algebra of card IRD section 8). Two hybrid streams on the bed without re-seats, made from `_rap_irdu_q1_nr3` and `_rap_irdu_tw_nr3` by `scripts/irdu_hybrid_products.py`, keep one stream's clocks, code biases, header and every other record and give the users the other stream's phase clocks exactly, by rewriting each phase bias as P_hybrid = P_phase-donor + C_code-donor - C_phase-donor:

- (a) `_rap_irdu_hyb_a_nr3`: Q1's phase clocks with the truth stream's clocks and code biases;
- (b) `_rap_irdu_hyb_b_nr3`: the truth stream's phase clocks with Q1's clocks and code biases.

Each is replayed at the five users, float and fixing (the recipe of section 2; arm.json of the truth-wire folder, holds off on both). Beside them, the range-like term r of card IRD section 8 (the stream's code error common to both bands), read as its spread between satellites at each epoch (each satellite's r less the epoch's median over satellites), for Q1 against the truth stream.

What the hybrids can and cannot show. A user's ionosphere takes up the geometry-free shift of the code datum it is given, and the two donors hold different code datums (Q1 pins each satellite's geometry-free code bias at the broadcast T_GD, the truth stream holds the truth): in each hybrid the phase's geometry-free part comes from the other datum. The users' float ambiguities absorb a constant shift of that kind, so the float position (the ionosphere-free part, where the shift cancels) is the reading that splits the gap; the fixing readings are reported, and their wide-lane fractions are expected near the null, as the phase-only wide lane reads in sections 11.2 and 12.1.

Expectation (written before the replays). Q1's phase clocks sit at the truth level (D_NL 0.02 cycle at every depth, per-epoch steps of 0.5 mm), so the gap is expected in the clocks and code biases: (a) near the truth wire at the float user (median over users in hour 2 at most 1.5 times its 26 mm), (b) near Q1 (100 mm or more), and the spread of r between satellites at the decimetre level on Q1. Rule: the half whose hybrid is farther from the truth wire at the float user's median in hour 2 carries the gap; both are reported with their ratio to the truth wire and to Q1, and a split in between is reported as shared.

The error grows with the cycle's length: at 60 min |e| p50 is 0.112, 0.175, 0.167 and 0.265 cycle on cycles through 2, 3, 4 and 5 receivers (at 20 min 0.316, 0.433, 0.555 and 0.489). The expectation holds for the slope and not for the trend: the correlation falls as the wet-delay errors shrink. Its corollary is revised by the data: the safe first fixes are the links whose cycles are short or whose receivers' mapping differences are small, and a joint decision has to take the wet delays of a cycle's receivers together, not one receiver's.

The rule, read from the code: `growChainForest` (`src/core/network/ar/ArcGraph.cpp`), called per component by `planChainComponent` (`src/core/network/solve/ChainDatum.cpp`), is a breadth-first walk from the datum receiver with two queues. The queue of the last epoch's live tree edges drains completely before any new edge is tried, so every live edge is kept, and a subtree cut off by a dead edge is "re-attached through its own old edges (one new edge per detached fragment) and is not re-wired vertex by vertex: a tree-edge pin moves only when its arc died" (the function's comment). Nothing in the rule looks at depth after the birth: a fragment hangs from whichever of its vertices the first new edge reaches. New edges are tried from vertices in the order they were attached; a second pass, in the legacy vertex order, runs only for a vertex reachable through a refused arc alone (a slipped arc, which the slip row refuses; the young-arc refusal `network.eq34_anchor_min_arc_ep` is 0 here). The datum receiver is the highest-coverage receiver at the birth and is re-elected by the satellites' vote afterwards; coverage is not centrality (r3c at 110 min: the best root would hang 12 satellites at depth 1 and 18 at depth 3).

What a shortest-path election would take and risk. It would attach every vertex at its breadth-first depth at each epoch, give up a live edge whenever a shorter route opens, and possibly move the root to the graph's centre. Every edge it gives up is a re-host, with its cut release and, until the new link is certified, section 11.4's 25 to 45 min of wide-lane cost on the moved subtree (none when the links are at their integers, Q4); a root move re-levels the whole component. On r3c the shortest-path tree changes whenever a satellite rises or sets at a shallow receiver, so the election would add releases to a bed whose difficulty is already a re-seat every 100 s. The ledger's two KEEP_OFF election keys are the measured cases of that trade: `network.phase_datum_eq34_longevity_election` ("Falsified as implemented: re-references 2483 to 2695 and DD step 9 to 103 cyc/ep on its 3 h arm") and `network.phase_datum_eq34_slip_aware_election` ("Measured net-harmful on a 3 h 63-station leg: 102 incumbent de-seats cost total_IF spread +15.7 % and clk_std +20 %"). A narrower form adds no release: keep every live edge, and act only where the walk already moves a pin, by re-attaching a cut fragment through the new edge that leaves its vertices shallowest (one new edge either way; the kept row already holds an edge whose direction turns), and by electing the datum receiver at the birth by depth instead of coverage.
