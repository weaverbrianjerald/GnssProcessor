# SPEC — the T3-class null: the statistic is scale-blind, so the defect is in the POOL

QUEUE item 4 (`docs/QUEUE_principled_replacements.md`): *"T3-class test statistics:
correct nulls, not recalibrated constants."*  Written 2026-08-14, fitting on
`runs/pareref_on` — the null instrument regenerated by item 1(d)
(`SPEC_pivot_visibility_gauge_defect.md` §5.08).

**Headline.** The bandaid is a Gaussian 6σ constant.  The reflex replacement is a
corrected extreme-value quantile.  **Both are wrong, and for the same reason: the
statistic cannot see scale at all.**  It is a ratio of two functionals of the same
pool, so any common rescaling of the residuals cancels exactly — proven here by an
A/B that rescales every row by **×1.45** and moves the statistic's median by
**×1.025**.  What the
statistic *does* see is the pool's internal heterogeneity, and on a fixture where
every station draws the **same** error model that heterogeneity is a **4.7×
multiplicative station effect** and a **2.75× signal effect**.  Removing the station axis alone, fitted
out-of-sample, cuts the false-alarm rate **4.46 % → 0.97 %** at **zero cost in
detection power** (§13: 20/24 injected slips, byte-identical per-rung, 3.9× fewer
flags).  The correct replacement is a **stratified studentization**, not a new
threshold.

⚠ **Field check (§12): the structure reproduces and is stronger — station spread 10.5×,
transferability +0.873 — but the remedy's leverage falls to 1.5×, because the field runs
with σ̂₀ clamped at the 1.0 floor on 88–96 % of epochs.  In that regime the denominator
is a constant and the statistic is an unnormalized max, so there is nothing for a
stratified pool to fix.  Unclamping comes first on the field.**

## 1. The statistic, as actually computed

`src/core/filter/NetworkRaim.cpp`, per epoch:

| step | code | what |
|---|---|---|
| pool | `:157` `ambPool(R)` (`qc_amb` else `pl_amb`) | the station's **carried** ambiguities — a fresh arc has no cell |
| residual | `:168` `resid = R.y − R.A_s·x_s − R.A_r·x_r` | **post-fit** O−C |
| standardize | `:235` `r = |resid|·√w` | **measurement**-standardized |
| (optional) | `:236-241` `r /= √r_i`, rows with `r_i ≤ kBaardaRedundancyFloor` dropped | Baarda redundancy — **`network.raim_redundancy_normalize` defaults to `false`** (`GnssConfigLoadNetwork.cpp:3461`) |
| row pool | `:242` `r_pool.push_back(r)` | every phase row of every carried ambiguity, network-wide |
| cell | `:243`,`:245-246` `r_max` over the ambiguity's rows, then over the (sta,sat)'s signals | a **max** |
| scale | `:262` `σ̂₀ = max(raim_s0_floor, 1.4826·median(r_pool))` | a **median**; `raim_s0_floor` is a local alias for `network.raim_sigma0_floor`, default **1.0** (`GnssConfig.hpp:5925`) |
| test | `:286-287` `s_norm = r_max/σ̂₀ > raim_k` | `raim_k = 6`, compared against a Gaussian two-sided per-observation α |
| counters | `:288` T3 cell, `:304-307` T3c consensus | `network_flag_rates.csv` |

Two structural notes that matter downstream:

- **The pivot station is excluded** unless `phase_datum_prioritized` (`:154-155`).
- **T3c's opportunity count is conditional** — `fr_raim_sat.opp += sat_flag_stations.size()`
  (`:305`) counts only satellites that *already spiked somewhere*, so its rate is a
  conditional probability and its `implied_alpha` column (the same unconditional
  Gaussian α) is not the right comparand at all.

So the shipped statistic is

$$S_c \;=\; \frac{\max_{i\in c} |z_i|}{\max\!\bigl(1,\; 1.4826\cdot \operatorname{med}_{j\in P} |z_j|\bigr)},
\qquad z = |v|\sqrt{w},$$

with $|c| = n_c$ small (3 on GPS triple-frequency uncombined: L1C/L2W/L5Q) and
$|P| = N$ large (≈ 560 rows/epoch on the 24-station fixture).

## 2. The invariance theorem — and why it decides the card

$S_c$ is **invariant under $z \mapsto \lambda z$ for any constant $\lambda$** whenever
the floor is not binding: numerator and denominator are both positively homogeneous of
degree 1 in $z$.

⚠ When the floor **does** bind, $\hat\sigma_0 \equiv 1$ and $S_c = \lambda\max_{i\in c}|z_i|$
— fully scale-*sensitive*.  That is not an escape from the argument; it is a second,
independent defect: **the test is non-pivotal**, switching between two different
statistics depending on whether the epoch's robust level happens to sit above 1.  On
this arm the floor binds on 38 of 114 epochs (concentrated in ignition), and those
epochs average 1.97 flags against 9.51 on the unpinned ones.  In the settled window
$\hat\sigma_0 \in [1.3, 1.6]$, so the invariance argument applies where it matters —
and §5 confirms it empirically end-to-end.

Three consequences, and they cover most of the "obvious" fixes:

1. **No global variance-model correction can change its false-alarm rate.**  Fixing
   the a-priori σ, the elevation weighting, or the redundancy standardization all
   rescale the pool nearly uniformly and therefore cancel.  §5 measures exactly this.
2. **A recalibrated constant `k` cannot work either**, and not only for the reason the
   pivot spec gave (rising implied-scale curve).  Under the *exact* null the shipped
   `k = 6` is **already stricter than a 10⁻⁶ per-cell test** (§3).  A threshold that is
   too strict cannot be the cause of a 10⁷× excess.
3. **What is left is the pool's internal structure.**  $S_c$ measures a cell's
   position *relative to the median of its pool*.  If the pool is a mixture of
   sub-populations with different scales, the numerator is drawn from the widest
   component while the denominator tracks the most numerous one, and the ratio has no
   fixed null at all — its distribution depends on the mixture weights, which change
   every epoch as the station/satellite set changes.

## 3. The implied-α arithmetic, corrected

On `runs/pareref_on`: T3 = 798/21788 = 3.662567e-02 against `gaussAlpha(6)` =
1.973175e-09 ⇒ **1.86e7×**.  The ladder across the arms on disk, for provenance:

| arm | posture | T3 opp | flags | rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `runs/inj_null` | legacy datum, pdp OFF — **the QUEUE's 3.78e7× figure** | 24238 | 1810 | 7.468e-02 |
| `runs/inj_null_pdp` ≡ `runs/pareref_off` | prioritized anchors, 1(d) OFF | 21788 | 1458 | 6.692e-02 |
| **`runs/pareref_on`** | + 1(d) carry re-reference — **the null instrument** | 21788 | 798 | 3.663e-02 |

Halving the rate took two datum fixes; the remaining 1.86e7× is what this card is
about.

The two "obvious" corrections are both negligible:

- **Max-over-rows.**  The exact per-cell null is
  $\alpha_{\text{cell}} = 1-(2\Phi(k)-1)^{n_c} = 5.9195\text{e-}9$ at $k=6,\,n_c=3$ —
  **3.00×** the quoted per-row α.  Against 1.86e7× this is a rounding error.
- **Denominator noise.**  Simulating $\hat\sigma$ honestly (draw the pool, take its
  actual median; a Gaussian surrogate puts mass near zero and yields absurd
  quantiles) and integrating the numerator in closed form via
  $P(S>k) = \mathbb{E}_{\hat\sigma}\bigl[1-(2\Phi(k\hat\sigma)-1)^{n_c}\bigr]$:
  the threshold for α = 1e-6 is **5.10** with $\hat\sigma\equiv1$, **5.06** at
  $N=1400$, **5.00** at $N=30$.  The `max(1,·)` floor truncates $\hat\sigma$ from
  below, so under a nominal null the shipped test is *conservative*.

⚠ **The "excess kurtosis 4391" figure does not reproduce on this arm.**  Settled
`s_raw` has excess kurtosis **10.38** (skew 2.70).  4391 belongs to a different
quantity or a different arm; do not carry it forward without re-deriving it.

## 4. Measured: the pool is a mixture, where every station draws the SAME model

`config/sim/simnet24_inject.json` specifies a single global
`noise: {code_sigma_zen_m: 0.3, phase_sigma_zen_m: 0.003}` and **no `multipath`
block**, so the generator defaults apply network-wide as well (Gauss-Markov phase
multipath, 2 mm zenith, τ = 300 s, 1/sin el amplified to a 20° cap —
`include/sim/SimConfig.hpp:150-169`).  Verified against the emitted per-station child
configs (`runs/simnet24_inject_null/_child_*.json`): each carries only its own
`station.seed_salt`, derived from the station name.

So **every station draws an independent realization of the SAME error model, and
there is no per-station σ anywhere in the generator.**  Independent realizations of a
common model give a few percent of sampling spread in a robust scale, not 4.7×.  A
systematic per-station scale of that size is therefore manufactured downstream of the
data, not present in it.

Settled window (> t₀+1800 s, `s_raw > 0`): 9975 cells over 53 epochs, 24 stations.

| axis | spread | source |
|---|---|---|
| **station** | **4.7×** (0.948 … 4.452) | fitted cell scale, `network_station_residuals.csv` |
| **signal** | **2.75×** (L2W 0.9974, L5Q 1.3325, **L1C 2.7435**) | `network_pf_keyed.csv` SIG rows, median `s0_phase`, settled window (over all 114 traced epochs: 0.9155 / 1.1656 / 2.3597 = 2.58×) |
| epoch | 1.3× (0.869 … 1.152) | fitted; i.e. the axis σ̂₀ *does* estimate is nearly irrelevant |

The signal axis sits **inside** the cell max — the cell is a max *across* L1C/L2W/L5Q,
so it is essentially always an L1C row, while the pooled median is dominated by
L2W/L5Q.  Note this also rules out elevation weighting as the cause: all three signals
share an elevation and a weight at a given (station, satellite, epoch).

Corroborating structure:

- Flags concentrate in **11 of 24 stations** and **40 (station,satellite) keys**;
  S20 carries 33.7 % of all 798 flags, S06 28.4 %, S01 16.7 %, S07 12.3 %.  Per-station
  flag rates run to **26.1 %** while 13 stations flag at **0.00 %**.
- Flags-per-epoch is **4.13× overdispersed** against Binomial(n_cells, p̂), and the
  cells are **strongly dependent in time**: within-arc lag-1 autocorrelation of
  `s_norm` is **0.8994** (21 555 consecutive pairs), and
  P(flag at t+1 | flag at t, cell still present) = 653/789 = **0.8276** against a
  marginal 0.0366 — a factor **22.6**.  The 798 flags are not 798 events; they are
  **145 contiguous arc-runs on 40 (station,satellite) keys**.  This is the measurement
  R3 turns on: the family is nowhere near 21 788 independent tests.
- **No observational covariate explains it**: corr(log station scale, ·) = −0.20
  (median elevation), −0.03 (fraction below 20°), +0.004 (median truth I_L1),
  +0.24 (max truth I_L1), from each station's `truth_atmo.csv`.
- The residual scale is **non-stationary through ignition** — epoch-median `s_raw`
  0.836 (first third) → 1.816 (last third).  Grade on a settled window or you are
  measuring convergence.
- **364 cells (1.67 %) have `s_raw` exactly 0** — a carried ambiguity with no phase row
  this epoch.  They can never flag, yet they are counted as T3 opportunities, which
  deflates the reported rate.

`scripts/t3_null_fit.py --run runs/pareref_on` (station scale fitted on the first

## 5. Falsified: the redundancy standardization (measured, not argued)

The most-cited candidate fix is that the statistic standardizes a **post-fit**
residual by the **measurement** σ, omitting the redundancy factor
($\operatorname{Var}(v_i) = \sigma^2 r_i/w_i$, so $z\sim N(0,r_i)$, not $N(0,1)$).
That is *true* and it is *not the cause*.

Single-knob A/B, `network.raim_redundancy_normalize`, one exe snapshot
(`79c8e8e1…`), arms `runs/t3b2_{off,on}`, configs
`config/test_suite/_simnet24_inj_t3b2_{off,on}.json`.
**Control: the OFF arm is bit-identical to `runs/pareref_on/logs_v2`** on
`network_solution.csv`, `network_dd_fractional.csv`, `network_station_residuals.csv`
and `network_epoch_status.csv` — which also proves the exe provenance.

The knob **is** solve-affecting as the loader warns (all three of
`network_solution.csv` / `network_dd_fractional.csv` / `network_epoch_status.csv`
differ between the arms, and the consumer moves 0.491 → 0.488 m), **but the T3
opportunity key set is identical** — the same 21 788 (epoch, station, satellite)
triples in both, verified as an exact set comparison.  So this is a *paired* test over
the same cells, which is the strongest form the comparison could take.

## 6. The principled replacement

**R1 — studentize per stratum, with a slow/fast split that the noise budget forces.**

$$\hat\sigma(s,t) \;=\; \hat\sigma_{\text{sta}}(s)\cdot\hat\sigma_{\text{net}}(t)$$

- $\hat\sigma_{\text{net}}(t)$ — the existing network-wide robust scale.  **Keep it.**
  It is the fast channel, and epoch-common contamination is exactly what RAIM exists
  to catch.
- $\hat\sigma_{\text{sta}}(s)$ — a **slow**, robust, time-pooled per-station factor
  (EWMA of the station's robust residual level, normalized to network median 1), with
  a floor/ceiling and a minimum sample.

⚠ **SUPERSEDED IN FORM by §7.5** — the per-stratum factor should be the variance
component Σw·v²/Σr_i, not a median of cell maxima.  The slow/fast split and the noise
budget below still stand; only the estimator inside the slow factor changes.

The split is not a style choice; it is arithmetic.  Relative SD of a robust scale
from *m* rows, $1.4826\sqrt{1/(4mf(q)^2)}$ with $f(q)=2\varphi(0.6745)=0.63556$:

| m | SD(σ̂)/σ | what |
|---|---|---|
| 564 | 4.91 % | network pool, one epoch |
| **23** | **24.32 %** | one station, one epoch — **too noisy to studentize against** |
| 415 | 5.73 % | one station, whole window |

A per-station-per-epoch scale would re-import a heavy tail from the *estimator*.
Pooling the station factor over time is what makes it usable, and §4.1's +0.847
half-to-half correlation is what licenses the pooling.

**R2 — declare the stratum, do not absorb it.**  A per-station denominator makes the
*cell* test correct, but on a homoscedastic fixture the heterogeneity it divides out
is model error, not noise.  Absorbing it silently would mask a real defect — the same
failure mode as recalibrating `k` until the alarm stops.  So the per-station factor
becomes a **first-class declared statistic** with its own event, counter and
threshold: a station whose slow factor departs from the network is precisely the "bad
station" that T4's median-polish row effect was built for and that is currently
deliberately disabled (`NetworkRaim.cpp:434-439`).  This is standing invariant #7
applied to a test statistic — the edge where a station gets its own scale is an event
at both sides, and both must be counted.

**R3 — set α by FDR over the family, not `k` by hand.**  With the pool stratified, the
exact per-cell null is usable directly (`exact_k(α, n_c)`).  But a fixed `k` has an
implied family-wise α that drifts with roster size and run length — 21 788 cells here,
~10× that at 305 stations.  Benjamini–Hochberg over the (cell × epoch) family is the
right control.  ⚠ **BH's independence/PRDS assumption is measurably violated here, and
not marginally**: §4's within-arc lag-1 autocorrelation is 0.8994 and
P(flag | flag at t−1) = 0.83 against a 0.037 marginal, so the 21 788 "tests" are closer
to **145 arc-runs on 40 keys**.  Any FDR scheme must be applied to a family whose
effective size is set by the arc structure, not the cell count — and cells additionally
share the σ̂₀ denominator and the shared satellite states.  Benjamini–Yekutieli's
$\sum_{i=1}^{M}1/i$ factor is the conservative fallback; its cost should be measured
rather than assumed.

**R4 — population hygiene (cheap, independent).**  Exclude the structurally untestable
cells (`s_raw` exactly 0, 1.67 %) from the T3 opportunity count, and give T3c an
opportunity definition that matches its conditional construction (§1) or drop its
`implied_alpha` column, which currently compares a conditional rate against an
unconditional α.

**R5 — do NOT do.**  Do not arm `raim_redundancy_normalize` for this purpose **on this
posture**: measured null on the flag rate, +38 % runtime (§5).  ⚠ **Sim-scoped** — §12.2
shows the field runs with σ̂₀ clamped at the floor on 88–96 % of epochs, where B2's ×1.45
lift would *unpin* the pool rather than cancel.  A field B2 arm is owed before this line
is read as general.  It may still be right for MDB, which needs
`r_i` for a different reason and hard-errors without it
(`GnssConfigLoadNetwork.cpp:3540-3551`).  Do not recalibrate `k`: under the exact null
`k=6` is already an α = 5.9e-9 per-cell test (§3), and on the settled window a
constant would have to be **k ≥ 14.6 to reach even α = 1e-3, and k ≥ 19.8 for
1e-4** — the latter above all but one cell in the whole run (max `s_norm` = 19.726).
The test would be dead.

⚠ **And the fixture cannot calibrate a 6σ-class constant empirically anyway.**  9975
settled cells resolve no rate below ≈ 1e-4; a 5.9e-9 threshold would need ~10⁸ cells,
i.e. ~10⁴ of these runs.  That is the standing argument for a *derived* null over a
fitted quantile — which is item 4's premise, and it survives.

## 7. The open question this card does NOT close

**Why does the post-fit standardized residual vary 4.7× by station on a fixture with
one global noise model, uncorrelated with elevation or ionosphere?**  That is a
stochastic-model / model-error question, not a test-statistic question, and it
deserves its own card.  Until it is answered the per-station factor is doing double
duty as a nuisance absorber — which is exactly why R2 (declare it) is not optional.

Leads, in order of how cheaply they can be falsified:

1. ~~**The sim's multipath is time-correlated and the producer's stochastic model is
   white.**~~  ❌ **FALSIFIED 2026-08-14 by direct experiment — §7.1.**
2. **The per-signal split** (L1C 2.7× L2W at identical elevation, identical weight and
   identical nominal σ) is frequency-specific and *not* ionospheric — iono would make
   L2/L5 worse, not better.  Receiver PCV, phase wind-up, or which signal carries the
   phase-bias datum pin.
3. **The ignition ramp** (0.84 → 1.82) points at ambiguity convergence changing the
   residual scale, i.e. at exactly the constraint-aware redundancy that
   `docs/SPEC_sigma0_constraint_aware.md` derives.

### 7.1 Lead 1 FALSIFIED — it is not the multipath

The sim's Gauss-Markov phase multipath was the best-motivated suspect: 2 mm zenith on
top of 3 mm white noise, τ = 300 s = 10 epochs, elevation-amplified, drawn per station
from its own `seed_salt` — exactly the shape of a persistent per-station scale.

**Tested by zeroing it.**  `config/sim/simnet24_inject_mp0.json` is
`config/sim/simnet24_inject.json` with `multipath.phase_amp_zen_m: 0.0` and nothing
else changed; fixture `runs/simnet24_mp0`; producer config
`_simnet24_inj_t3mp0.json`, byte-identical to `_simnet24_inj_pareref_on.json` except
for the fixture paths; arm `runs/t3mp0`, same exe snapshot.

⚠ **The control that makes this a single knob:** regenerating the *unmodified* fixture
into a fresh directory reproduces `runs/simnet24_inject_null` **byte-identically** (5/5
station RINEX checked by sha256), so the generator is deterministic and the only thing
that moved between the two fixtures is the multipath realization.

| channel | multipath ON | multipath OFF |
|---|---|---|
| T3 cell rate (all epochs) | 3.663e-02 | **3.906e-02** *(up)* |
| T3 rate, settled window | 4.381 % | **5.294 %** *(up)* |
| **station scale spread** | **4.7×** | **4.8×** |
| **per-signal spread** | **2.70×** (L1C 2.70 / L2W 1.00 / L5Q 1.32) | **2.77×** (L1C 2.50 / L2W 0.90 / L5Q 1.20) |
| station-stratified flag rate | 0.672 % | 0.622 % |

**Every structural feature survives removing the error source entirely**, and the flag
rate moves the wrong way.  The multipath contributes essentially nothing to the
heterogeneity.

**Bonus — a second, independent confirmation of §2.**  Removing the multipath lowered
all three per-signal levels by a near-uniform ~8–10 % (L1C 2.70→2.50, L2W 1.00→0.90,
L5Q 1.32→1.20) — a *common* rescaling of the pool.  The statistic did not follow it.
That is the invariance theorem demonstrated by a completely different mechanism from
§5's B2 arm, and the two agree.

**What this leaves.**  Lead 2 (the L1C-specific 2.5–2.7×, at identical elevation,
identical weight, identical nominal σ, and now with multipath gone) is promoted to
first.  Note the likely reframing: it may not be "L1C is loud" but "L2W and L5Q are
*suppressed*" — if the estimator has more freedom to absorb L2/L5 (the per-LOS iono
state and the code biases soak them up), their residuals are crushed while L1C's are
not.  That is a per-signal redundancy story, i.e. lead 3 wearing a different hat, and
it is not measurable from any trace on disk today: the RAIM cell is a max *over*
signals, so `network_station_residuals.csv` has no signal column, and per-row r_i is
never streamed.  **Instrumenting per-signal r_i is the prerequisite for the next step.**

### 7.2 The prerequisite BUILT, and lead 2/3 FALSIFIED — r_i is uniform

§7.1 closed by naming per-signal r_i as the prerequisite: the RAIM cell is a max
*over* signals, so no trace on disk could separate L1C from L2W/L5Q, and per-row r_i
was never streamed.  Both are now instrumented.

**`network.trace_raim_redundancy`** (default OFF) emits one row per post-fit RAIM
**phase row** — `week,tow,station,sat,signal,row,w,r_i,resid_m,z_meas,z_baarda,below_floor`
— i.e. one level finer than the cell.  The design point that makes it usable: it causes
`row_r` to be **computed** but never **used**, so unlike `raim_redundancy_normalize`
(which drops rows at the floor and is solve-affecting) **the census is solve-neutral**.
Proven, not asserted: with the census armed, `network_solution.csv`,
`network_dd_fractional.csv`, `network_station_residuals.csv`, `network_epoch_status.csv`,
`network_events.csv` and `network_flag_rates.csv` are all **bit-identical** to
`runs/pareref_on/logs_v2`; with it off, no file is produced and the same six are
bit-identical.  Cost 17.4 s → 20.3 s (+17 %), 65 364 rows.  Arm `runs/t3ri`, config
`_simnet24_inj_t3ri.json`.  `z_baarda = z_meas/√r_i` is emitted *without* arming the
normalization, so both standardizations are comparable **on one solve**.

| L5Q | 0.4630 | 0.8445 | 1.2705 |
| **spread** | **1.25×** | **2.59×** | **2.72×** |

**r_i is essentially uniform across signals (1.25×) while the residual it is supposed
to explain spans 2.59×** — and dividing by √r_i makes the signal spread slightly
*worse* (2.59× → 2.72×).  The same holds on the station axis: per-station r_i spreads
**1.23×** against a z_meas spread of **3.01×**, with corr(log z_meas, log r_i) =
**−0.284** — far too weak to be the mechanism.  Over the whole settled population r_i
is remarkably flat: median **0.5114**, q01 0.0015, q99 0.6687.

So lead 2's reframing ("L2W/L5Q are *suppressed* because the iono state and code biases
soak them up") is **wrong**: absorption would show up as low r_i on exactly those
signals, and it does not.  Lead 3 (redundancy/convergence) is the same claim and falls
with it.  The heterogeneity is **genuine misfit that varies by station and signal, on an
estimator whose information distribution is uniform**.

### 7.3 A sharper defect surfaced by the census: E[w·v²] ≠ r_i

If `v` were a correct post-fit residual under the assumed stochastic model, then
$E[w_i v_i^2] = r_i$ and $z_{	ext{baarda}} = |v|\sqrt{w}/\sqrt{r_i}$ would be exactly
standard normal — so $1.4826\cdot\operatorname{med}|z_{	ext{baarda}}|$ should read
**1.0**.  Measured, settled, above floor:

| standardization | 1.4826 · median | distance from nominal |
|---|---|---|
| `z_meas` = \|v\|√w — **the shipped one, theoretically wrong** | **1.3683** | 0.37 |
| `z_baarda` = z_meas/√r_i — **the theoretically correct one** | **1.9291** | 0.93 |

**The correct standardization lands 2.5× FURTHER from nominal than the wrong one.**
That is not a tuning detail — it says $E[w v^2] 
eq r_i$ on this solve, i.e. the
redundancy numbers do not describe the actual residual covariance.  Two candidates, both
concrete: `rowRedundancy` evaluates on the **gauge-nulled free covariance**
(`NetworkEstimator.cpp:451-453`), which is not the system that produced `v` if the datum
constraints bind; or the stochastic-model error is large enough to swamp r_i entirely.
This is the same class the in-house `docs/SPEC_sigma0_constraint_aware.md` was written
for, and it is now the sharpest open lead in the card — it also explains §5 cleanly:
B2 measured null on the flag rate *and* pushed σ̂₀ 1.246 → 1.819 precisely because it is
dividing by a quantity that is not the residual's variance.

Incidental, and worth keeping: **the ignition ramp IS r_i.**  Median r_i runs
0.0000 → 0.0004 over the first three assessed epochs and reaches ~0.52 by mid-run, then
sits flat — the 0.836 → 1.816 rise in `s_raw` (§4) is redundancy converging, not the
residual growing.  And the floor drops only 0.49 % of settled rows, **identically across
signals** (49/10026 each), so it is not a per-signal filter.

### 7.4 `rowRedundancy` IS the right covariance — the model is what is wrong

§7.3 named two candidates for `E[w·v²] ≠ r_i`: a wrong covariance in `rowRedundancy`,
or stochastic-model error.  **The covariance is exonerated on four independent links.**

1. **The algebra is exact.**  `NetworkNormalEquations::rowRedundancy` computes
   $\mathrm{lev} = (a_s - M^	op a_r)^	op \Sigma_{ss} (a_s - M^	op a_r) + a_r^	op N_{rr}^{-1} a_r$
   with $M = N_{rr}^{-1}N_{sr}^	op$.  Re-deriving the arrowhead block inverse
   ($\Sigma_{sr} = -\Sigma_{ss}M^	op$, $\Sigma_{rr} = N_{rr}^{-1} + M\Sigma_{ss}M^	op$)
   gives $a^	op\Sigma a$ **identically** that expression.  No approximation.
2. **The datum is handled correctly.**  `NetworkEstimator::solve` realizes each pin as a
   true **Dirichlet elimination** — move the known contribution to the RHS, zero the
   row *and* column, set the diagonal (`NetworkEstimator.cpp:280-292`) — so
   `shared_cov_` is genuinely the covariance *conditional on* the datum, and the
   row/col zeroing at `:444-449` only strips the artificial `1/pin_scale`.  Independently,
   redundancy numbers are datum-invariant for estimable rows, and every row here is a
   row of A.
3. **The priors are in the same system.**  `addStationPrior` throws if `solved_`, forcing
   injection *before* the Schur reduction; the DD priors are folded into the reduced
   shared normals (`NetworkEstimator.cpp:243-251`) before `solveSharedConstrained` fills
   `shared_cov_`.  So Σ includes the prior information.
4. **The residual comes from that system.**  `reconstructStation` solves
   $x_r = N_{rr}^{-1}(u_r - N_{sr}^	op x_s)$ with the **same** `Nrr_ldlt` and `N_sr`
   the leverage uses.

And the identity is not naive-LS-only: for a Bayesian solve with prior
$	heta\sim N(	heta_0,P)$, expanding $v = y - A\hat	heta$ shows the prior
contributions cancel exactly and $\operatorname{Cov}(v) = W^{-1} - A\Sigma A^	op$
**still holds — provided P is the true dispersion of θ about θ₀.**  That proviso is the
whole story.

**What the census measures is therefore a VARIANCE FACTOR, and it is enormous and
structured.**  With $E[w v^2] = r_i$, the classical variance-component estimator
$\hat\sigma^2 = \sum w v^2 / \sum r_i$ must read 1.0.  Settled rows with r_i > 0.01
(29 333):

| stratum | σ̂² = Σz²/Σr | σ̂ | robust med(z²/r) | mean r_i |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **global** | **11.80** | 3.44 | 1.78 | 0.51 |
| L1C | **27.37** | 5.23 | 5.85 | 0.5151 |
| L2W | **2.05** | 1.43 | 0.80 | 0.5548 |
| L5Q | 5.87 | 2.42 | 1.70 | 0.4464 |
| S06000SIM (worst) | **61.80** | 7.86 | — | 0.4496 |
| S16000SIM (best) | **2.14** | 1.46 | — | 0.5373 |

**The variance factor spreads 13.4× across signals and 28.9× across stations while mean
r_i is flat at 0.45–0.55 (1.24×).**  That is the cleanest statement of the whole card:
**r_i correctly describes how much freedom the estimator HAS; it cannot describe how
wrong the estimator's model IS, and only the latter varies.**  Note L2W sits at 2.05 —
nearly consistent with the assumed model — while L1C is 27×, so whatever is
mis-specified is concentrated on L1C rather than spread over the pool.

(The gap between the aggregate 11.80 and the robust 1.78 is itself the heavy tail: the
*typical* row is ~1.8× over-dispersed, the *aggregate second moment* 11.8×.  Report both;
a variance factor wants the aggregate, a robust scale wants the median.)

### 7.5 This upgrades R1 from a heuristic to the correct estimator

§6's R1 specified a slow per-station factor fitted as a **median of cell maxima** — a
heuristic, chosen because nothing better was measurable.  The census removes that
constraint.  The right per-stratum scale is the **variance component with the redundancy
in the denominator**:

$$\hat\sigma^2_{	ext{stratum}} \;=\; rac{\sum_{i\in	ext{stratum}} w_i v_i^2}{\sum_{i\in	ext{stratum}} r_i}$$

which is exactly what `docs/SPEC_sigma0_constraint_aware.md` derives globally, now
computable **per station and per signal** because r_i is streamed.  This is strictly
better than the heuristic on three counts: it is unbiased under the model rather than
calibrated to a reference quantile; it needs no `n_c` assumption (the median-of-maxima
reference constant disappears); and it degrades gracefully as the stratum's redundancy
changes, where a median of maxima silently conflates "quiet" with "unchecked".
⚠ It is also outlier-sensitive, so a production form needs a robust variant (trimmed or
Huberized numerator) — and the 11.80-vs-1.78 gap above says that choice is not cosmetic.

### 7.6 The residual lives in the IONO DIRECTION — and ten candidates are dead

Decomposing the post-fit phase residual 3-vector (v_L1, v_L2, v_L5) at each
(station, sat, epoch) onto three physically meaningful directions — `(1,1,1)`
(anything common in metres: clock, troposphere, orbit, position, tides), the iono
direction `(1, γ₂, γ₅)` with γ₂ = 1.6469 / γ₅ = 1.7933, and the remaining orthogonal
direction (reachable only by the per-signal ambiguity/bias states):

| direction | share of residual variance | rms |
|---|---|---|
| common (clock / tropo / geometry) | 5.02 % | 7.7 mm |
| **iono** | **89.76 %** | **32.4 mm** |
| perp (bias/ambiguity only) | 5.22 % | 7.8 mm |

**This is the L1C answer.**  Once the common mode is removed the iono direction is
`(−0.804, +0.280, +0.525)` — **L1-dominant**, because L1 has the *smallest* iono
coefficient (1 vs 1.65 / 1.79) and therefore sits furthest from the mean.  Per signal,
L1C's 30.6 mm rms is 26.0 mm of iono; L2W's 8.1 mm is 9.0 mm of iono (partly
cancelling).  **L1C is not noisy — it is where unabsorbed iono-direction error lands.**

And the per-station heterogeneity is the *same* effect, not a second one: the loudest
four stations are **93.2 %** iono share at 36.3 mm rms; the quietest four are **65.6 %**
iono at 8.2 mm.

**Structural corollary — the decomposition rules out an entire class by itself.**  Every
signal-independent error (satellite/receiver clock, troposphere, orbit, station
position, solid-earth tide, relativity) is identical *in metres* on all three signals
and can only appear in the common direction, which carries **5 %**.  So the culprit is
necessarily frequency-dependent.  Phase wind-up (∝ λ) and the ionosphere (∝ 1/f²) both
have the **wrong sign** — they load L2/L5 more than L1.

### 12.1 Why — the σ̂₀ floor binds on the field, and that voids §2's premise

| run | σ̂₀ median | epochs **pinned at the 1.0 floor** |
|---|---|---|
| sim `pareref_on` (24 sta) | 1.247 | 38/114 = 33 % |
| field `net50_1h` (49 sta) | **1.0000** | **110/115 = 95.7 %** |
| field `h3_guard` (305 sta) | **1.0000** | **312/354 = 88.1 %** |

**On the field the denominator is a constant almost all the time.**  §2's invariance
theorem holds only while the floor is *not* binding; when it binds, $S_c$ degenerates
to the bare $\max_{i\in c}|z_i|$ compared against a fixed 6σ bar — no normalization at
all.  Stratifying the pool cannot buy much when the statistic is discarding the pool's
scale by clamp.  **The field runs almost entirely in the regime the sim barely visits.**

**Why the field pool sits below the floor is an OPEN question, and the obvious answer
is contradicted by the configs.**  The tempting explanation is redundancy shrinkage —
$E[w_i v_i^2] = r_i < 1$ for a post-fit residual, so $1.4826\cdot\operatorname{med}|v|\sqrt{w}$
lands under 1 and the missing $\sqrt{r_i}$ is what pushes it under the clamp.  That is
consistent, universal, and would close the loop neatly.  But it cannot be the whole
story here: **the field posture assumes `phase_std_m = 0.002 m`, SMALLER than the sim's
0.003** (both `stochastic_model: elevation`), which pushes $|v|\sqrt{w}$ *up*, not down.
Inverting the two σ̂₀ values gives the elevation-scaled post-fit phase misfit directly:

$$\operatorname{med}(|v|/f) \;pprox\; 1.35\ 	ext{mm (field)} \quad	ext{vs}\quad 2.52\ 	ext{mm (sim)}$$

**The sim's post-fit phase residuals are ~1.9× LARGER than real DOY-129 data's**, on the
same estimator.  So the field is not "noisier and clamped"; the *sim* is the loud one,
and the clamp is what a well-behaved pool looks like under this floor.  Whether that is
redundancy shrinkage, a genuinely quieter field posture, or the sim being unrealistically
noisy (§7 lead 1) is unresolved — and it is now the sharpest question in the card,
because the answer decides whether "unclamp the field" or "quiet the sim" is the right
next move.

### 12.1b ⚠ At 305 stations the remedy stops working — and the reason is drift

`pdp305_pinaware/h3_guard` (DOY-129 field, **303 stations present**, 354 epochs,

| as shipped, held-out half | 4.46 % | 27.58 % | 9.57 % |

over the run".
